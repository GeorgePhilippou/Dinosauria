## Overview

Hesperosaurus (meaning 'western lizard') was a herbivorous stegosaurid dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of the western United States, living approximately 155–143 million years ago. It is known from a complete skeleton and skull, collected from the Morrison Formation of Wyoming and Montana. It reached an estimated 6 metres in length and bore the distinctive plates along its back and spikes on its tail characteristic of stegosaurs. It was named by Carpenter, Miles and Cloward in 2001.

Hesperosaurus is placed within Stegosaurinae, the subfamily that includes the iconic Stegosaurus. The well-preserved skull material makes it one of the more completely known stegosaurids from the Morrison Formation and provides comparative data against which more fragmentary material can be assessed. Some researchers have considered Hesperosaurus to be a junior synonym of Stegosaurus, though it is generally retained as a distinct genus based on differences in plate morphology and other features.

## Anatomy & life

Hesperosaurus bore a series of large, possibly oval-shaped plates along its back and four tail spikes (thagomizer), consistent with other stegosaurids. Its skull is low and elongate. Compared to Stegosaurus, the plates of Hesperosaurus are somewhat wider relative to their height. It reached approximately 6 metres, making it a medium-sized stegosaurid.

The relatively complete skull of Hesperosaurus is valuable for understanding stegosaurid cranial anatomy, which is otherwise poorly documented for Morrison taxa. Its relationship to Stegosaurus continues to be debated, with some cladistic analyses supporting synonymy and others maintaining generic distinctiveness, making it a useful case study in Morrison stegosaur taxonomy.

## Fossil record

Hesperosaurus mjosi was described in 2001 by Kenneth Carpenter, Clifford Miles and Karen Cloward from a remarkably complete skeleton with skull recovered from the Morrison Formation. The genus name means 'western lizard', and the species is named in honour of the discoverer. The skull, rare among Morrison stegosaurs, is a key feature of the holotype.

Several partial skeletons, including cranial and dermal armour elements.

- The type is incomplete but includes rare cranial material and dorsal plates.
- The characteristic plate and tail-spike arrangement is reconstructed from more than one specimen.
- It was a quadrupedal herbivore; exact social behaviour and display functions are not directly known.

## Where it lived

Hesperosaurus inhabited the floodplains and river systems of the Late Jurassic Morrison Formation, sharing its environment with Stegosaurus, Allosaurus, Brachiosaurus, Diplodocus, and many other dinosaurs. It would have been a low browser, feeding on ferns, cycads, and other ground-level vegetation.

Primary source of Hesperosaurus material, including the well-preserved holotype skull from the Kimmeridgian-age Morrison deposits.

Hesperosaurus-bearing lower Morrison strata are Kimmeridgian and generally older than classic Stegosaurus stenops horizons.

## Open questions

- Generic separation from Stegosaurus remains disputed.
- Armour orientation and functions remain partly inferential.
