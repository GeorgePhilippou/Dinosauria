## Overview

Haplocanthosaurus (meaning 'simple-spined lizard') was a large herbivorous sauropod dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of the western United States, living approximately 155–142 million years ago. Known from the Morrison Formation of Colorado, Wyoming, Utah, and Montana, it reached an estimated length of up to 21.5 metres, placing it among the larger sauropods of its time. It lacked the bifurcated (forked) vertebral spines found in diplodocids, giving rise to its name. The genus was described by John Bell Hatcher in 1903.

Haplocanthosaurus is placed within Diplodocoidea by the PBDB, though its precise position within the Morrison sauropod fauna has been debated — some analyses have placed it closer to the base of Macronaria or even as a basal eusauropod. Its relatively primitive vertebral structure compared to the more derived diplodocids and camarasaurids makes it informative for understanding sauropod diversity in the Morrison ecosystem. Because skulls are unknown for this genus, reconstructions of the head are based on related taxa.

## Anatomy & life

Haplocanthosaurus reached lengths estimated up to about 21.5 metres, with a long neck and tail typical of sauropods. Its vertebrae are notably simple in structure compared to more derived Morrison sauropods, and no skull material is known, so head shape is inferred from related genera. Its limb proportions suggest a relatively robust build for its size.

Haplocanthosaurus provides evidence for sauropod diversity and morphological disparity within the Morrison Formation ecosystem. Its debated phylogenetic placement — variously allied with diplodocoids, camarasauromorphs, or more basal positions — makes it a useful test case for competing hypotheses of sauropod relationships, and continued reanalysis of its anatomy contributes to understanding of the Morrison sauropod fauna.

## Fossil record

Haplocanthosaurus was named in 1903 by John Bell Hatcher based on partial skeletons lacking skulls from the Morrison Formation of Colorado. Two species are generally recognised: H. priscus and H. utterbacki. The genus name refers to its simple, unbifurcated vertebral spines, distinguishing it from diplodocids like Apatosaurus whose spines are deeply forked.

Several incomplete postcranial skeletons with very limited cranial evidence.

- The genus is diagnosed largely from vertebral and limb anatomy.
- Most modern analyses place it near the base of Diplodocoidea, though exact position varies.
- Adult length and mass estimates depend on incomplete specimens.

## Where it lived

The Late Jurassic Morrison Formation records a semi-arid to seasonally wet floodplain environment across what is now the American West. Haplocanthosaurus shared this habitat with a diversity of other sauropods including Camarasaurus, Diplodocus, Brachiosaurus and Apatosaurus, as well as theropods such as Allosaurus and Ceratosaurus.

Primary source of Haplocanthosaurus material, with specimens from multiple Colorado sites including the famous Cañon City area.

Known horizons fall mainly in older-to-middle Morrison intervals, broadly Kimmeridgian.

## Open questions

- Cranial anatomy is almost unknown.
- Species boundaries, referrals and basal diplodocoid position need further work.
