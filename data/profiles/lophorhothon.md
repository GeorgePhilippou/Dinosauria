## Overview

Lophorhothon was a hadrosauroid dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous (Santonian to Campanian), approximately 83–78 million years ago, in what is now Alabama, USA. It is one of the few dinosaurs known from the southern United States east of the Mississippi, a region with a relatively sparse dinosaur fossil record for this time period. Estimates suggest it reached roughly 4–5 metres in length, making it a medium-sized herbivore. The type species is Lophorhothon atopus.

Described in 1960 by Langston, Lophorhothon is significant as one of the earliest hadrosauroids known from the eastern coastal plain of North America, an area that was covered by a shallow sea (the Western Interior Seaway) in the Late Cretaceous, with dinosaur-inhabited landmasses restricted to coastal margins. A disarticulated skull and skeleton were found together, making it the most complete dinosaur specimen known from Alabama.

## Anatomy & life

During the Santonian–Campanian, the region that is now Alabama was situated along the eastern coast of the Appalachian landmass, a narrow strip of land bordering the Western Interior Seaway. Dinosaur fossils from this coastal plain environment are rare because marine and brackish sediments dominate the geological record of the region. Lophorhothon's recovery from chalk deposits suggests the carcass was transported into or near a marine setting.

As one of the rare dinosaur specimens from the southeastern USA, Lophorhothon contributes important information about the dinosaur fauna of Appalachia — the eastern landmass of the Late Cretaceous North American continent, which was isolated from the western Laramidia landmass by the inland sea. The Appalachian dinosaur fauna appears to have been distinct from the better-known western assemblages.

## Fossil record

Lophorhothon atopus was named in 1960 by Wann Langston Jr. based on a disarticulated skull and postcranial skeleton found in Alabama. The genus name means 'crested snout', referring to a nasal ridge on the skull, while atopus means 'strange' or 'out of place', reflecting its unusual occurrence in the southeastern United States. It was the first dinosaur to be scientifically described from Alabama.

## Classification

Lophorhothon has been classified as a hadrosauroid — a broad group that includes the true hadrosaurs (duck-billed dinosaurs) and their close relatives. Its exact position within Hadrosauroidea has been uncertain, with some analyses placing it as a basal member outside Hadrosauridae proper. The fragmentary and disarticulated nature of the holotype complicates detailed phylogenetic placement.

## Open questions

- The type is juvenile and incomplete.
- Its position inside or immediately outside Hadrosauridae remains analysis-dependent.
