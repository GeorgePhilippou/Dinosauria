## Overview

Anchisaurus was a small early sauropodomorph from the Early Jurassic of northeastern North America. It lived before the gigantic sauropods, at a stage when sauropodomorphs were still relatively light-bodied animals that could move actively on land.

The genus has a tangled naming history, replacing earlier names that were already occupied. PBDB records it from the Portland / Agawam unit in Connecticut and Massachusetts, making it part of the early Jurassic rift-basin dinosaur record of eastern North America.

## Anatomy & life

Anchisaurus captures sauropodomorphs before the full sauropod body plan: still small, active and close to the evolutionary transition toward giant quadrupedal herbivores.

## Fossil record

Its history includes replacement names and taxonomic housekeeping, a reminder that early North American dinosaur nomenclature was often unstable.

Several incomplete skeletons, one substantially complete and designated as neotype.

- The accepted species spelling is polyzelus, not polyzelous.
- The Manchester quarry material formerly named Ammosaurus major belongs to Anchisaurus in the modern revision.

## Where it lived

The Portland Formation material is Early Jurassic, broadly Hettangian-Sinemurian.

## Open questions

- Its exact phylogenetic position near Sauropoda varies.
- Some historical referrals require renewed comparison.
