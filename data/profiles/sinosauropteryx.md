## Overview

Sinosauropteryx prima was the first non-avian dinosaur found with direct evidence of feathers — described from the Yixian Formation of Liaoning, China in 1996. Its simple, unbranched filamentous integument (proto-feathers) covers the head, back and tail and represents the simplest feather-like structure known: hollow, hair-like filaments without barbules or barbs.

The original description of Sinosauropteryx triggered immediate scientific controversy: critics argued the filaments were decayed collagen fibres, not feathers. Subsequent analyses of feather microstructure, including melanosome analysis revealing an orange-and-white ringed tail, have comprehensively confirmed the feather interpretation and established Sinosauropteryx as a cornerstone of feathered dinosaur research.

## Anatomy & life

Sinosauropteryx is the first dinosaur to have its colour pattern reconstructed with chemical evidence. Analysis of melanosomes (melanin granelles) in the tail filaments reveals two different melanosome types alternating along the tail: phaeomelanosomes (rusty orange) and eumelanosomes (dark). This creates a vivid orange-and-white striped tail, probably used for display, analogous to ringed tails in modern mammals.

Several specimens preserve stomach contents. One contains a small mammal (a shrew-like tribosphenidan), another a small lizard. This shows Sinosauropteryx was an active, opportunistic predator of small vertebrates. A third specimen appears to contain eggs, possibly indicating female reproductive anatomy rather than stomach content.

## Fossil record

The first specimen reached Ji Qiang of the National Geological Museum of China in 1996; he and colleagues described it that year. Philip Currie and Chen Pei-ji independently confirmed the filamentous integument. The controversy over whether the structures were feathers or collagen fibres persisted for a decade until chemical and structural analyses — showing melanosomes, hollow structures and consistent diameter distributions — confirmed their feather nature.

Multiple articulated skeletons with filamentous integument, pigmentation and some gut contents.

Filamentous feather impressions and melanosome analysis allowed colour reconstruction; stomach contents preserve lizard and mammal prey.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous lake and forest ecosystem of northeastern China.
