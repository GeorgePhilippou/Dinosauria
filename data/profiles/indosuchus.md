## Overview

Indosuchus (meaning 'Indian crocodile') was a large carnivorous abelisaurid theropod from the Late Cretaceous of India, living approximately 72–66 million years ago during the Maastrichtian stage. Known primarily from the Lameta Formation of Madhya Pradesh, it is estimated to have reached about 7 metres in length. It was an apex predator of the latest Cretaceous Gondwanan landmass, occupying a niche comparable to Tyrannosaurus in the northern continents. The genus was described by Friedrich von Huene and C.A. Matley in 1933.

Indosuchus is classified within Abelisauridae, the group of stocky, short-armed predators characteristic of the Cretaceous southern continents (Gondwana). Its Indian provenance is significant for biogeographic reasons: India was drifting northward as an isolated landmass during this period, and its abelisaurid fauna provides evidence for the persistence of Gondwanan theropod lineages on the subcontinent. The material attributed to Indosuchus includes an almost complete skeleton, though the exact completeness of the best specimens has been debated.

## Anatomy & life

Indosuchus was a large, bipedal predator estimated at approximately 7 metres in length. Like other abelisaurids, it likely had a deep skull with reduced forelimbs, relying on its powerful jaws and hindlimbs for hunting. It would have been a dominant predator of the Maastrichtian Indian subcontinent, preying on titanosaur sauropods and other large animals.

Indosuchus is one of the key abelisaurids from the Indian subcontinent, contributing to understanding of how Gondwanan theropod faunas persisted on the Indian plate as it drifted toward Asia. Its presence alongside titanosaurs in the Lameta Formation mirrors contemporaneous South American and Madagascan assemblages, providing biogeographic evidence for shared Gondwanan evolutionary history.

## Fossil record

Indosuchus raptorius was described in 1933 by Friedrich von Huene and C.A. Matley from material collected in the Lameta Formation of central India. The genus name reflects the initial misinterpretation of the material as crocodilian-like, before its theropod identity was clarified. 'Raptorius' (the species name) refers to its predatory nature.

Fragmentary cranial bones of uncertain association.

## Where it lived

The Lameta Formation records fluvial and lacustrine environments in what is now central India during the latest Cretaceous. Indosuchus shared this environment with titanosaur sauropods including Isisaurus, as well as other theropods. The contemporaneous Deccan Traps volcanic province was beginning to erupt during this time, placing Indosuchus in one of the most geologically dramatic intervals of Earth history.

The primary source of Indosuchus material, a Maastrichtian-age deposit also yielding sauropod titanosaurs and other Late Cretaceous fauna.
