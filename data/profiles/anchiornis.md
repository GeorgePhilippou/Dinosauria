## Overview

Anchiornis huxleyi was a small, feathered paravian dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of China, living approximately 165–153 million years ago. About 34 centimetres tall and 60 centimetres long with a wingspan comparable to a modern crow, it was covered in elaborate plumage including long pennaceous feathers on all four limbs. Fossils are known almost exclusively from the Tiaojishan Formation of Liaoning Province.

Named by Xu and colleagues in 2009, Anchiornis is one of the most scientifically influential Jurassic dinosaurs ever discovered, sitting close to the origin of birds within Paraves. Melanosomes preserved in its feathers have been used to reconstruct its colour pattern with considerable confidence, revealing black-and-white wing plumage and a russet-coloured crest. Its phylogenetic position has been contentiously debated — some analyses place it as a troodontid, others as a basal avialan.

## Anatomy & life

Anchiornis is the first extinct animal to have its colour pattern scientifically reconstructed from fossil evidence. Analysis of melanosomes — microscopic pigment-bearing organelles — preserved within feather impressions revealed that Anchiornis had black-and-white striped wings, a black body, and a russet-red crown and face. This landmark study, published in 2010, demonstrated that detailed colour reconstruction is possible from non-avian dinosaur fossils.

Anchiornis possessed long flight feathers not only on its forelimbs but also on its hind limbs, creating a four-winged or 'tetrapteryx' body plan comparable to that of Microraptor. The hind wing feathers were large but asymmetrically vaned, and were likely too small and positioned too awkwardly for effective aerodynamic use. Whether Anchiornis was capable of any form of aerial locomotion remains debated.

## Fossil record

Anchiornis huxleyi was described in 2009 by Xu Xing, Zhang Fucheng, Hou Lianhai, and Hu Dongyu from specimens collected in Liaoning Province, China. The generic name means 'near bird', while the species name honours Thomas Henry Huxley, the Victorian naturalist who championed the evolutionary link between birds and dinosaurs. Following the initial description, additional specimens were referred to the genus, making it one of the most abundantly known Jurassic paravians.

Hundreds of articulated skeletons, many with feather impressions.

## Where it lived

Dozens of exceptionally preserved Anchiornis specimens have been recovered here, many retaining feather impressions that enable detailed reconstructions of plumage colour and structure.

## Classification

The precise placement of Anchiornis within Paraves has been debated since its discovery. Some analyses recover it as a troodontid, others as a member of Archaeopterygidae or as a basal avialan close to the origin of birds. Its mix of features shared with troodontids, dromaeosaurids, and early birds illustrates the mosaic nature of evolution at the base of the avian lineage. Its Jurassic age predates Archaeopteryx in some analyses, though the stratigraphic relationship between these taxa is complex.

## Open questions

- Its exact position among early paravians varies.
- Flight capability and colour reconstruction are model-dependent.
