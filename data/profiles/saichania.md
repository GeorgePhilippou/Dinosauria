## Overview

Saichania (meaning 'beautiful' in Mongolian) was a large, heavily armoured ankylosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia and China, living approximately 80 million years ago (Campanian). Named by Maryańska in 1977, it reached an estimated 6.6 metres in length and is known from the Baruungoyot Formation of Omnogov province, Mongolia, and from probable occurrences in China. It was one of the most heavily armoured ankylosaurs known, with extensive dorsal and lateral body armour and a tail club.

Saichania belongs to Ankylosaurinae within Ankylosauridae and is closely related to Tarchia from the slightly younger Nemegt Formation. Its well-preserved material, including partial skulls and body armour, has made it a key reference for understanding Asian ankylosaur anatomy. Unlike the drier Djadokhta fauna of Pinacosaurus, Saichania may have inhabited more arid dune environments based on its depositional context in the Baruungoyot Formation.

## Anatomy & life

Saichania possessed some of the most extensive body armour known among ankylosaurs, with large flat osteoderms on the back, smaller ossicles between them, and spines projecting from the shoulders and flanks. The broad, low body and well-ossified eyelids suggest a strongly defensive lifestyle. Its tail club would have been a powerful weapon against large predators such as Tarbosaurus.

The skull of Saichania shows complex nasal passages that looped back on themselves—more elaborate than those of some other ankylosaurs. These tortuous airways may have helped warm and humidify inhaled air in the dry, dusty Cretaceous desert environment, or may have played a role in thermoregulation or olfaction.

Saichania and Tarchia are closely related Mongolian ankylosaurids. Tarchia, from the overlying Nemegt Formation, was named by Maryańska in 1977 from a specimen with a notably large braincase. The two genera co-define a distinctively Mongolian Late Cretaceous ankylosaur assemblage distinct from the North American ankylosaurid fauna.

## Fossil record

Saichania chulsanensis was described by Polish palaeontologist Teresa Maryańska in 1977 from material collected during Polish-Mongolian expeditions to the Gobi Desert. The genus name, meaning 'beautiful' in Mongolian, was chosen to reflect the quality of preservation of the specimens. The species name refers to the Khulsan (Chulsan) locality.

Holotype skull and partial skeleton plus disputed referrals.

- The holotype preserves an excellent skull, neck armour, vertebrae, girdles and forelimbs but not a complete body.
- Some mounted or referred specimens have been questioned or reassigned, so tail and hindquarter anatomy must be labelled by specimen.

## Where it lived

Campanian desert-environment deposits that yielded the type and referred Saichania material with preserved osteoderms.

The type is late Campanian Baruungoyot Formation; an isolated 80 Ma point is not justified.

## Open questions

- Referred skeletons and species boundaries with Tarchia require care.
