## Overview

Gilmoreosaurus (meaning 'Gilmore's lizard') was a herbivorous hadrosauromorph dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Asia, approximately 96–83 million years ago. The type species, Gilmoreosaurus mongoliensis, is known from the Iren Dabasu Formation of Inner Mongolia, China. It reached an estimated length of around 6 metres and would have been a ground-dwelling, bipedally capable plant-eater, likely gregarious in behaviour. Additional fragmentary material from Uzbekistan has been tentatively assigned to the genus, though these attributions remain disputed.

Gilmoreosaurus was named by Brett-Surman in 1979 and sits within Hadrosauroidea, making it an early relative of the true duck-billed dinosaurs (Hadrosauridae). Its position near the base of hadrosaur evolution gives it significance for understanding how the enormously successful hadrosaurid lineage arose in Asia before spreading into North America. The questionable referral of Uzbekistan specimens highlights the difficulty of assigning fragmentary Cretaceous ornithopod material to genus level and underscores ongoing work on Asian hadrosaur diversity.

## Anatomy & life

Gilmoreosaurus is estimated to have reached approximately 6 metres in length, making it a medium-sized ornithopod. Its anatomy places it among the more primitive hadrosaur relatives, lacking the highly derived dental batteries and elaborate cranial crests seen in later hadrosaurids. The postcranial material suggests it was a capable biped that may also have browsed quadrupedally.

As one of the better-known basal hadrosauroids from Asia, Gilmoreosaurus provides evidence for the Asian origins of hadrosaur diversity. Its phylogenetic position near the base of Hadrosauroidea helps palaeontologists trace the evolutionary steps leading to the highly derived crests and dental batteries of later duck-billed dinosaurs.

## Fossil record

Gilmoreosaurus mongoliensis was formally named in 1979 by Michael Brett-Surman, honouring American palaeontologist Charles W. Gilmore, who conducted early work on Asian dinosaurs. The holotype material was collected from the Iren Dabasu Formation of Inner Mongolia. Subsequent referrals of Central Asian specimens have expanded the potential range of the genus, though most remain poorly supported.

Disarticulated cranial and postcranial remains representing multiple individuals.

## Where it lived

The Iren Dabasu Formation records a semi-arid floodplain environment of the Middle Campanian in what is now Inner Mongolia. Gilmoreosaurus shared this habitat with other dinosaurs including the tyrannosaur Alectrosaurus and the ornithomimosaur Archaeornithomimus, suggesting a diverse Late Cretaceous ecosystem in eastern Asia.

The primary type locality for Gilmoreosaurus mongoliensis, yielding postcranial and dental material dating to the Campanian stage.

## Open questions

- Disarticulation complicates individual association.
- Formation age and precise hadrosauroid position are unresolved.
