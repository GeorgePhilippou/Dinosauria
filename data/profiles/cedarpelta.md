## Overview

Cedarpelta was a large ankylosaurid dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of North America, approximately 121–100 million years ago (Aptian to Albian), found in the Cedar Mountain Formation of Utah, USA. Estimated at about 10 metres in length, it was one of the largest ankylosaurs of its time in North America. Its name means 'Cedar Mountain Formation shield', referencing its discovery horizon and the body armour characteristic of ankylosaurs.

Named by Carpenter, Kirkland, Burge, and Bird in 2001, Cedarpelta is known from a skull and partial skeleton. Importantly, the skull morphology led researchers to reconsider the classification of some other ankylosaur taxa. As an ankylosaurid from the Cedar Mountain Formation—a unit deposited during a key interval of North American dinosaur diversification—Cedarpelta contributes to understanding the temporal and biogeographic patterns of ankylosaur evolution.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Cedarpelta is relatively undecorated compared to later ankylosaurs, lacking the fused dermal plates that characterise more derived ankylosaurids. This more open skull texture led to comparisons with primitive ankylosaurs and renewed interest in the taxonomic assignment of fragmentary Early Cretaceous ankylosaur material. The body was estimated at about 10 metres, making it a large representative of the group for its time.

## Fossil record

Cedarpelta bilbeyhallorum was named in 2001 by Kenneth Carpenter, James Kirkland, Donald Burge, and John Bird, based on a skull and partial postcranial skeleton from the Cedar Mountain Formation of Utah. The species name honours Virginia Bilbey and Ann Hall for their contributions to Utah palaeontology. The name Cedarpelta references both the formation and the shield-like armour of ankylosaurs.

Disarticulated cranial, postcranial and armour elements from at least two individuals.

## Where it lived

The Cedar Mountain Formation represents a major stratigraphic interval spanning the Early to mid-Cretaceous of the Colorado Plateau. Its dinosaur fauna includes sauropods, ornithopods, and multiple theropods alongside Cedarpelta. The depositional environment was a semi-arid floodplain with seasonal rivers, and the fauna shows faunal transitions reflecting changing palaeoenvironments and possible intermittent connections with Asia.

Early Cretaceous fluvial and floodplain deposits in eastern Utah, the primary source of Cedarpelta material.

## Classification

Cedarpelta lived during the Aptian–Albian, a period when North American dinosaur faunas were diversifying after the Late Jurassic Morrison fauna. Its position within Ankylosauridae suggests that true ankylosaurids were already present in North America by the Early Cretaceous, predating the diversification that would produce taxa like Euoplocephalus and Ankylosaurus in the Late Cretaceous.

## Open questions

- Material represents multiple individuals.
- Its placement at or just outside Ankylosauridae varies.
- The arrangement of osteoderms is incompletely known.
