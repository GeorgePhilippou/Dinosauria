## Overview

Gargoyleosaurus (meaning 'gargoyle lizard') was an armoured ankylosaur dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of Wyoming, USA, living approximately 154–145 million years ago during the Kimmeridgian to Tithonian stages. Estimated at around 4 metres in length, it is one of the oldest and most primitive ankylosaurs known, providing valuable information about the early evolution of Ankylosauria. Its fossils include a skull and partial articulated postcranium, recovered from the Morrison Formation.

Gargoyleosaurus parkpinorum was described by Kenneth Carpenter and colleagues in 1998. Its discovery revealed that by the Late Jurassic, ankylosaurs had already achieved a body plan broadly similar to later forms, complete with bony armour plates and a broad skull. PBDB places Gargoyleosaurus within Nodosauridae, though some analyses have placed it as a basal ankylosaur outside both Nodosauridae and Ankylosauridae, reflecting ongoing uncertainty about its precise phylogenetic position.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Gargoyleosaurus is broad and triangular in dorsal view, with prominent lateral projections and a complex pattern of fused osteoderms covering its surface. These features are broadly consistent with later ankylosaurians, indicating that the characteristic armoured skull design had evolved early in ankylosaur history. The body armour includes rows of keeled osteoderms and spines similar to those of later nodosaurids.

## Fossil record

Gargoyleosaurus parkpinorum was described by Kenneth Carpenter, Chure, and Kirkland in 1998. The genus name derives from its imposing armoured appearance, reminiscent of architectural gargoyle figures, while the species name honours the park and pin — a reference to the landowners who permitted excavation. The specimen was found in the Morrison Formation of Wyoming and includes an associated skull.

One partial skull and substantial incomplete postcranial skeleton with osteoderms.

- The skull is informative but crushed and incomplete.
- It was a low, quadrupedal herbivore without evidence for an ankylosaurid tail club.
- Its placement just inside Ankylosauria versus near Nodosauridae varies among analyses.

## Where it lived

Gargoyleosaurus lived alongside some of the most iconic Jurassic dinosaurs, including Allosaurus, Diplodocus, Brachiosaurus, and Stegosaurus in the Morrison Formation ecosystem. As a low-slung armoured herbivore, it would have fed on ground-level vegetation such as ferns and cycads, relying on its dermal armour and possibly flank spines for protection against large theropod predators.

The only known locality; the partially articulated skeleton was found in the Late Jurassic Morrison Formation of Albany County, Wyoming.

The type is from the Upper Jurassic Morrison Formation; member-level correlation gives only an approximate numerical age.

## Classification

Gargoyleosaurus is significant as one of the oldest ankylosaurs with reasonably complete material. Its Late Jurassic age pushes the documented ankylosaur record back in time and demonstrates that by the Kimmeridgian, the armoured dinosaur lineage had already diverged from stegosaurs and established a distinct body plan. Whether Gargoyleosaurus sits within Nodosauridae or represents a more basal position varies between analyses.

## Open questions

- The rear body, tail and complete armour layout are incompletely represented.
- Basal ankylosaur relationships remain sensitive to analysis.
