## Overview

Zephyrosaurus ('west wind lizard') was a small orodromine ornithischian dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of North America, living approximately 120–110 million years ago during the Aptian–Albian stages. It reached about 1.8 metres in length and was a lightly built, bipedal herbivore. The type and only species, Zephyrosaurus schaffi, is based on a partial skull and postcranial fragments discovered in the Cloverly Formation of Carbon County, Montana.

Named by Hans-Dieter Sues in 1980, Zephyrosaurus is placed within Orodrominae, a group of small to medium ornithischians within or close to the base of Ornithopoda. Its skull material, though fragmentary, shows features comparable to other small ornithopods of the Early Cretaceous, and tentatively referred tracks from Maryland and Virginia suggest a potentially wider range. The genus is named for the Greek god of the west wind, Zephyrus.

## Anatomy & life

The known skull material of Zephyrosaurus includes portions of the premaxilla, maxilla, jugal, and other elements that reveal a small, lightly built herbivore with a horny beak and relatively simple leaf-shaped cheek teeth. The postorbital region appears robust compared to some other small ornithopods, and ridge-like structures on the snout may have had display or species-recognition functions.

Zephyrosaurus has been placed within Orodrominae, a group of small ornithopods that also includes Orodromeus makelai from Montana and Oryctodromeus cubicularis, a burrowing ornithischian. Orodrominids were a distinctive Early Cretaceous radiation of small North American herbivores that occupied a niche somewhat different from larger ornithopods, likely browsing on ground-level vegetation in floodplain environments.

Zephyrosaurus shared the Cloverly Formation environment with the large ornithopod Tenontosaurus and the predatory dromaeosaurid Deinonychus. As a small herbivore, it likely occupied a different niche from Tenontosaurus, perhaps feeding on different vegetation or in different microhabitats. Its modest size of about 1.8 metres would have made it vulnerable to predation from Deinonychus.

## Fossil record

Zephyrosaurus schaffi was described in 1980 by Hans-Dieter Sues from a partial skull and postcranial bones collected in the Cloverly Formation of Carbon County, Montana. The genus name refers to Zephyrus, the Greek god of the west wind, in reference to the western United States provenance. The species name honours Schaf, a collector associated with the material. New, undescribed Zephyrosaurus material was reportedly under study in later publications.
