## Overview

Archaeoceratops oshimai was a small, basal neoceratopsian dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of north-central China, living approximately 121–110 million years ago during the Aptian to Albian stages. It was bipedal and quite small, estimated at only about 0.9 to 1.3 metres in length and roughly 10 kilograms in body mass. It had a comparatively large head with a small bony frill but no horns, and its fossils come from the Xinminbao Group of Gansu Province.

Named by Dong and Azuma in 1997, Archaeoceratops represents a basal member of Neoceratopsia and is important for understanding the early evolutionary history of the group in Asia. Its relatively primitive anatomy, combined with its Early Cretaceous age, places it among the basal neoceratopsians that preceded the evolution of the large-bodied, horn-bearing ceratopsids of the Late Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Archaeoceratops had a large head relative to its small body, with a bony frill extending from the back of the skull and a parrot-like beak for cropping vegetation. It lacked horns, a condition shared with many early ceratopsians. Despite its bipedal stance, it may have occasionally adopted a quadrupedal posture. Its overall anatomy is more primitive than Protoceratops but more derived than the earliest ceratopsians such as Psittacosaurus.

Archaeoceratops provides important evidence for the early diversification of neoceratopsians in Asia during the Early Cretaceous. Its position as a basal neoceratopsian, combined with its relatively complete material, has made it a useful anchor point for phylogenetic analyses of the ceratopsian family tree. It demonstrates that small, bipedal, frill-bearing ceratopsians were present in Asia well before the appearance of large horned forms.

## Fossil record

Archaeoceratops oshimai was described by Dong Zhiming and Yoichi Azuma in 1997 from material collected in Gansu Province, China. The generic name means 'ancient horned face', referencing its position as one of the earliest neoceratopsians. The species name honours Dr. Oshima, who contributed to the expedition that found the fossils. Multiple specimens have since been referred to the genus.

Several partial skulls and postcranial skeletons.

- A. oshimai is represented by multiple partial individuals; A. yujingziensis is a second species described from the same regional unit.
- Broad herbivory is supported by dental anatomy, but exact diet and social behaviour are unknown.

## Where it lived

The Xinminbao Group of Gansu Province represents arid to semi-arid desert and river environments of Early Cretaceous central Asia. The dinosaur fauna of this region is not fully known, but it appears to have included a variety of small ornithischians and theropods adapted to relatively dry conditions. Archaeoceratops was likely a ground-dwelling browser of low-growing vegetation.

Multiple Archaeoceratops specimens have been recovered from the Early Cretaceous formations of Gansu, providing the primary basis for describing the genus.

The Xinminpu Group localities are Aptian-Albian but their individual horizons are not all dated precisely.

## Open questions

- Local stratigraphic ages are broad.
- Basal neoceratopsian relationships vary among analyses.
