## Overview

Liliensternus liliensterni was a large Late Triassic theropod from Germany (~210 Ma), reaching about five metres — one of the largest predatory dinosaurs of its time in Europe. Known from much of the postcranium but lacking a complete skull, it is placed among the coelophysoids or as a close relative of them, representing one of the larger members of the early theropod radiation.

The Löwenstein Formation of Germany where Liliensternus is found provides a rare glimpse into a Late Triassic European fauna otherwise poorly known. Its large size relative to contemporaries suggests that ecological niche diversification among theropods began rapidly after the origin of the group.

## Anatomy & life

Known elements include cervical and dorsal vertebrae, limb bones and parts of the pelvis. The hindlimb proportions suggest a fast runner, typical of coelophysoid-grade theropods. Skull material is limited, so dietary and feeding inferences rely on comparisons with better-known relatives like Coelophysis.

The Löwenstein Formation records a semi-arid environment with seasonal rivers — the classic Late Triassic European landscape. Liliensternus was the largest predator in a fauna that included the prosauropod Plateosaurus, various small reptiles and early mammals. Its large body size relative to Coelophysis shows that theropods quickly evolved a range of body sizes after their origin.

## Fossil record

First described by Friedrich von Huene in 1934 as Halticosaurus liliensterni, the material was reassigned to a new genus by Welles in 1984. The specimens come from Triassic deposits in Baden-Württemberg. The partial remains are significant as among the largest Late Triassic theropods from Europe.

Disarticulated partial skeletal material from at least two individuals, with fragmentary cranial bones.

Large coelophysoid proportions and early theropod skeletal features confirm its placement near the base of Neotheropoda.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic European terrestrial environments where early dinosaurs were beginning to diversify.

## Open questions

- The type requires a comprehensive modern redescription.
- Cranial anatomy, species diagnosis and fine phylogeny remain poorly resolved.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
