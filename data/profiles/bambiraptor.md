## Overview

Bambiraptor was a small dromaeosaurid theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous period, approximately 83–72 million years ago (Campanian age), in what is now Montana, USA. Reaching about 1 metre in length as a known specimen, it was a swift, bird-like predator equipped with sickle-shaped claws typical of the dromaeosaurid family. Its fossils were recovered from the Two Medicine Formation and described in 2000 by Burnham and colleagues.

Bambiraptor is scientifically significant for its remarkably bird-like skeleton, which contributed to debates on theropod-bird relationships. The holotype is a juvenile, raising questions about whether it represents a distinct genus or a young individual of Saurornitholestes; some researchers have suggested the specimen may even be a chimera. The genus was named after the Disney character Bambi, reflecting the specimen's small, young appearance, and its study at major US universities underscored early 21st-century interest in avian origins among dromaeosaurids.

## Anatomy & life

The known specimen is less than 1 metre long, but this appears to be a juvenile individual, so adult size is uncertain. Like other dromaeosaurids, Bambiraptor likely possessed a rigid tail, raptorial hind claws, and grasping forelimbs. Its proportionally large brain case and wrist anatomy have been cited as evidence for close evolutionary ties between dromaeosaurids and birds.

## Fossil record

The holotype of Bambiraptor was discovered by a young fossil hunter, Wes Linster, on the Blackfeet Reservation in Montana in 1994. It was purchased by the Feinberg family and lent to the Graves Museum of Natural History in Florida. The genus and species Bambiraptor feinbergorum were formally named by Burnham, Derstler, Currie, Bakker, Zhou, and Ostrom in 2000, with the name commemorating the Feinberg family and referencing the Disney character Bambi due to the specimen's small, young appearance.

A largely complete, partially articulated juvenile holotype (AMNH FR 30556), plus a separate paratype assemblage containing duplicate elements from at least two larger individuals.

## Where it lived

Bambiraptor's bird-like skeletal proportions, particularly its forelimb and brain anatomy, made it a focal point in discussions of the dinosaur-to-bird transition. However, its taxonomic validity remains disputed: the holotype may be a juvenile Saurornitholestes, and the possibility that elements from multiple individuals were combined in the specimen has been raised. These unresolved questions make Bambiraptor an important but cautionary case in palaeoecological interpretation.

The Two Medicine Formation environment was a semi-arid floodplain ecosystem at the southern margin of the Western Interior Seaway. Bambiraptor shared this habitat with hadrosaurs and other small theropods. As a small, active predator, it likely hunted lizards, small mammals, and other vertebrates.

Late Campanian-age formation that yielded the holotype specimen, a near-complete juvenile skeleton.

## Open questions

- The paratype combines at least two larger individuals and cannot define one adult skeleton.
- The holotype may represent a juvenile of another dromaeosaurid.
- A modern monographic redescription is needed.
