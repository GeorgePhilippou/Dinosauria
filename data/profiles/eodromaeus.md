## Overview

Eodromaeus murphi ("dawn runner") is one of the earliest known true theropods, described in 2011 from the Ischigualasto Formation of Argentina (~231 Ma). At 1.2 metres long and under 5 kg, it was a small, agile predator whose anatomy is remarkably close to what cladistic analysis predicts for the ancestral theropod — making it a crucial reference point for reconstructing the origin of the theropod lineage.

Found in the same beds as the larger Herrerasaurus and the herbivore Eoraptor, Eodromaeus lived at the very dawn of the dinosaur radiation. Its discovery helped clarify that Eoraptor was actually a basal sauropodomorph rather than a basal theropod, and that true theropods appear in the fossil record as small, fast predators right from the outset.

## Anatomy & life

The skull is elongate and lightly built with large eye sockets. The hindlimbs are long and slender relative to body size, indicating a fast runner. Three large-clawed fingers were suited to grasping small prey. The cervical vertebrae are pneumatised — a character shared with later theropods and birds.

At 231 Ma, Eodromaeus lived in a world still recovering from the Carnian Pluvial Event. In the Ischigualasto fauna, small theropods like Eodromaeus coexisted with the larger Herrerasaurus and a variety of non-dinosaurian archosaurs. Eodromaeus was probably an insectivore and small vertebrate predator, occupying a niche that small theropods would maintain throughout the Mesozoic.

## Fossil record

Martinez and colleagues described Eodromaeus murphi in 2011, naming it for the "dawn" of the theropod lineage. Its skull preserves features consistent with basal theropod ancestry — including antorbital fenestrae, recurved serrated teeth and pneumatised skull bones. Its overall anatomy allowed researchers to reconsider whether Eoraptor (which lacks some theropod features) belonged in Theropoda at all.

Several partial skeletons including substantial cranial and appendicular remains.

A combination of theropod and more basal features places Eodromaeus near the base of the theropod lineage.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic ecosystem of the Ischigualasto basin, one of the earliest well-known dinosaur faunas.

## Open questions

- Its placement as a basal theropod versus non-theropod saurischian remains debated.
- Some skeletal associations and adult size are imperfectly known.
