## Overview

Yangchuanosaurus shangyouensis was a large metatetanuran theropod from the Late Jurassic Shangshaximiao Formation of Sichuan, China (~160 Ma), reaching 10–11 metres. It was the apex predator of the Sichuan dinosaur fauna, which rivals the North American Morrison Formation in scale and richness and includes the stegosaur Tuojiangosaurus and the sauropod Mamenchisaurus.

Two species are known: Y. shangyouensis from 1978 and the larger Y. magnus described in 1983 from the same formation. Yangchuanosaurus belongs to Metatetanurae — large-bodied theropods related to Sinraptor — and shows that large tetanuran theropods were present in Asia during the Middle to Late Jurassic, contemporaneous with Allosaurus in North America.

## Fossil record

Yangchuanosaurus shangyouensis was described by Dong Zhiming and colleagues in 1978 from the Shangshaximiao Formation near Yongchuan, Chongqing. A second, larger species Y. magnus was described in 1983. Both are displayed at the Zigong Dinosaur Museum, which was built directly around the excavation site.

Substantial skulls and partial skeletons from more than one named species.

Large theropod skull, serrated teeth and allosauroid proportions confirm its identity as the apex predator of the Chinese Late Jurassic.

## Where it lived

The Shangshaximiao Formation preserves one of the richest Jurassic dinosaur faunas globally: sauropods (Mamenchisaurus, Omeisaurus), stegosaurs (Tuojiangosaurus, Chialingosaurus), small ornithopods and large theropods. This Chinese fauna is broadly contemporaneous with the Morrison Formation, providing opportunities to compare Laurasian and Asian dinosaur communities during a period of possible intercontinental connection.

Late Jurassic terrestrial ecosystem of Sichuan Basin alongside giant sauropods, stegosaurs and ornithopods.

The Upper Shaximiao occurrences are Oxfordian; the range should not extend through all 163-154 Ma.

## Classification

Yangchuanosaurus had a large skull with prominent ridges and crests, powerful hindlimbs and a typical large-theropod body plan. It is placed within Sinraptoridae — a family of large metatetanurans — alongside the similarly-sized Sinraptor dongi from Xinjiang. These animals occupied the large-predator niche in Asian Jurassic faunas analogous to Allosaurus in North America.

## Open questions

- Y. magnus synonymy and species-specific size remain debated.
