## Overview

Aardonyx celestae was a sauropodomorph dinosaur that lived in what is now South Africa during the Early Jurassic, approximately 201–193 million years ago. It reached an estimated length of over 8 metres and a mass of around 1,500 kilograms, making it one of the larger animals of its time and place. Fossils were recovered from the Elliot Formation of the Free State Province. Uniquely, the known remains represent two juvenile individuals, meaning the adults may have been even larger.

Aardonyx is scientifically significant as a transitional form between bipedal prosauropod ancestors and the fully quadrupedal sauropods that followed. Osteological evidence suggests it habitually walked on its hind legs but was anatomically capable of bearing weight on all four limbs. Named in 2009 by Yates and colleagues, it occupies a position within Sauropodiformes and illuminates the evolutionary pathway leading to the great long-necked giants of the Jurassic.

## Anatomy & life

Aardonyx was a long-necked, small-headed plant-eater from what is now South Africa. The two known animals were about seven metres long when they died, but bone tissue shows that both were still growing, so the proportions and maximum size of a mature animal are not directly known.

Its jaws retain the narrow, pointed outline seen in earlier sauropodomorphs, while other features suggest a wider gape and a shift towards taking in vegetation in bulk. The particular plants it ate are not preserved.

The hindlimbs indicate habitual bipedal movement, but the forelimbs and feet also show features connected with bearing weight on all four limbs. This mosaic of traits makes Aardonyx useful for studying a gradual evolutionary transition rather than a simple halfway form.

- A long neck, small head and deep body placed Aardonyx within the early radiation of large-bodied sauropodomorphs.
- Jaw anatomy combines a narrow snout with features associated with a wider gape and less selective bulk feeding.
- Limb anatomy supports mostly two-legged movement while showing an increasing capacity for four-legged weight-bearing.

**Feeding: Plant-eater.** The skull supports herbivory and a developing bulk-browsing style. No gut contents preserve a specific menu.

**Movement: Mostly bipedal.** Hindlimb anatomy supports habitual bipedality; the forelimbs and feet show an increasing capacity to bear weight.

**Appearance: Skeletal outline supported.** Overall proportions can be inferred from the bones; colour, skin texture and the exact external profile are unknown.

## Fossil record

Aardonyx is known from disarticulated skull, vertebral, girdle and limb bones representing at least two immature individuals. The name-bearing fossil is part of the upper jaw rather than a complete skeleton.

Collectively, the quarry material represents most major parts of the skeleton, including parts of the skull and jaws, vertebral column, shoulder and hip girdles, and all four limbs.

Because the bones were found disarticulated and both animals were still growing, the precise proportions and maximum adult size of Aardonyx remain uncertain.

## Where it lived

## Classification

Aardonyx is an early sauropodomorph close to the evolutionary origin of Sauropoda. It preserves a combination of ancestral and sauropod-like traits, although its exact position changes among phylogenetic analyses.

## Open questions

### How large was an adult?

Both known individuals were still growing. Larger adult estimates are extrapolations rather than measurements from a mature skeleton.

### Which bones belonged together?

The quarry material is disarticulated. The assemblage supports two individuals, but every bone cannot be assigned to a single associated skeleton.

### Exactly where does it sit on the family tree?

Analyses consistently place Aardonyx near the sauropod transition, but its precise relationship to other early sauropodomorphs varies.
