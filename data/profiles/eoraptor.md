## Overview

Eoraptor lunensis was a small early saurischian from the Ischigualasto Formation of Argentina. A nearly complete holotype and additional partial specimens preserve much of the skeleton; modern analyses commonly place it near the base of Sauropodomorpha, though dinosaur-root relationships remain debated.

Eoraptor lunensis ("dawn thief of the valley of the moon") is one of the earliest dinosaurs, from the Ischigualasto Formation of Argentina (~231 Ma). At 1.7 metres long and about 10 kg, it was a small, generalist predator-omnivore with a mixed dentition — leaf-shaped teeth for plant material and recurved teeth for flesh. Its exact position in the dinosaur tree has shifted repeatedly: originally considered a basal theropod, it is now placed as a basal sauropodomorph.

The discovery of Eoraptor in 1991 by Paul Sereno's team, alongside Herrerasaurus and the ornithischian Pisanosaurus, confirmed that the major dinosaur lineages diverged rapidly from a common ancestor in the Late Triassic. Its mixed dentition — an omnivorous toolkit — may represent the ancestral dinosaur condition before the dietary specialisations of later groups evolved.

## Anatomy & life

Eoraptor was a small, lightly built biped only about a metre long and perhaps ten kilograms in weight. It lived in the Ischigualasto valley about 230 million years ago alongside Herrerasaurus, Eodromaeus and a fauna dominated by rhynchosaurs, cynodonts and pseudosuchian archosaurs.

Its skull was low and slender with a long snout. The teeth are the puzzle: the front of the jaw carries leaf-shaped teeth like those of plant-eating sauropodomorphs, while further back they become recurved and serrated like a predator's. This mixture is often read as omnivory, but tooth shape alone cannot settle what it ate.

Named in 1993 as one of the most primitive dinosaurs known, Eoraptor was first treated as a basal theropod. The full description of the skeleton in 2013 moved it to the base of Sauropodomorpha, the lineage leading to the giant sauropods, and most analyses since have followed, though its position remains sensitive to dataset choice.

- A nearly complete skull and skeleton makes it one of the best-preserved Triassic dinosaurs of its size.
- Leaf-shaped front teeth and recurved rear teeth hint at a mixed diet that cannot be confirmed.
- Reclassified from earliest theropod to earliest sauropodomorph as analyses improved.

**Feeding: Uncertain.** Heterodont teeth suggest a varied diet, but no gut contents, wear studies or coprolites tie the animal to specific food.

**Movement: Bipedal.** Long hindlimbs and short arms with grasping hands in the holotype show habitual two-legged movement.

**Appearance: Skeleton well established.** Proportions come from the articulated holotype; skin, colour and any filamentous covering are not preserved.

## Fossil record

Eoraptor rests on the holotype PVSJ 512, an articulated skeleton with skull that preserves the great majority of the animal. Additional partial specimens from the same formation add little that the holotype does not already show.

A complete skull with lower jaws and full dentition, the entire vertebral column with ribs and chevrons, both girdles, forelimbs with hands, and hindlimbs with feet, largely in articulation.

Some elements are crushed or damaged and the tail tip is incomplete; referred specimens are less complete than the holotype.

Mixed tooth shapes and basal anatomy are important for understanding early saurischian evolution.

- The mixed tooth shapes do not by themselves prove omnivory.
- Its lightly built body was bipedal.
- Classification has shifted from basal theropod to basal sauropodomorph or basal saurischian as datasets changed.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic Ischigualasto ecosystem near the early radiation of dinosaurs.

Radiometric dates constrain the fossil-bearing Ischigualasto interval near 231-229 Ma.

## Classification

Eoraptor is an early saurischian whose exact position is contested. The 2013 monograph and most later analyses place it at the base of Sauropodomorpha, but some studies recover it as a basal theropod or as a saurischian outside both groups, so the catalogue marks the sauropodomorph placement with a query.

## Open questions

### What did it eat?

The teeth combine plant-eater and predator shapes. Omnivory is the usual reading, but it remains an inference from tooth form rather than direct evidence.

### Sauropodomorph or theropod?

Its placement shifts with the characters and taxa included in each analysis. The current majority view favours the sauropodomorph stem.

### Was the holotype fully grown?

Bone surface texture and skeletal fusion suggest the animal was not a juvenile, but adult size and growth pattern are not firmly established.
