## Overview

Gasparinisaura cincosaltensis was a small ornithopod from the Late Cretaceous of Neuquén Province, Argentina (~83–80 Ma), about 1.7 metres long. Named after palaeontologist Zulma Gasparini, it is one of the best-documented small ornithopods from South America and helps fill a gap in our understanding of Late Cretaceous South American herbivore diversity.

A bonebed with multiple Gasparinisaura individuals has been found, providing bone histology data showing these animals were fast-growing and reached adult size within a few years — a growth strategy more similar to modern birds than to modern reptiles, consistent with the high metabolic rates now inferred for ornithischians generally.

## Anatomy & life

Bone histology from multiple Gasparinisaura specimens shows rapid growth rates, with the animals reaching adult size within a few years. This pattern — more bird-like than reptile-like — is now recognised as widespread in ornithischians and supports the inference of elevated metabolic rates in non-avian dinosaurs generally.

## Fossil record

Rodolfo Coria and Leonardo Salgado described Gasparinisaura cincosaltensis in 1996 from Neuquén Province. The name honours Zulma Gasparini, a leading Argentine palaeontologist. The type specimen preserves skull and postcranial material; additional specimens from a bonebed extend the sample.

Several partial skulls and skeletons representing multiple growth stages.

Slender limb bones and ornithopod dentition identify it as a small cursorial herbivore in the southern dinosaur fauna.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous Patagonian terrestrial ecosystem.

## Classification

Gasparinisaura's position within Ornithopoda is debated: some analyses place it near the base of the group, others closer to Thescelosaurus or to Iguanodontia. Its relatively primitive features — small size, simple teeth — are consistent with a basal position, but without a clearly diagnostic skull it is difficult to resolve.

## Open questions

- Exact position within Elasmaria and adult maximum size remain uncertain.
- Some specimens are incomplete and taphonomically compressed.
