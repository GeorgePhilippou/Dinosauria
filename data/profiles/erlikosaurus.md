## Overview

Erlikosaurus (named after Erlik, a demon-king in Mongolian mythology) was a therizinosaurian theropod from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 93–84 million years ago during the Turonian to Santonian. Estimated at around 3.4 metres in length, it is known from a skull and partial skeleton, making it one of the better-understood members of Therizinosauria. Unlike most theropods, therizinosaurs were herbivorous or omnivorous, with leaf-shaped teeth, a broad pelvis, and long curved claws used for pulling down vegetation rather than catching prey.

Erlikosaurus andrewsi was described by Barsbold and Perle in 1980 from the Baynshire Formation of Mongolia. Its well-preserved skull provided important information about therizinosaurian cranial anatomy, including the structure of the elongated snout and the nature of its dentition. The genus is placed within Therizinosauria, sometimes called segnosaurs, a group of coelurosaurs that evolved a fundamentally herbivorous lifestyle convergently with sauropods and ornithischians.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Erlikosaurus is among the most complete known from Therizinosauria, featuring a long, low snout and small, leaf-shaped teeth arranged in a battery suitable for processing plant material. Unlike many theropods with pointed, serrated teeth for gripping prey, Erlikosaurus's dentition reflects a dietary shift toward herbivory. The snout may have been used for selective cropping of vegetation.

Therizinosaurs represent one of palaeontology's most striking examples of major dietary evolution within a traditionally carnivorous lineage. Descended from theropod ancestors, they evolved many features convergently with herbivorous ornithischians: broad hips, large guts, and leaf-shaped teeth. Erlikosaurus's anatomy documents this transition, showing a mosaic of retained theropod features alongside derived herbivore adaptations.

In the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, therizinosaurs like Erlikosaurus occupied an unusual ecological position among theropods — primarily herbivorous, they would have competed more with ornithopods and ankylosaurs than with predatory dinosaurs. The long forearms and clawed hands of therizinosaurs are thought to have been used for raking and pulling down leafy branches, possibly reaching higher vegetation than could contemporaneous low-browsing herbivores.

## Fossil record

Erlikosaurus andrewsi was described by Rinchen Barsbold and Altangerel Perle in 1980 from material collected in the Gobi Desert of Mongolia. The genus is named after Erlik Khan, the god of the underworld in Mongolian and Turkic mythology, while the species honours Roy Chapman Andrews, the American explorer whose expeditions to Mongolia in the 1920s revolutionised dinosaur palaeontology.

Nearly complete skull with incomplete associated postcranial skeleton.

- The skull has a keratin-covered beak and small leaf-shaped teeth consistent with predominantly plant processing.
- Large claws and a broad trunk are inferred partly from other therizinosaurs because the type is incomplete.
- A plant-heavy diet is well supported, but exact foods and any animal matter are unknown.

## Where it lived

The only confirmed locality; Turonian-age deposits in the Mongolian Gobi Desert have produced material including the well-preserved skull of Erlikosaurus.

## Open questions

- Much of the postcranium is missing.
- The formation numerical age and detailed feeding ecology remain uncertain.
