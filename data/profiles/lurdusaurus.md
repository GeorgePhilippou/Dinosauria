## Overview

Lurdusaurus was an unusual, heavily built iguanodontian ornithopod dinosaur that lived during the Early Cretaceous (Aptian), approximately 120–112 million years ago, in what is now the Agadez Region of Niger, Africa. It was a large animal estimated at around 9 metres in length, and its robust, low-slung body build has been compared to that of a hippopotamus — suggesting it may have lived a semi-aquatic lifestyle near rivers or lakes. The type species is Lurdusaurus arenatus.

Named in 1999 by Paul Sereno and colleagues, Lurdusaurus is one of the most distinctive ornithopod dinosaurs known, with proportionally short but massively built limbs and an overall body plan unlike any other iguanodontian. It is known from a partial skull and postcranial skeleton, which, while incomplete, provide enough information to identify it as a genuinely unusual anatomical outlier among its relatives.

## Anatomy & life

Lurdusaurus is remarkable among iguanodontians for its extremely robust and stocky body plan, with limb proportions and overall build quite unlike other members of the group. Its short, powerful limbs and heavy barrel-shaped body have led some researchers to suggest a semi-aquatic or amphibious lifestyle analogous to a hippopotamus, though this ecological inference remains speculative. It stands in stark contrast to the generally more gracile, cursorial iguanodontians.

The Elrhaz Formation of Niger preserves a diverse Early Cretaceous ecosystem. Lurdusaurus shared its environment with other large dinosaurs including the sail-backed iguanodontian Ouranosaurus and the spinosaurid theropod Suchomimus, as well as large crocodyliforms and early teleost fishes. This fauna indicates a lush, river-dominated landscape in what is now the Sahara Desert.

## Fossil record

Lurdusaurus arenatus was described in 1999 by Paul Sereno, Dutheil, Iarochene, Larsson, Lyon, Magwene, Sidor, Varricchio, and Wilson based on material from the Elrhaz Formation of Niger. The genus name means 'heavy lizard', referring to its massive build, while arenatus means 'sandy', referencing the desert environment where it was found. The fossils had been collected during expeditions to the Gadoufaoua locality in the Ténéré Desert.

One incomplete associated postcranial skeleton.

## Where it lived

The holotype partial skull and postcranial skeleton of Lurdusaurus arenatus were recovered from Early Cretaceous fluvial deposits in the Ténéré Desert.

## Classification

Lurdusaurus is placed within Iguanodontia, the broad group of ornithopod dinosaurs that includes Iguanodon, hadrosaurs, and their relatives. Its exact position within the group has been debated, with some analyses placing it as a relatively basal iguanodontian outside Hadrosauriformes. Its unusual morphology makes it difficult to place with confidence using available material.

## Open questions

- The skull is unknown and only one principal skeleton is available.
- Body mass, habitual posture and any aquatic association are uncertain.
