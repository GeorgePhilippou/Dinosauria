## Overview

Agilisaurus louderbacki was a small ornithischian dinosaur from the Middle to Late Jurassic of China, estimated to have lived approximately 170–155 million years ago. It was a lightly built biped approximately 1.5 metres long, discovered in Sichuan Province. The genus is known from a nearly complete skull and skeleton recovered from the Lower Shaximiao Formation, making it one of the better-preserved early ornithischians from Asia.

Agilisaurus is classified within Neornithischia and is noteworthy as one of the earliest and most completely known small ornithopods from the Jurassic of Asia. Named by Peng in 1990, it provides important evidence for the early diversification of ornithischian dinosaurs in East Asia. Its relatively complete skeleton has helped researchers understand the ancestral body plan shared by a wide range of later herbivorous dinosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

Agilisaurus was a slender, bipedal herbivore with long hind limbs suited to rapid locomotion. Its skeleton shares features with both basal ornithopods and more derived neornithischians, making its precise phylogenetic placement a subject of ongoing study. The well-preserved skull shows simple leaf-shaped teeth appropriate for processing plant material.

As one of the most completely known basal neornithischians from the Jurassic of Asia, Agilisaurus provides an important reference point for understanding the early evolutionary radiation of ornithischian dinosaurs. Its relatively unspecialised anatomy makes it a useful outgroup for phylogenetic analyses of more derived ornithopods.

## Fossil record

Agilisaurus louderbacki was described by Peng in 1990 from material found in the Lower Shaximiao Formation of Zigong, Sichuan, China. The generic name means 'agile lizard', reflecting its inferred fleet-footed lifestyle. The species name honours geologist George Louderback. The specimen is housed at the Zigong Dinosaur Museum and remains one of the most complete early ornithischian skeletons known from Asia.

One largely complete articulated skeleton with skull.

- The single principal skeleton provides unusually good anatomical evidence for a small Middle Jurassic ornithischian.
- It was bipedal, but diet beyond broad herbivory or omnivory cannot be demonstrated directly.

## Where it lived

The Shaximiao Formation represents a subtropical, seasonally wet environment of the Middle to Late Jurassic. Agilisaurus shared this environment with the sauropods Shunosaurus and Omeisaurus, the stegosaur Huayangosaurus, and the carnivore Gasosaurus. This diverse fauna reflects the high productivity of Jurassic East Asian ecosystems.

A nearly complete skull and skeleton of Agilisaurus were recovered here, making it among the most completely known early neornithischians from the Jurassic of Asia.

The lower Shaximiao Formation is Middle Jurassic, commonly Bathonian-Callovian, rather than Late Jurassic.

## Open questions

- Only one principal individual is known.
- Its position near the base of Neornithischia differs among analyses.
