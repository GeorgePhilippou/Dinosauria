## Overview

Lusotitan was a large herbivorous brachiosaurid sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic (Tithonian), approximately 150 million years ago, in what is now the Lourinhã region of Portugal. Experts estimate it reached around 21 metres in length, though this is an approximation based on incomplete remains; precise dimensions are uncertain as no complete skeleton has been found. The type species is Lusotitan atalaiensis.

Lusotitan was formerly considered a species of Brachiosaurus until a 2003 study by Antunes and Mateus recognised it as a distinct genus. Its name combines Lusitania (the Roman name for the region of modern Portugal) with Titan (from Greek mythology), reflecting both its geographic origin and enormous size. The Jurassic record of Portugal is exceptionally rich in large sauropods, and Lusotitan contributes to our understanding of titanosauriform diversity in Late Jurassic Europe.

## Anatomy & life

No skull of Lusotitan has ever been found, and the known postcranial material is incomplete. This means that reconstructions of its head and many body features are necessarily inferred from related brachiosaurids. The absence of skull material also means that soft tissue features sometimes depicted in popular restorations — such as inflatable air sacs shown in the 2025 BBC documentary Walking With Dinosaurs — have no direct fossil support for Lusotitan specifically.

The Lourinhã Formation of Portugal is one of the most important Late Jurassic fossil sites in Europe, yielding a rich fauna of large sauropods, theropods, stegosaurs, and other dinosaurs. This region was part of a semi-arid coastal environment during the Jurassic, and several of the dinosaur genera found here have close relatives in the Morrison Formation of North America, reflecting faunal connections between Laurasia and Gondwana.

## Fossil record

The fossils originally assigned to Lusotitan were discovered near Atalaia in central Portugal and were initially described in 1957 as Brachiosaurus atalaiensis by de Lapparent and Zbyszewski. In 2003, Miguel Telles Antunes and Octávio Mateus reassessed the material and determined it represented a distinct genus, naming it Lusotitan atalaiensis. The genus name honours Lusitania, the ancient Roman province that roughly covered Portugal, combined with the Titan giants of Greek mythology.

Substantial incomplete postcranial lectotype skeleton with disputed referrals.

- The lectotype represents one incomplete skeleton dominated by vertebral, girdle and limb material.
- Long forelimbs support a high-shouldered brachiosaurid body plan.
- Mass, total length and exact neck posture remain model-dependent.

## Where it lived

Fragmentary postcranial bones attributed to Lusotitan atalaiensis were recovered from Late Jurassic coastal deposits in this highly productive formation.

The Sobral Member of the Lourinha Formation is late Kimmeridgian to early Tithonian.

## Classification

Lusotitan was long grouped with Brachiosaurus because of similarities in overall body plan and certain skeletal features. The 2003 reclassification identified enough distinctive features to justify a separate genus, though both belong to Brachiosauridae. Like Brachiosaurus, Lusotitan would have had forelimbs longer than its hindlimbs, giving it a giraffe-like forward-sloping posture ideal for browsing high vegetation. Whether it was smaller or larger than Brachiosaurus cannot be determined from available material.

## Open questions

- The skull, much of the neck and tail are unknown.
- Some referred Portuguese brachiosaurid material may not belong to the lectotype species.
