## Overview

Eolambia (meaning 'dawn lambeosaurine') was a large herbivorous ornithopod from the Late Cretaceous of Utah, USA, living approximately 99–94 million years ago during the Cenomanian stage. Its body length is not firmly established but is thought to have been substantial, comparable to other early hadrosauroids. Known from skulls and postcrania of multiple individuals spanning juveniles to adults, Eolambia provides valuable information about growth and ontogeny in early hadrosauroid dinosaurs.

Described by James Kirkland in 1998 from material in the Cedar Mountain / Dakota Formation, Eolambia caroljonesa is placed within Hadrosauroidea — the broader group that includes true hadrosaurs (duck-billed dinosaurs). It represents one of the earlier North American members of this lineage, predating the hadrosaurid radiation that would dominate the later Cretaceous. Its evolutionary position helps calibrate when and how hadrosaurs diversified in North America.

## Anatomy & life

The occurrence of both juvenile and adult Eolambia individuals in the Cedar Mountain deposits is scientifically significant, as it allows study of growth patterns in early hadrosauroids. Such ontogenetic series are relatively rare in the fossil record and help researchers understand how hadrosaur body proportions and features changed through development.

The Cedar Mountain Formation preserves a rich Cenomanian vertebrate fauna, including early tyrannosauroids, dromaeosaurids, and a variety of herbivores. Eolambia would have been one of the larger herbivores in this ecosystem, potentially feeding on low to mid-height vegetation. Its environment was characterised by meandering river systems and seasonally flooded coastal plains.

## Fossil record

Eolambia caroljonesa was described by James Kirkland in 1998 from material collected in Utah's Cedar Mountain Formation. The species is named in honour of Carol Jones, who helped with fieldwork. The genus name reflects the original interpretation that it might be an early member of Lambeosaurinae, though subsequent analyses place it more broadly within Hadrosauroidea.

Abundant cranial and postcranial remains from multiple individuals and growth stages.

## Where it lived

The only confirmed locality, producing multiple specimens of juveniles and adults that document ontogenetic variation in the species.

## Classification

Eolambia represents an early stage in the evolution of the hadrosaur lineage in North America, pre-dating the diverse assemblages of hadrosaurids known from the Campanian. Its anatomy preserves a mixture of features shared with basal iguanodontiforms and derived characteristics pointing toward true hadrosaurs, making it an informative transitional taxon for understanding ornithopod evolution.

## Open questions

- Exact placement among non-hadrosaurid hadrosauromorphs varies.
- Body-mass estimates depend on composite reconstruction.
