## Overview

Guaibasaurus (meaning 'Rio Guaiba lizard') was a small, early dinosaur from the Late Triassic of southern Brazil, living approximately 227–206 million years ago. Known from partial skeletal material collected from the Caturrita Formation (also known as the Rosário do Sul Group) of Rio Grande do Sul, it reached an estimated length of about 1.8 metres. It is classified within Guaibasauridae, a family of early dinosaurs close to the base of Saurischia, and was described by Bonaparte, Ferigolo and Ribeiro in 1999.

Guaibasaurus is scientifically significant as one of the earliest dinosaurs known, appearing near the base of the dinosaur family tree at a time when dinosaurs were first diversifying during the Norian stage of the Late Triassic. Its classification has been debated, with some researchers placing it close to the origin of sauropodomorphs or theropods, while others consider it an early saurischian of uncertain affinity. The Triassic fauna of southern Brazil where it is found represents one of the best records of early dinosaur evolution globally.

## Anatomy & life

As one of the earliest known dinosaurs, Guaibasaurus helps palaeontologists understand the initial radiation of the group during the Late Triassic. Its uncertain phylogenetic position is itself informative: it highlights how blurred the anatomical boundaries between early dinosaurian lineages were, and underscores the importance of southern South America as a cradle of early dinosaur evolution.

## Fossil record

Guaibasaurus candelariensis was described in 1999 by José Bonaparte, Joel Ferigolo and Ana Maria Ribeiro from specimens found in Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil. The genus name honours the Rio Guaíba near where the fossils were collected, and the species name refers to the Candelária region. Multiple specimens have been recovered, providing reasonably good knowledge of its skeletal anatomy.

Several incomplete postcranial skeletons, with little diagnostic cranial evidence.

- No complete diagnostic skull is known.
- A crouched pose in one specimen records burial posture, not habitual resting behaviour with certainty.
- Diet cannot be stated confidently while its relationships and teeth remain poorly known.

## Where it lived

The Caturrita Formation records a terrestrial environment of Late Triassic Gondwana, characterised by river systems and floodplains in what is now southern Brazil. Guaibasaurus shared this environment with other early dinosaurs and a diverse array of non-dinosaurian archosaurs, reflecting the period when dinosaurs were beginning to diversify but had not yet achieved ecological dominance.

A key site for early dinosaur evolution in South America, preserving Norian-age material including multiple Guaibasaurus specimens.

The Caturrita Formation is Norian; its numerical duration remains subject to South American Triassic correlations.

## Classification

Guaibasaurus was a slender, lightly built dinosaur estimated at around 1.8 metres in length. Its classification has been debated, with some analyses placing it as a basal saurischian outside both Theropoda and Sauropodomorpha, and others recovering it within early sauropodomorph or herrerasaurid lineages. Its phylogenetic position remains somewhat uncertain given the fragmentary nature of early dinosaur material.

## Open questions

- The skull is essentially unknown.
- Its position at the base of Saurischia and resulting dietary inference remain disputed.
