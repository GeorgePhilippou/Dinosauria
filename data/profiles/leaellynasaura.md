## Overview

Leaellynasaura was a small herbivorous ornithischian dinosaur that lived during the late Aptian to early Albian stage of the Early Cretaceous, approximately 118–110 million years ago, in what is now Dinosaur Cove, Victoria, Australia. Its estimated length was around 2 metres, making it a lightly built bipedal animal adapted to a high-latitude polar environment that would have experienced extended periods of winter darkness. The only known species is Leaellynasaura amicagraphica.

Described in 1989 by Tom Rich and Patricia Vickers-Rich, Leaellynasaura is notable for its proportionally large eye sockets, which led to speculation that it had enlarged eyes as an adaptation for seeing in low-light polar winters — though this inference remains debated. Its discovery at Dinosaur Cove, a site painstakingly excavated from sea cliffs, provided critical evidence that dinosaurs inhabited polar regions and raised questions about their thermoregulatory capabilities.

## Anatomy & life

The large orbits in Leaellynasaura's skull led to the widely popularised suggestion that it possessed unusually large eyes for navigating polar darkness. However, orbit size does not directly measure eye size in dinosaurs, and the inference remains speculative. What is not in dispute is that Leaellynasaura inhabited a polar environment that experienced seasonal darkness, raising genuine questions about how it survived winter months.

## Fossil record

Leaellynasaura amicagraphica was described in 1989 by Australian palaeontologists Tom Rich and Patricia Vickers-Rich. The genus is named after their daughter Leaellyn Rich, while the species name amicagraphica means 'friend writing', honouring both the Friends of the Museum of Victoria and the National Geographic Society for their financial support of Australian palaeontology. Fossils were recovered from the spectacularly difficult excavation site at Dinosaur Cove on the Victorian coast.

Incomplete type cranium and limited referred jaw material; postcranial referrals disputed.

## Where it lived

Leaellynasaura is among the best-known dinosaurs from high-palaeolatitude Early Cretaceous Australia, which at that time sat within the Antarctic Circle. Its presence contributes to understanding how polar dinosaur communities were structured and provides evidence that small ornithopods were ecologically successful even in harsh seasonal environments.

The holotype and referred specimens were recovered from Early Cretaceous fluvial sediments in coastal sea-cliff outcrops.

## Classification

Leaellynasaura has been classified as a basal ornithopod, though its exact phylogenetic position has been debated. Some analyses place it close to hypsilophodontid-grade ornithopods, a grouping now understood to be paraphyletic. Its precise relationships to other Australian ornithischians such as Atlascopcosaurus remain uncertain.

## Open questions

- The postcranial hypodigm is unresolved.
- Fine phylogenetic position and adult size remain uncertain.
