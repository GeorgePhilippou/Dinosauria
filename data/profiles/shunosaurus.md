## Overview

Shunosaurus (meaning 'Shu lizard', after the ancient name for Sichuan) was a medium-sized sauropod dinosaur from the Middle to Late Jurassic of Sichuan Province, China, living approximately 170–157 million years ago. It reached about 10 metres in length and is one of the best-known early sauropods, represented by dozens of specimens including several nearly complete skeletons. Shunosaurus was a eusauropod, an evolutionary grade that forms the base of the more derived Neosauropoda.

Named by Dong, Zhou, and Zhang in 1983, Shunosaurus lii is particularly significant for possessing a bony tail club — a feature previously known only in the derived ankylosaurs and recently confirmed in Shunosaurus by articulated tail material. This convergent evolution of a defensive tail weapon in an early sauropod has attracted considerable attention. Shunosaurus shared its habitat with other Chinese Jurassic sauropods such as Omeisaurus and Datousaurus, as well as stegosaurs like Huayangosaurus.

## Anatomy & life

One of the most striking features of Shunosaurus is its tail club — a mass of fused vertebrae and osteoderms at the tail tip. This structure had previously been considered unique to ankylosaurs, making its presence in an early sauropod a striking example of convergent evolution. The club was likely used for defence against predators such as the contemporaneous theropod Gasosaurus.

Shunosaurus had a moderately long neck compared to later, more derived sauropods, with spoon-shaped teeth well suited to cropping vegetation. Its limb proportions suggest a slower, more deliberate gait than its descendants. As a eusauropod, it was herbivorous, likely browsing on low to mid-level vegetation in the lush floodplain environments of Jurassic Sichuan.

## Fossil record

Shunosaurus lii was formally described by Dong Zhiming, Zhou Shiwu, and Zhang Yihong in 1983, based on excavations at Dashanpu, Zigong, Sichuan, which began in 1979. The discovery of multiple articulated skeletons made Shunosaurus one of the most completely known Jurassic sauropods globally. The genus name references 'Shu', a poetic name for Sichuan, and the species honours palaeontologist Li Bing.

Numerous partial and articulated skeletons with skulls and tail clubs.

- A bony tail club formed by fused terminal vertebrae and osteoderms is directly preserved.
- Several skeletons collectively document nearly the whole body, but reconstructions remain composites.

## Where it lived

The Lower Shaximiao Formation preserves a remarkably diverse dinosaur fauna including multiple sauropods (Shunosaurus, Datousaurus, Omeisaurus, Protognathosaurus), the early stegosaur Huayangosaurus, and the ornithopod Xiaosaurus. This assemblage gives a rich picture of Middle Jurassic Asian dinosaur diversity.

Dozens of Shunosaurus specimens, including near-complete skeletons, have been recovered from these Middle Jurassic fluvial and lacustrine beds.

The Lower Shaximiao sample is Middle Jurassic, broadly Bathonian-Callovian.
