## Overview

Conchoraptor was a small oviraptorid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, approximately 84–72 million years ago (Campanian), found in the Baruungoyot Formation of the Gobi Desert. Estimated at about 1.5 metres in length, it was a feathered omnivore with a toothless beak and no cranial crest, distinguishing it from its crested relative Oviraptor. Its name, meaning 'conch thief', reflects an early (and now uncertain) hypothesis that it fed on molluscs using its strong beak.

Named by Barsbold in 1986, Conchoraptor gracilis is placed within Oviraptoridae, within the subfamily Heyuanninae (also referred to as Ingeniinae). Remarkably, some specimens preserve evidence of feathers and show differences in plumage between sexes, making Conchoraptor one of the few non-avian dinosaurs for which sexual differences in integument have been suggested. It is one of the best-known small oviraptorids from the Mongolian Late Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Some specimens of Conchoraptor preserve evidence of feathers, confirming that it was feathered like other maniraptoran theropods. Notably, differences in plumage features between specimens have been interpreted as possible sexual dimorphism—one of the few cases where such a distinction has been proposed for a non-avian dinosaur. If confirmed, this would suggest that feather displays played a role in sexual selection in oviraptorids.

Conchoraptor is distinguished from many oviraptorids by its lack of a cranial crest. This feature has been used to distinguish it from crested taxa like Oviraptor and Citipati. The beak was robust and the jaws were powered by large jaw muscles, suggesting an ability to process hard food items. Its forelimbs were long with three-fingered hands, and the overall build was lightly constructed.

## Fossil record

Conchoraptor gracilis was described by Rinchen Barsbold in 1986 from multiple specimens collected from the Baruungoyot Formation of Mongolia. The genus name references the hypothesis that it used its strong beak to crack open mollusc shells, though this diet has not been confirmed. The species name gracilis means 'slender', referencing the lightly built skeleton. Multiple specimens of various sizes are known.

Multiple skulls and incomplete postcranial skeletons.

## Where it lived

The Baruungoyot Formation was deposited in a desert environment during the Late Cretaceous Campanian. The fauna includes protoceratopsids, ankylosaurs, small theropods, and multiple oviraptorid genera. Conchoraptor coexisted with larger oviraptorids and likely occupied a slightly different ecological niche owing to its smaller size and distinct jaw morphology.

Late Campanian Gobi Desert sandstones yielding the primary Conchoraptor specimens with preserved feather evidence.

## Open questions

- Diet is unknown despite the shellfish-based name.
- Several small oviraptorid taxa from Mongolia are difficult to distinguish.
- No direct plumage is preserved.
