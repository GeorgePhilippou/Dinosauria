## Overview

Segisaurus (meaning 'Segi Canyon lizard') was a small carnivorous theropod dinosaur from the Middle Jurassic of what is now northeastern Arizona, USA, living approximately 195–180 million years ago. It is estimated to have been about 1.5 metres long and likely weighed only a few kilograms, making it one of the smaller members of the Coelophysidae. Only a partial postcranial skeleton of a probable subadult has been recovered, so body proportions are inferred partly from close relatives.

Named by Charles Lewis Camp in 1936, Segisaurus halli is notable as one of the very few Jurassic coelophysid theropods known from North America. The absence of fused clavicles — bones that are fused in most theropods — was long regarded as a primitive feature, though later work suggested the holotype may simply have been a juvenile. Segisaurus helps palaeontologists chart the early diversification of small cursorial theropods in North America.

## Anatomy & life

With an estimated length of around 1.5 metres, Segisaurus was a slender, lightly built predator. Its hindlimbs suggest a cursorial lifestyle, and the forelimbs bore small grasping hands. An unusual absence of fused collarbones was once seen as primitive, but may reflect the young age of the individual.

As a small coelophysid, Segisaurus was almost certainly a carnivore, likely preying on insects, small lizards, and other invertebrates. Its desert environment — the Navajo Formation represents ancient dune fields — suggests it was adapted to arid conditions similar to those inferred for Coelophysis.

## Fossil record

The holotype of Segisaurus halli was collected in 1933 from Tsegi Canyon (Segi Canyon), Arizona, during an expedition led by Charles Lewis Camp. Camp formally named the genus and species in 1936, honouring discoverer J. W. Hall. The material consists solely of postcranial bones — no skull has been found — making detailed dietary and sensory inferences difficult.

## Where it lived

The Navajo Formation was deposited in a vast erg (sand-sea) environment during the Early to Middle Jurassic. Segisaurus was among the earliest vertebrates known from this hostile setting, sharing its habitat with early crocodylomorphs and lizard-like archosaurs preserved in adjacent oasis deposits.

The holotype partial skeleton was recovered from early Jurassic aeolian sandstones of the Navajo Formation in Tsegi Canyon.
