## Overview

Tarchia (meaning 'brainy one', from the Mongolian word tarkhi for brain) was a large ankylosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 75–70 million years ago. It reached about 5.5 metres in length and was heavily armoured, bearing a characteristic bony tail club. Multiple specimens have been recovered from the Gobi Desert, and in 2021 a new specimen was found with impact damage to the tail and hips, suggesting it had been struck by the tail club of another ankylosaur during intraspecific combat.

Named by Maryanska in 1977 and revised with species Tarchia kielanae, this dinosaur shared its environment with the ankylosaurs Saichania and Zaraapelta, as well as the apex predator Tarbosaurus — a close relative of Tyrannosaurus. Tarbosaurus tooth marks have been found on a Tarchia skull, confirming that it was preyed upon despite its armour. Tarchia had a proportionally larger braincase than Saichania, which is the origin of its evocative name, though this does not necessarily indicate greater intelligence.

## Anatomy & life

In 2021, researchers described a Tarchia specimen bearing healed and unhealed bone damage to the hip region and base of the tail consistent with impact from another ankylosaur's tail club. This provides rare direct physical evidence of combat between individuals of the same species — or possibly between different sympatric ankylosaur species sharing the same habitat — rather than predator defence.

Tarbosaurus, the dominant large theropod of the Mongolian Late Cretaceous, coexisted with Tarchia, and a Tarchia skull bearing Tarbosaurus tooth marks confirms that the giant predator targeted armoured prey when opportunity arose. The armour and tail club of Tarchia were presumably important deterrents, but they did not provide complete protection from a predator as large and powerful as Tarbosaurus.

Tarchia, Saichania, and Zaraapelta form a triplet of ankylosaurs that co-occurred in the Late Cretaceous Gobi Desert. Their coexistence suggests they may have partitioned resources through subtle habitat or dietary differences, though the mechanisms of co-occurrence among closely related large-bodied herbivores in this arid or semi-arid environment are not fully understood.

## Fossil record

Tarchia was named by Teresa Maryanska in 1977 from specimens collected during Polish-Mongolian expeditions to the Gobi Desert. The genus name, derived from the Mongolian word tarkhi (brain), was chosen because this ankylosaur has a proportionally larger braincase than its close relative Saichania. The type species was initially T. gigantea, but revision has established T. kielanae as the valid species, honouring palaeontologist Zofia Kielan-Jaworowska.

Type skull and limited postcranium plus numerous historically referred specimens.

- The type is not a complete skeleton.
- Minotaurasaurus has often been synonymised with Tarchia, while recent revisions recognise more than one Tarchia species.

## Where it lived

Tarchia specimens have been recovered from the Nemegt Formation, a Late Cretaceous fluvial-deltaic deposit in the Gobi Desert famous for its diverse dinosaur fauna.

The type species is late Campanian Baruungoyot Formation; younger Nemegt referrals need separate review.
