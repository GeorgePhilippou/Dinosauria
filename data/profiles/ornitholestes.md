## Overview

Ornitholestes hermanni was a small Late Jurassic coelurosaur of the Morrison Formation (~155–150 Ma), about two metres long and probably weighing around 12 kg. Its name means "bird robber," coined under the assumption it was fast enough to catch early birds — though no birds are known from the Morrison, and its actual diet likely consisted of small lizards, mammals and insects.

A single, well-preserved but somewhat crushed skull and associated postcranium from the Bone Cabin Quarry in Wyoming represents the only known specimen. The skull originally appeared to bear a small nasal horn (leading to many reconstructions showing one), but this protrusion is now considered a taphonomic artefact — a displaced piece of skull bone.

## Anatomy & life

Ornitholestes had slender proportions, a moderately long neck, three-fingered hands with strong curved claws and hindlimbs adapted for fast running. The skull bears an enlarged nasal region that supports the narial horn misidentification. It was among the smallest predators in the Morrison fauna, exploiting prey too small for Allosaurus or Ceratosaurus.

Ornitholestes occupied the small-predator niche in the Morrison alongside Tanycolagreus and small ornithomimosaur relatives. It would have been prey for larger theropods and in turn preyed on the lizards, early mammals and invertebrates that are also known from Morrison deposits. Its classification within Coelurosauria — even the specific placement — remains debated due to limited material.

## Fossil record

The only specimen was collected by the American Museum of Natural History's Bone Cabin Quarry expedition in 1900 and described by Henry Fairfield Osborn in 1903. The skull was initially reconstructed with a nasal horn; detailed re-examination by Carpenter and colleagues in 2005 identified this as an artefact of crushing and preservation, removing the horn from reconstructions.

Single incomplete articulated skeleton with crushed skull and substantial postcranium.

The complete skeleton documents a small, lightly built coelurosaur; the controversial nasal crest is only preserved in crushed condition.

- The holotype preserves much of the skeleton but lacks portions and has a distorted skull.
- A nasal horn once reconstructed from a displaced bone is not supported.
- Carnivory and bipedality are secure; prey choice, plumage extent and exact coelurosaur branch are uncertain.

## Where it lived

Late Jurassic Morrison floodplains alongside the largest animals that had ever lived on Earth.

The Bone Cabin Quarry holotype is Kimmeridgian Morrison Formation, near 154-152 Ma.

## Open questions

- Only one principal skeleton is known.
- Skull distortion complicates reconstruction and relationships.
