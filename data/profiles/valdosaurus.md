## Overview

Valdosaurus ('Wealden lizard') was a small ornithopod dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of England, living approximately 142–121 million years ago during the Berriasian–Barremian stages. Reaching about 3 metres in length, it was a bipedal herbivore known from postcranial bones, teeth, and dentary elements rather than a complete skeleton. Fossils are primarily known from the Wealden Group of southern England, with additional material tentatively referred to from Romania and Niger.

Named by Peter Galton in 1975, Valdosaurus canaliculatus is placed within Dryosauridae, a family of relatively advanced ornithopods that also includes the African and North American Dryosaurus. Its presence in both England and possibly other regions during the Early Cretaceous provides data on the global distribution of dryosaurids and their persistence from the Jurassic into the Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Known material of Valdosaurus includes femora, other hindlimb elements, teeth, and dentary fragments, but no complete skull or skeleton has been described. Its assignment to Dryosauridae is based on femoral morphology consistent with that family. Without cranial material, many aspects of its dietary specialisation remain inferred from comparison with better-known dryosaurids such as Dryosaurus and Dysalotosaurus.

Material from Romania and Niger has at various times been tentatively referred to Valdosaurus, potentially extending its known range, but these referrals are uncertain given the fragmentary nature of the specimens. If correct, this wide distribution would reflect the geographic connections between Early Cretaceous Laurasian landmasses. Most researchers treat these referrals with caution pending more diagnostic material.

## Fossil record

Valdosaurus canaliculatus was named by Peter Galton in 1975 from Early Cretaceous Wealden material from the Isle of Wight and elsewhere in southern England. The generic name references the Wealden, the collective term for a series of Early Cretaceous non-marine deposits across southern England and the southern North Sea area. The species name canaliculatus refers to a groove (channel) on the femur.

Paired type femora plus limited referred British postcranial material.

## Where it lived

Valdosaurus lived in the fluvial and lagoonal environments of the Wealden Basin, sharing its habitat with large predators such as Baryonyx and Neovenator, sauropods, and other ornithopods including Iguanodon and Hypsilophodon. As a relatively small herbivore, it likely occupied a different feeding niche from the large iguanodonts, perhaps browsing on lower vegetation along watercourses.

Primary source of Valdosaurus material; the Isle of Wight Wealden is one of England's richest Early Cretaceous dinosaur sites.
