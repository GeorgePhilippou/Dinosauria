## Overview

Irritator challengeri is a spinosaurid from the Early Cretaceous Santana Formation of Brazil (~110 Ma), known from a nearly complete skull — one of the best-preserved spinosaurid skulls from South America. Its name reflects the frustration of its describers: the specimen had been heavily restored with plaster by commercial collectors before reaching scientific hands, requiring painstaking preparation to separate real bone from filler.

As a spinosaurid closely related to Suchomimus and Baryonyx (subfamily Baryonychinae), Irritator provides important information about the distribution and diversity of this group in South America. The Santana Formation preserves an extraordinary freshwater and estuarine fauna including giant fish and the pterosaur Anhanguera.

## Anatomy & life

The skull is narrow and elongate with a rounded sagittal crest running along the top. The teeth are conical and slightly oval in cross-section — typical of baryonychine spinosaurids. Isotope analysis of Irritator teeth shows values intermediate between terrestrial and aquatic prey, consistent with a mixed fish-and-terrestrial diet.

The Romualdo Member preserves an extraordinary coastal to estuarine ecosystem. Giant fish (Calamopleurus, Vinctifer) reaching 4 m co-existed with Irritator, pterosaurs and early chelonians. The spinosaurid feeding on fish in this environment mirrors the ecology reconstructed for Baryonyx in England and Suchomimus in Niger, confirming the piscivorous specialisation of baryonychines across their Gondwanan and Laurasian range.

## Fossil record

The skull was found by commercial fossil collectors and sold; by the time it reached scientific study (described by Martill, Cruickshank and colleagues in 1996) the specimen had been plastered and altered to appear more complete. Removing the fake material and identifying the real anatomy required considerable effort — hence the name Irritator. A second species proposed in 2004 (Angaturama limai) is now considered a synonym of Irritator.

One nearly complete but altered and restored skull without associated postcranium.

The elongated skull with conical teeth is diagnostic for spinosaurids; the Brazilian occurrence extends the family's known range to South America.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous South American environment with rivers, lakes and coastal settings.

## Open questions

- Postcranial anatomy is unknown for the holotype.
- Angaturama synonymy, precise provenance and feeding breadth remain unresolved.
