## Overview

Bagaceratops rozhdestvenskyi was a small protoceratopsid ceratopsian dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia and Inner Mongolia, China, living approximately 80–66 million years ago during the Campanian to Maastrichtian stages. Estimated at about 1 metre in length, it was a ground-dwelling herbivore with a small bony frill at the back of its skull and a small nasal horn. It is known from numerous specimens including adults and juveniles recovered from the Baruungoyot Formation and Djadokhta Formation.

Named by Maryanska and Osmólska in 1975, Bagaceratops is classified within Protoceratopsidae and is one of the most abundantly known small ceratopsians from the Late Cretaceous of Asia. The rich fossil record of this genus, spanning multiple individuals and growth stages, has made it valuable for studying ontogenetic changes in ceratopsian skull morphology. Its name means 'small-horned face'.

## Anatomy & life

Bagaceratops was a small, stout quadruped with a relatively large head bearing a small but distinct nasal horn and a short bony frill projecting from the back of the skull. The frill was more developed in adults than in juveniles, and its margins bore small epoccipital bones. Compared to Protoceratops, Bagaceratops had a shorter, more robust skull and a more reduced frill. The beak was sharp and well suited to cropping tough vegetation.

The relatively large number of Bagaceratops specimens spanning multiple growth stages has allowed researchers to study how the skull changed through development. Studies have shown that the frill and nasal horn became proportionally larger as individuals grew. The presence of juveniles alongside adults in some deposits suggests that young individuals may have stayed with or near adult animals, though direct evidence of herding behaviour is limited.

## Fossil record

Bagaceratops rozhdestvenskyi was described by Teresa Maryanska and Halszka Osmólska in 1975 from material found during Polish-Mongolian palaeontological expeditions to the Gobi Desert. The generic name combines Mongolian baga (small) with Greek ceratops (horned face), meaning 'small-horned face'. The species name honours Anatoly Rozhdestvensky, the Soviet palaeontologist who contributed greatly to the study of Asian dinosaurs.

Numerous skulls and partial postcranial skeletons from multiple growth stages.

## Where it lived

Bagaceratops inhabited the arid, sand-dune and dry-riverbed environments of the Late Cretaceous Gobi Desert. The Baruungoyot Formation represents a desert or semi-desert setting with periodic river activity. Bagaceratops coexisted with small theropods, ankylosaurs, and oviraptorosaurs in this challenging environment and was likely a browser of low-growing desert scrub vegetation.

Multiple Bagaceratops specimens including adults and juveniles have been found here, making it the primary source of material for the genus.

## Open questions

- Synonymy of Gobiceratops, Lamaceratops, Platyceratops and Magnirostris is influential but not universally adopted.
- Formation age is broad.
