## Overview

Anchiceratops was a chasmosaurine ceratopsid from Alberta, Canada, living near the end of the Cretaceous. It had the familiar chasmosaurine plan of long brow horns and a large frill, with a rectangular frill margin ornamented by coarse triangular projections.

About a dozen skulls have been attributed to the genus, making it better represented than many of the imported profiles. PBDB lists records from the Horseshoe Canyon / Edmonton and St. Mary River formations, spanning late Campanian to early Maastrichtian time.

## Anatomy & life

Anchiceratops documents chasmosaurine diversity after the classic Dinosaur Park Formation faunas and before the final Triceratops-dominated latest Cretaceous.

Skull material is comparatively good, but postcranial anatomy and population biology remain less clear than in better-sampled ceratopsids.

## Fossil record

Multiple skulls and partial postcranial skeletons.

## Open questions

- The interpretation of skull variation remains debated.
- Some referred material lacks precise stratigraphic control.
