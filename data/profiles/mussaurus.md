## Overview

Mussaurus (meaning 'mouse lizard') was a herbivorous sauropodomorph dinosaur that lived during the Early Jurassic (Sinemurian stage, approximately 199–193 million years ago) in what is now Santa Cruz Province, Patagonia, Argentina. Fossils come from the Laguna Colorada / El Tranquilo Formation. Adult individuals could reach roughly 3 metres in length, while the juveniles and hatchlings that were originally discovered were tiny enough to fit in a human hand — giving rise to the misleading name.

Named by Bonaparte and Vince in 1979 on the basis of juvenile material, Mussaurus became scientifically important when adult specimens were later found, showing the genus is a sauropodiform rather than a prosauropod in the narrow sense. PBDB places it within Sauropodiformes. The discovery of nests and eggs alongside skeletal material has shed light on early sauropodomorph reproductive biology and social behaviour.

## Anatomy & life

The dramatic size difference between hatchlings and adults in Mussaurus — from hand-sized juveniles to 3-metre adults — makes it a key taxon for studying dinosaur growth rates and ontogenetic changes. Bone histology studies suggest sauropodomorphs like Mussaurus grew rapidly compared to other reptiles, a trait shared with later sauropods.

Multiple juveniles and eggs were found together at the type locality, suggesting gregarious nesting behaviour or parental association. This early evidence of possible herding or communal nesting in sauropodomorphs has been cited in broader discussions of social behaviour evolution in dinosaurs.

## Fossil record

Mussaurus was named by José Bonaparte and Martín Vince in 1979. The type specimens were tiny hatchling-sized skeletons from Patagonia, which led to the name 'mouse lizard'. The species epithet patagonicus refers to Patagonia. Subsequent fieldwork recovered adult material that fundamentally changed understanding of the genus.

Eggs and multiple articulated skeletons spanning hatchling to adult growth stages.

- A growth series documents major changes from quadrupedal hatchlings to predominantly bipedal adults.
- Egg clutches and age-segregated groups provide strong evidence of colonial nesting and social aggregation.
- Herbivory is inferred from sauropodomorph anatomy; exact plant selection is unknown.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic to Early Jurassic continental deposits yielding nests, eggs, juveniles and adult Mussaurus material.

Radiometric and stratigraphic work places the nesting locality in the Early Jurassic, not the Late Triassic.

## Classification

Originally placed among 'prosauropods', Mussaurus is now recognised as a sauropodiform — a grade more closely related to true sauropods than to plateosaurids. This reclassification, supported by cladistic analyses of limb and vertebral characters, highlights the paraphyletic nature of 'Prosauropoda' as traditionally defined.

## Open questions

- The precise locomotor transition through growth remains model-dependent.
- Social evidence represents particular assemblages, not every population.
