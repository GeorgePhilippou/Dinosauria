## Overview

Zalmoxes was a rhabdodontid ornithopod dinosaur from the Maastrichtian stage of the Late Cretaceous in what is now Romania, living approximately 69 million years ago right near the end of the Mesozoic. Reaching about 3 metres in length, it was a bipedal herbivore that represents one of the most common vertebrates in the latest Cretaceous of Transylvania. Two species are recognised: the type Z. robustus and the more gracile Z. shqiperorum.

The genus was established in 2003 by Weishampel and colleagues, though its fossils were first described as far back as 1899 by Franz Nopcsa as Mochlodon robustum. The name Zalmoxes honours the Dacian deity Zalmoxis. Scientifically, Zalmoxes is notable as a member of Rhabdodontidae, a group of European ornithopods that may illustrate island dwarfism or distinct evolutionary trajectories on the ancient European archipelago during the Late Cretaceous — the so-called 'Hateg Island' fauna.

## Anatomy & life

Zalmoxes belongs to Rhabdodontidae, a family of ornithopod dinosaurs known from the Late Cretaceous of Europe. Rhabdodontids had robust, laterally compressed teeth adapted for tough plant material and were among the dominant herbivores on European Cretaceous islands. The family also includes Rhabdodon from France and Spain, and together they represent a distinctly European radiation of ornithopod dinosaurs.

Zalmoxes robustus, the type species, is characterised by more robust bones, while Z. shqiperorum (also described in 2003) is more gracile. Notably, Z. shqiperorum was later reassigned by some researchers to a separate ceratopsian genus (Ferenceratops), though this reassignment has been questioned. If the two forms represent different ontogenetic stages rather than distinct species, the taxonomy of Zalmoxes would need further revision.

## Fossil record

The material now attributed to Zalmoxes was first described in 1899 by the pioneering Transylvanian palaeontologist Franz Nopcsa as Mochlodon robustum, before he reclassified it as Rhabdodon robustum in 1915. A 1990 name correction changed this to Rhabdodon robustus, and in 2003, Weishampel, Csiki, Benton, Buffetaut, and Grigorescu formally erected the genus Zalmoxes to accommodate the Transylvanian material distinct from the French Rhabdodon. The name derives from Zalmoxis, a deity of the ancient Dacian people who inhabited the region.

Many disarticulated cranial and postcranial elements across two species.

## Where it lived

The primary Maastrichtian deposit of the Hateg Basin from which most Zalmoxes material has been recovered.

## Classification

During the Maastrichtian, the Hateg Basin formed part of an island or island chain in the Tethys Sea (the 'Hateg Island' hypothesis proposed by Nopcsa and later elaborated by others). The Hateg fauna includes titanosaur sauropods (Magyarosaurus, considered a dwarf form), ankylosaurs (Struthiosaurus), and the unusual pterosaur Hatzegopteryx. Some researchers have proposed that Zalmoxes itself might be a dwarfed form, though this interpretation is debated.
