## Overview

Othnielia was a small ornithischian dinosaur from the Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian-Tithonian, approximately 155–143 million years ago) of the Morrison Formation in Wyoming and Colorado, USA. The type species is Othnielia rex, and at around 1.4 metres in length it was a lightly built, bipedal herbivore. It was described by Peter Galton in 1977, honouring the palaeontologist Othniel Charles Marsh.

Othnielia has a complicated taxonomic history and its validity as a distinct genus is uncertain: the NHM notes that 'scientists no longer consider this a valid dinosaur', reflecting ongoing debate about whether the material is diagnosable. PBDB places it within Ornithopoda without a family assignment. Much of the material that was formerly attributed to Othnielia has been reassigned to Othnielosaurus or considered undiagnosable, making this one of the more problematic ornithopod taxa from the Morrison Formation.

## Anatomy & life

Small ornithopods were a component of the famously diverse Morrison Formation fauna, sharing the landscape with large sauropods such as Diplodocus and Brachiosaurus, stegosaurs, and large theropods like Allosaurus and Ceratosaurus. Their small body size, bipedal agility and herbivorous diet suggest they were ecological counterparts of modern small cursorial mammals.

Despite its uncertain validity, 'Othnielia' material from the Morrison Formation has contributed to understanding the diversity of small ornithischians in the Late Jurassic. The specimens have been important reference points in discussions of basal ornithopod phylogeny, even as the taxon itself has been progressively dismantled through taxonomic revision.

## Fossil record

Othnielia rex was named by Peter Galton in 1977, honouring Othniel Charles Marsh, the American palaeontologist famous for his role in the 'Bone Wars'. The material comes from the Morrison Formation of the American West. The species name rex means 'king', a somewhat ironic choice for such a small animal.

Nondiagnostic isolated femur; historically referred skeletons cannot be attached securely to the name.

## Where it lived

Late Jurassic semi-arid floodplain deposits of Wyoming where material attributed to Othnielia has been collected.

## Classification

The taxonomy of Othnielia has been highly unstable. Material originally referred to the genus has been variously assigned to Laosaurus, Nanosaurus and other small Morrison ornithopods over the decades. Galton and others later argued that the holotype material is too fragmentary to diagnose, and much of the referred material has been transferred to Othnielosaurus consors. Some researchers regard Othnielia as a nomen dubium.
