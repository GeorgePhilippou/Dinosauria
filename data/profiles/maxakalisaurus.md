## Overview

Maxakalisaurus was a large titanosaurian sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous, approximately 80 million years ago, in what is now Minas Gerais State, Brazil. Estimated at around 13 metres in length, it was a medium-to-large titanosaur with ridged teeth unusual for sauropods, and its skeleton preserved osteoderms — bony skin armour elements that titanosaurs are known to possess but which are rarely preserved. The type species is Maxakalisaurus topai.

Described in 2006 by Kellner and colleagues, Maxakalisaurus is named after the Maxakali indigenous people of Minas Gerais. The fossil material includes partial skull fragments, vertebrae, and osteoderms, and notably bears tooth marks on several bones consistent with scavenging by theropods after death, providing a rare direct record of post-mortem bone modification. It contributes to the growing but still relatively incomplete picture of South American titanosaur diversity in the Late Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Unlike most sauropods, which have simple peg-like or spoon-shaped teeth, Maxakalisaurus possessed ridged teeth. This feature is unusual within Titanosauria and may reflect dietary specialisation, though the ecological significance of this dentition remains uncertain. Ridged teeth in sauropods are known in only a small number of other genera, making Maxakalisaurus noteworthy for this characteristic.

The skeleton of Maxakalisaurus preserves osteoderms — bony dermal armour elements embedded in the skin — which are known from various titanosaurs but rarely preserved in articulation. Several bones of the specimen also bear tooth marks, indicating that theropod dinosaurs scavenged the carcass after the animal's death. This provides a direct taphonomic record of predator-scavenger interaction in the Late Cretaceous of Brazil.

South America was a major centre of titanosaur diversity during the Cretaceous, and Brazil in particular has produced numerous important titanosaur genera from Bauru Group deposits. Maxakalisaurus joins a diverse cast of Brazilian titanosaurs including Trigonosaurus, Baurutitan, and Gondwanatitan, collectively documenting a rich and varied sauropod fauna in Late Cretaceous South America.

## Fossil record

Maxakalisaurus topai was described in 2006 by Alexander Kellner, Diogenes de Almeida Campos, and colleagues based on a partial skeleton from Minas Gerais. The genus name honours the Maxakali indigenous people native to the region of its discovery, while the species name topai refers to Topa, a god in Maxakali cosmology. The specimen is housed at the Museu Nacional in Rio de Janeiro.

Disarticulated partial skeleton plus referred dentary and teeth.

- The type includes vertebrae, limb and girdle bones, skull fragments and osteoderms.
- A referred dentary and teeth expanded knowledge of its feeding anatomy.
- Precise placement within derived Titanosauria varies among analyses.

## Where it lived

The partial skeleton of Maxakalisaurus topai was recovered from Late Cretaceous continental redbeds in the Triângulo Mineiro region of Minas Gerais.

The Adamantina Formation is not uniformly dated to a single 80 Ma point; a broad Late Cretaceous interval is more defensible.

## Open questions

- The formation age and association of the referred jaw remain imperfectly constrained.
- The original National Museum material was affected by the 2018 fire and its present condition requires collection-level confirmation.
