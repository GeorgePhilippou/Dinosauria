## Overview

Masiakasaurus was a small predatory ceratosaurian theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian), approximately 72–66 million years ago, on the island of Madagascar. It reached about 1.8–2.1 metres in length and weighed around 20 kilograms. Most unusually, its front teeth projected forward rather than pointing straight down, a highly distinctive dentition unlike any other known theropod. The type species is Masiakasaurus knopfleri.

Named in 2001 by Scott Sampson, Matthew Carrano, and Catherine Forster, Masiakasaurus knopfleri honours musician Mark Knopfler of Dire Straits, whose music the field crew played during excavations. Masiakasaurus belongs to Noasauridae, a family of small ceratosaurian theropods found mainly in South America, its presence on Madagascar providing evidence for faunal connections between Gondwanan landmasses during the Late Cretaceous. Its procumbent front teeth suggest a specialised diet, possibly including fish, small vertebrates, or invertebrates.

## Anatomy & life

The most distinctive anatomical feature of Masiakasaurus is its anteriorly projecting (procumbent) front teeth, which point forward from the jaw rather than downward as in most theropods. This configuration is unique among known theropod dinosaurs and suggests a highly specialised feeding strategy. Proposed dietary preferences include fish, small vertebrates, and invertebrates, though the exact prey items cannot be established with certainty from fossil evidence alone.

Masiakasaurus belongs to Noasauridae, a family otherwise known mainly from South America (including genera such as Noasaurus and Velocisaurus from Argentina). Its presence in Madagascar provides evidence that noasaurids dispersed across or between Gondwanan landmasses, possibly when Madagascar and the Indian subcontinent were still in closer proximity to South America earlier in the Cretaceous. This biogeographic pattern is consistent with other Malagasy Late Cretaceous dinosaurs that show South American affinities.

Masiakasaurus co-existed in the Maevarano Formation with the large abelisaurid theropod Majungasaurus, the titanosaurian sauropod Rapetosaurus, and the unusual dromaeosaurid Rahonavis, among others. This Late Cretaceous Malagasy fauna is one of the best-documented from the southern hemisphere for this time period and provides important insights into the final stages of Gondwanan ecosystem evolution before the end-Cretaceous extinction.

## Fossil record

Masiakasaurus knopfleri was described in 2001 by Scott Sampson, Matthew Carrano, and Catherine Forster from specimens collected in the Maevarano Formation of Madagascar during field expeditions in the late 1990s. In Malagasy, masiaka means 'vicious', so the genus name translates as 'vicious lizard'. The species name knopfleri honours Mark Knopfler, the guitarist and vocalist of Dire Straits, whose music the expedition team played during fieldwork — a charming example of scientific nomenclature reflecting cultural appreciation.

Many disarticulated cranial and postcranial elements from multiple individuals; collectively much of the skeleton.

## Where it lived

Multiple specimens of Masiakasaurus knopfleri, comprising much of the skeleton, were recovered from Late Cretaceous terrestrial deposits at the Berivotra Study Area.
