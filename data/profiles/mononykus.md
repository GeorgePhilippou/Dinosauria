## Overview

Mononykus (meaning 'single claw') was a small alvarezsaurid theropod that lived during the Late Cretaceous, approximately 83–66 million years ago, in what is now Mongolia. It is known from partial skeletal material recovered from the Nemegt and Djadokhta Formations of the Omnogovi region. Estimated at roughly 1 metre in length, it was a lightly built, ground-dwelling carnivore that walked on two legs.

Named by Perle, Norell, Chiappe and Clark in 1993, Mononykus is among the best-studied alvarezsaurids and has been central to debates about the relationship between birds and non-avian theropods. Its most distinctive feature is the extreme reduction of the forelimb to a single robust functional claw, thought to have been used for digging into insect nests. Its placement within Parvicursorinae makes it a key taxon for understanding the evolution of this unusual clade.

## Anatomy & life

The most striking feature of Mononykus is its forelimb: the radius and ulna are fused and very short, while digits II and III are vestigial, leaving only digit I as a powerful, stout claw. The hindlimbs are long and slender, consistent with a fast-running lifestyle. The skull was small and lightly built with relatively large eye sockets.

Palaeontological consensus regards Mononykus as a carnivore, most plausibly specialised for breaking open termite mounds or similar colonial insect nests — a diet analogous to modern anteaters. PBDB ecospace data categorises it as ground-dwelling and solitary. It inhabited the arid-to-semi-arid environments of Late Cretaceous central Asia.

## Fossil record

Mononykus was described in 1993 by Perle, Norell, Chiappe and Clark on the basis of partial skeletons from Mongolia. The genus name means 'single claw' in Greek, referring to the animal's dramatically reduced forelimb ending in one large functional digit. Earlier material had been referred to 'Mononychus' but that name was preoccupied, leading to the amended spelling.

One incomplete postcranial skeleton with diagnostic forelimbs and hindlimb material.

## Classification

When first described, Mononykus was controversially proposed as a flightless bird more derived than Archaeopteryx, sparking intense debate about the bird–dinosaur boundary. Subsequent cladistic analyses placed it firmly within Alvarezsauridae, a clade of maniraptorans, and it is no longer considered a flightless bird. Nevertheless, the controversy spurred important work clarifying the characters that diagnose Avialae.

## Open questions

- No skull is known for the holotype.
- Forelimb function and diet are not directly demonstrated.
