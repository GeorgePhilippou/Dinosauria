## Overview

Vulcanodon ('volcano tooth') was an early sauropod dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of what is now Zimbabwe, living approximately 205–202 million years ago. Reaching about 6.5 metres in length, it was a quadrupedal herbivore and represents one of the most primitive sauropods known, occupying a key position near the base of the sauropod family tree. The type and only species, Vulcanodon karibaensis, was discovered in 1969 in the uppermost Forest Sandstone Formation near Lake Kariba.

Named by Michael Raath in 1972, Vulcanodon was for many years considered the earliest known true sauropod and remains one of the most basal members of the group. Its discovery helped illuminate the transition from the more slender sauropodomorph body plan to the massive, column-limbed sauropod design. It gives its name to the family Vulcanodontidae, though this grouping is now considered by many researchers to be a grade of basal sauropods rather than a natural clade.

## Anatomy & life

Vulcanodon retains several features considered primitive for sauropods, including proportionally shorter neck vertebrae and hindlimbs that, while clearly adapted for quadrupedal stance with columnar limbs, are not as massively built as those of later eusauropods. Its feet retain claws rather than the hoof-like structures of more derived sauropods, and the overall proportions suggest a transitional body form between the facultatively bipedal prosauropods and the obligately quadrupedal eusauropods.

Vulcanodon is one of the earliest sauropods known from Africa, a continent that later in the Jurassic became home to giant sauropods such as Brachiosaurus brancai (now Giraffatitan) and Janenschia from the Tendaguru Formation of Tanzania. Its presence in Early Jurassic Zimbabwe indicates that sauropods had diversified and spread across at least parts of Gondwana shortly after the Triassic–Jurassic boundary.

## Fossil record

Vulcanodon karibaensis was described in 1972 by Michael Raath from an incomplete skeleton discovered in 1969 on a small island in Lake Kariba, Zimbabwe. The genus name references the volcanic rocks interbedded with the fossil-bearing sediments; the teeth originally associated with the specimen were later found to belong to a carnivorous dinosaur, but the sauropod nature of the skeleton was confirmed by the postcranial anatomy.

One partial postcranial skeleton with limbs, pelvis and tail; skull absent.

## Where it lived

The Early Jurassic aeolian and fluvial deposits near Lake Kariba where Vulcanodon karibaensis was found.

## Classification

Vulcanodon is the type genus of Vulcanodontidae, a family that has been used to group several basal sauropods, including Barapasaurus and Kotasaurus. However, many phylogenetic analyses do not recover Vulcanodontidae as a natural (monophyletic) group, instead treating these taxa as successive outgroups to Eusauropoda. Vulcanodon's phylogenetic position consistently places it among the most basal members of Sauropoda.

## Open questions

- Formation age and exact position among early sauropods remain uncertain.
