## Overview

Unenlagia ('half bird' in the Mapuche language) was a medium-sized dromaeosaurid or paravian theropod from the Late Cretaceous of Argentina, living approximately 94–86 million years ago during the Coniacian–Santonian interval. Reaching about 2.4 metres in length, it was known from postcranial remains collected in Neuquén Province, Patagonia. Its shoulder joint was oriented in a way that would have allowed a wide arc of wing-like arm movement, reminiscent of the flight stroke in birds.

Named in 1997 by Fernando Novas and Pablo Puerta, Unenlagia comahuensis became an important piece in the puzzle of bird origins. The anatomy of its shoulder was initially interpreted as indicating that it might have been flightless and derived from flying ancestors, or alternatively that wing-like arm motions evolved in the theropod lineage before true flight. Unenlagia is placed within Dromaeosauridae or as a basal paravian close to Gondwanan deinonychosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

The glenoid (shoulder socket) of Unenlagia is oriented laterally rather than ventrally as in most non-avian theropods, allowing the forelimb to move in a near-vertical arc analogous to the flight stroke of modern birds. Novas and Puerta originally suggested this indicated derivation from flying ancestors. Subsequent debate has centred on whether this configuration represents a flying ancestor, a preadaptation for flight, or a locomotor specialisation for prey capture in a ground-dwelling predator.

Unenlagia is the founding member of the subfamily Unenlagiinae, a Gondwanan dromaeosaurid group distinct from the Laurasian lineages that include Velociraptor and Deinonychus. Other unenlagiines include Buitreraptor from the same formation and Neuquenraptor. Gondwanan dromaeosaurids appear to have a distinct evolutionary history from their northern counterparts, with some analyses suggesting that unenlagiines are more closely related to birds than to Velociraptor-like forms.

Because Unenlagia is known only from postcranial remains without skull material, many aspects of its biology — including diet, sensory capabilities, and precise phylogenetic position — remain uncertain. A second species, Unenlagia paynemili, was named in 2002, but some researchers consider it a separate genus (Neuquenraptor). Further well-preserved material from Patagonia would substantially advance understanding of this important taxon.

## Fossil record

Unenlagia comahuensis was described in 1997 by Fernando Novas and Pablo Puerta from postcranial remains — including the ilium, ischium, femur, tibia, fibula, and elements of the shoulder girdle — recovered from the Portezuelo Formation of Neuquén, Argentina. The species name comahuensis refers to Comahue, the indigenous name for the Neuquén–Río Negro region.

Two incomplete postcranial type specimens with limited additional referrals.
