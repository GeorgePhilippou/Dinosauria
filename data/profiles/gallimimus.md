## Overview

Gallimimus bullatus was the largest ornithomimosaur known at the time of its description — up to six metres long — from the Late Cretaceous Nemegt Formation of Mongolia. Its long arms, toothless beak, three-toed feet and overall proportions are remarkably similar to a large flightless bird, and it is one of the first dinosaurs demonstrated by trackways to have run at speeds exceeding 40 km/h.

Originally interpreted as a filter-feeder (based on presumed comb-like structures in the beak), Gallimimus is now thought to have been an omnivore, taking insects, small vertebrates and plant material in the manner of a modern cassowary. The Jurassic Park herd stampede scene used Gallimimus as the first large-scale computer-generated dinosaur crowd.

## Anatomy & life

Gallimimus was a tall, lightly built theropod from the Nemegt Formation of southern Mongolia. Adults reached about six metres in length, making it the largest ornithomimid known from good material. It had a small head on a long neck, a toothless beak, a compact body and very long hindlimbs with a short thigh and elongated shin and foot.

The skull is low and long-snouted, with large eyes and no teeth at all; the jaws were sheathed in a horny beak. The arms were relatively short compared with other ornithomimids, ending in three-fingered hands whose claws could not be strongly grasped. The tail was long and stiffened towards the tip.

Because its skeleton is known from several individuals at different growth stages, Gallimimus is the reference animal for how ornithomimids were built. Its diet is another matter: the beak has been compared with those of plant-eaters, small-prey hunters and even filter-feeding birds, and the fossils do not yet decide between these ideas.

- A toothless, beaked skull with large eye sockets on a long, slender neck.
- Extremely long hindlimbs with elongated shins and feet, the classic build of a fast bipedal runner.
- A growth series from small juveniles to adults, unusual for a Cretaceous theropod.

**Feeding: Unresolved.** A keratinous beak with fine ridges is preserved in one specimen, but whether it cropped plants, caught small animals or filtered food from water remains hypothesis.

**Movement: Bipedal runner.** Long shins and feet relative to the thigh are the hallmark of cursorial animals. Actual top speed cannot be read from bones.

**Appearance: Skeleton well known.** No feathers are preserved with Gallimimus itself. A feathered body with bare legs is inferred from Ornithomimus specimens from Canada.

## Fossil record

Gallimimus bullatus was named in 1972 from a nearly complete adult skeleton collected by the Polish–Mongolian expeditions. Further skeletons of various sizes from the Nemegt Formation make it one of the best-documented ornithomimosaurs.

Complete and near-complete skulls, full vertebral series, pectoral and pelvic girdles, forelimbs with hands and the long hindlimbs and feet, in adults and juveniles. One specimen preserves the keratinous beak.

No feathers, skin or gut contents are known for Gallimimus itself, and some referred skeletons have not been individually re-described since the 1970s.

Long limbs, small head and toothless jaws support ostrich-like locomotion and omnivorous/generalist feeding interpretations.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous Mongolian floodplain deposits.

## Classification

Gallimimus is an ornithomimid, one of the ostrich-like coelurosaurs. It is usually recovered close to Ornithomimus and Struthiomimus within Ornithomimidae, with the giant Deinocheirus as a more distant relative in Ornithomimosauria.

## Open questions

### What did it eat?

The toothless beak is compatible with several diets. Ridged structures inside one specimen's beak were compared with the filtering lamellae of ducks, but the comparison is contested and gut contents are unknown.

### How fast could it run?

Limb proportions suggest a fast runner, and it is often described as ostrich-like in speed, but no published figure is a direct measurement.

### Do all the referred skeletons belong to one species?

Most Nemegt ornithomimid material has been referred to Gallimimus bullatus. Some specimens differ in details and would benefit from individual reassessment.
