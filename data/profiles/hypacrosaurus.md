## Overview

Hypacrosaurus altispinus was a large lambeosaurine hadrosaur from the late Campanian and early Maastrichtian of Alberta and Montana (~75–67 Ma), bearing a tall hollow crest similar to Corythosaurus. Remarkable nest sites from the Two Medicine Formation preserve eggs, embryos, hatchlings and juveniles at different growth stages — providing detailed data on hadrosaur developmental biology.

Bone histology from Hypacrosaurus growth series shows that it grew from a 50-cm hatchling to a 9-metre adult in as little as 10–12 years — among the fastest growth rates documented for any dinosaur — before slowing sharply at maturity. Proteins extracted from Hypacrosaurus eggshell fragments have been proposed as among the oldest biological molecules preserved in fossils, though this remains debated.

## Anatomy & life

Nesting sites in the Two Medicine Formation preserve complete clutches of Hypacrosaurus eggs (spherical, about 20 cm diameter) and hatchlings with incompletely formed skulls and limb bones. The developmental sequence documents how the hollow crest formed progressively through ontogeny — small in juveniles, full-sized in adults — paralleling the pattern in Corythosaurus.

Bone histology shows one of the fastest known growth rates among hadrosaurs: reaching adult size in 10–12 years. The growth rings (lines of arrested growth) record annual growth cycles, showing rapid juvenile growth that decelerated sharply at maturity. This pattern mirrors fast-growing large mammals and is inconsistent with ectothermic (cold-blooded) physiology.

A 2020 paper reported osteopontin (a bone matrix protein) extracted from 75-million-year-old Hypacrosaurus eggshell — potentially the oldest biological molecule identified from a fossil. The interpretation is controversial: contamination by microbial biofilms or other modern sources cannot be fully excluded at this age, and independent verification has not yet confirmed the result.

## Fossil record

Several skulls and partial-to-substantial skeletons of H. altispinus.

Embryonic skulls inside eggs preserve the earliest crest development stages; skin impressions are also known.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous floodplain environments of Alberta, Canada.

## Open questions

- Genus monophyly and distinctions between H. altispinus and H. stebingeri continue to be tested.
- Crest acoustics and display are biomechanical inferences.
