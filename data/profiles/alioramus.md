## Overview

Alioramus remotus and the later-described A. altai were small tyrannosaurs from the Late Cretaceous Nemegt Formation of Mongolia, notable for their elongated, low skulls bearing a row of small hornlets along the nasal bones — unlike the deep skull of Tarbosaurus, which shared the same ecosystem. This has led to debate about whether Alioramus represents a distinct lineage or a juvenile Tarbosaurus.

Phylogenetic analyses consistently place Alioramus within Tyrannosauridae, closer to Asian tyrannosaurs than to Tyrannosaurus. The gracile skull and relatively slender build compared to Tarbosaurus suggest that if these two were adults of the same species, we would expect more intermediate forms — the balance of evidence favours Alioramus as a genuine distinct taxon.

## Anatomy & life

The nasal hornlets distinguish Alioramus from all other known tyrannosaurids. The skull is proportionally low and long compared to the deep robust skull of Tarbosaurus. The teeth are slightly more numerous than in Tarbosaurus for the jaw length. These differences are consistent with dietary niche partitioning within the Nemegt ecosystem.

Two tyrannosaurs in the same formation — Tarbosaurus (large, robust) and Alioramus (smaller, gracile) — likely partitioned prey resources differently, analogous to how lions and cheetahs exploit different prey sizes in modern African ecosystems. Alioramus probably took smaller prey than the apex predator Tarbosaurus.

## Fossil record

Alioramus remotus was described by Kurzanov in 1976 from a skull and foot material. A more complete species, A. altai, was described in 2009 by Novas and colleagues from a nearly complete juvenile-like skeleton. The juvenility hypothesis — that Alioramus is a young Tarbosaurus — is rejected on the basis of bone texture indicating skeletal maturity and consistent morphological differences from Tarbosaurus at comparable sizes.

One fragmentary type and one substantially complete skull with partial skeleton, plus limited referred remains.

Long, low skull and many small teeth distinguish it from deeper-skulled tyrannosaurids.

## Where it lived

Nemegt floodplain deposits preserve large tyrannosaurids, ornithomimosaurs, oviraptorosaurs and hadrosaurs.

## Open questions

- Species-level differences may include ontogenetic effects.
- Adult body size is not securely known.
