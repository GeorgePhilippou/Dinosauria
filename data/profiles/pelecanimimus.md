## Overview

Pelecanimimus (meaning 'pelican mimic') was an early ornithomimosaurian theropod from the Early Cretaceous (Barremian, approximately 126–121 million years ago) of Cuenca Province, Castilla-La Mancha, Spain. The holotype comes from the La Huérguina Formation. At roughly 2.5 metres in length it was a small, lightly built, bipedal dinosaur with an omnivorous or carnivorous diet. Its most extraordinary feature is the possession of over 200 small teeth — far more than in most theropods, and unique among ornithomimosaurs.

Described by Pérez-Moreno and colleagues in 1994, Pelecanimimus polyodon is the earliest and most primitive ornithomimosaur known from Europe and one of the only toothed members of the group. The exceptionally preserved holotype also shows evidence of a soft-tissue throat pouch analogous to that of modern pelicans, and impressions of a possible crest. These soft-tissue details make it one of the most informative Early Cretaceous theropod specimens from Europe.

## Anatomy & life

All other ornithomimosaurs are either toothless or have very reduced dentitions, making Pelecanimimus anomalous with its more than 200 tiny, closely packed teeth. This dense dental array — the highest tooth count of any known theropod — has been interpreted as a filter-feeding or fish-straining adaptation. The teeth are extremely small and fine, suggesting a diet of soft prey items rather than large vertebrates.

The holotype preserves traces of soft tissue including what appears to be a keratinous or fleshy beak-like covering over the jaws, a throat pouch similar to that of pelicans, and possible evidence of a small soft-tissue crest. Soft-tissue preservation in Cretaceous theropods is rare and the Las Hoyas locality is one of very few European sites where such detail is preserved.

As the earliest known European ornithomimosaur, Pelecanimimus documents the presence of this lineage in Europe during the Early Cretaceous, at a time when Europe was an archipelago of islands. Whether it represents an immigration from Asia or an independent European radiation of early ornithomimosaurs is not yet resolved, but its primitive toothed condition is consistent with an early divergence from the ornithomimosaur stem.

## Fossil record

Pelecanimimus polyodon was described in 1994 by Pérez-Moreno and colleagues from the Las Hoyas locality of the La Huérguina Formation, Cuenca, Spain. The genus name means 'pelican mimic', referring to the throat pouch evident in the holotype. The species name polyodon means 'many-toothed', alluding to its exceptional dental count of over 200 teeth.

Single articulated anterior skeleton with skull, forelimbs and soft-tissue traces; hindquarters largely absent.

## Where it lived

Late Barremian lacustrine Konservat-Lagerstätte deposits near Las Hoyas, Cuenca, yielding the exquisitely preserved holotype of Pelecanimimus including soft-tissue impressions.

## Open questions

- Only one incomplete individual is known.
- Diet and the function of throat soft tissues remain uncertain.
