## Overview

Janenschia (named after German palaeontologist Werner Janensch) was a large herbivorous sauropod dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of Tanzania, living approximately 149–143 million years ago. Known from the Tendaguru Formation of the Lindi Region, it reached an estimated 20 metres in length. It was a member of the rich Tendaguru dinosaur fauna — one of the most productive Late Jurassic dinosaur assemblages outside North America — and was named by Rupert Wild in 1991.

Janenschia is placed within Titanosauria (or at least Titanosauriformes) by some analyses, which would make it one of the earliest members of this lineage known, though its exact phylogenetic position has been debated. The Tendaguru Formation has yielded a remarkable array of sauropods, including Giraffatitan (Brachiosaurus brancai), Dicraeosaurus, and Tendaguria, and Janenschia represents a further element of this diverse community. Understanding Janenschia's relationships helps trace the evolutionary history of large sauropods between Africa and other Gondwanan landmasses.

## Anatomy & life

Janenschia is known from postcranial material including limb bones and vertebrae, reaching an estimated 20 metres in length. As a titanosauriform, it was a large, robust sauropod with columnar limbs. No skull is known, so reconstructions of the head are based on related taxa. Its large size is consistent with other sauropods from the diverse Tendaguru assemblage.

If its titanosaurian placement is confirmed, Janenschia would be one of the earliest-known titanosaurs, with significant implications for understanding when and where this successful clade originated. Its African provenance from the Late Jurassic contributes to biogeographic models of sauropod dispersal and the reconstruction of Late Jurassic Gondwanan dinosaur faunas.

## Fossil record

Material now assigned to Janenschia was collected during the German Tendaguru expeditions of 1909–1913, one of the most significant palaeontological ventures in Africa. The genus was formally erected by Rupert Wild in 1991, honouring Werner Janensch who led and described much of the Tendaguru material. Earlier, the fossils had been assigned to the genus Torneria.

Restricted associated partial hindlimb and caudal material, with disputed referrals.

## Where it lived

The Tendaguru Formation records a coastal floodplain and estuary environment in what is now coastal Tanzania during the Tithonian stage of the Late Jurassic. The fauna is remarkably similar in some respects to that of the North American Morrison Formation, suggesting faunal exchange between Africa and Laurasia during the Late Jurassic, possibly via temporary land connections.

The world-famous Tendaguru site has produced extensive Late Jurassic dinosaur material including Janenschia, collected during German expeditions in the early twentieth century.

## Open questions

- Historic referrals have inflated the known skeleton.
- A modern specimen-level revision is still needed to resolve sauropod relationships.
