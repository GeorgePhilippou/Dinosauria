## Overview

Rugops (meaning 'wrinkle face') was a medium-sized abelisaurid theropod from the Late Cretaceous of Niger, living approximately 100–94 million years ago (Cenomanian). Named by Sereno, Wilson, and Conrad in 2004, it is estimated at around 7 metres in length and is known from an almost complete skull recovered from the Echkar/Tegama Formation of the Agadez region. The skull's textured, wrinkled surface—reflecting rows of small vascular pits—is the source of its name.

Rugops is a member of Abelisauridae within Ceratosauria and is the most completely cranially known abelisaurid from Cenomanian Africa. Scientifically, it is important for documenting Gondwanan abelisaurid distribution during a time when Africa was becoming more isolated. The delicate skull construction and vascular pits have been interpreted as evidence of scavenging behaviour and the possible presence of a facial crest or display structure, though these interpretations are debated.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Rugops is perforated by rows of small foramina (pits) that in life likely accommodated blood vessels supplying overlying skin or a keratinous covering. Sereno and colleagues suggested this could indicate a facial crest or scales. The relatively light and narrow construction of the skull, in contrast to more robustly built abelisaurids, led to speculation that Rugops may have been more of a scavenger, though this remains debated.

Abelisauridae was the dominant large theropod family in Gondwana during the Cretaceous, with members known from South America, India, Madagascar, and Africa. Rugops helps document African abelisaurid presence during the Cenomanian, a time when Africa's faunal connections to other Gondwanan landmasses were being severed. Discovering it in Niger alongside Spinosaurus and Carcharodontosaurus reveals a remarkably predator-rich ecosystem.

The discovery of Rugops in Africa, alongside contemporaneous abelisaurids from South America (e.g., Ekrixinatosaurus) and Madagascar (e.g., Majungasaurus), provides evidence that abelisaurids dispersed widely across Gondwana before its final fragmentation. The Cenomanian age of Rugops places it at a critical juncture in Gondwanan biogeographic history.

## Fossil record

Rugops primus was described by Paul Sereno, Jeffrey Wilson, and Jack Conrad in 2004 from a skull excavated in Niger by Sereno's expeditions to the Sahara. The name means 'first wrinkle face', reflecting both the textured skull surface and its stratigraphic age as one of the earliest known abelisaurids from equatorial Africa.

Single incomplete skull with very limited additional material.

## Where it lived

Cenomanian continental deposits of the Sahara yielding the holotype Rugops skull alongside diverse large theropods and sauropods.

## Open questions

- Only one partial skull is securely known.
- Its exact abelisaurid position and adult size remain uncertain.
