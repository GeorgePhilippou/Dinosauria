## Overview

Oryctodromeus (meaning 'digging runner') was a small ornithischian dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous (Albian-Cenomanian, approximately 99–94 million years ago) of Montana and Idaho, USA. It is known from material including a skull, vertebrae, ribs, forelimb and hindlimb elements from the Blackleaf Formation. At around 2.1 metres in length it was a slender, bipedal herbivore. It is placed within Orodrominae in Thescelosauridae.

Named by Varricchio, Martin and Katsura in 2007, Oryctodromeus cubicularis is remarkable for being the first non-avian dinosaur for which direct evidence of burrowing behaviour was documented. The holotype adult and two juveniles were found within what is interpreted as a fossilised burrow, providing compelling evidence that this dinosaur dug and used underground dens — possibly for nesting, shelter from predators, or thermoregulation.

## Anatomy & life

The holotype specimens were found within a structure interpreted as a fossilised burrow approximately 30 cm wide and 70 cm long — dimensions consistent with the body size of Oryctodromeus. Anatomical features such as the robust snout, widened pelvis, and proportionally shorter forelimbs are consistent with digging adaptations seen in extant burrowing vertebrates. This represents the first direct skeletal and behavioural evidence of burrowing in any non-avian dinosaur.

The co-occurrence of one adult and two juveniles in the same burrow suggests possible parental care or family-group behaviour in Oryctodromeus. Whether the adult was guarding, brooding or had simply died alongside the juveniles is uncertain, but the association is consistent with extended parental-offspring contact seen in some modern reptiles and birds.

Burrowing would have afforded Oryctodromeus significant advantages: shelter from large theropod predators, protection from temperature extremes, and a safe nesting environment. The discovery expanded the known behavioural repertoire of ornithopod dinosaurs considerably. It has prompted re-examination of other small ornithopod taxa for skeletal correlates of fossorial behaviour.

## Fossil record

Oryctodromeus cubicularis was described by David Varricchio, Anthony Martin and Yoshihiro Katsura in 2007. The holotype was recovered from the Blackleaf Formation of southwestern Montana and consisted of a partial adult skeleton found alongside two smaller juvenile individuals. The genus name means 'digging runner' and the species name cubicularis ('of the bedchamber') refers to the burrow in which the animals were found.

Partial adult and juvenile skeletons associated inside a fossil burrow; further referred material.

- The original burrow contained an adult-sized individual and two juveniles.
- Skeletal features are consistent with digging, but it was not as specialised as modern obligate burrowers.
- Herbivory or omnivory is inferred from thescelosaurid anatomy; direct diet evidence is absent.

## Where it lived

Late Albian fluvial deposits of Montana where the holotype adult and two juvenile Oryctodromeus specimens were found together in a fossilised burrow structure.

The relevant formations are Cenomanian; the type locality is not simply a 99-94 Ma point.

## Open questions

- Whether the preserved group represents parental care is uncertain.
- The extent of habitual burrowing across the species is unknown.
