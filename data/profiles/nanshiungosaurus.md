## Overview

Nanshiungosaurus (meaning 'Nanxiong lizard') was a therizinosaur — a member of the distinctive sickle-clawed, herbivorous theropod clade — that lived during the Late Cretaceous (approximately 84–66 million years ago) in what is now China. Specimens attributed to the genus come from the Zhonggou/Xinminbao group in Gansu Province and the Yuanpu/Nanxiong Formation in Guangdong Province. Estimated length is up to about 5.3 metres. Only vertebral column and pelvic material have been recovered, so skull reconstructions are necessarily speculative.

Named by Dong Zhiming in 1979, Nanshiungosaurus is one of the earliest-named therizinosaurs from China and has been significant for establishing the Asian diversity of this clade. Its classification within Therizinosauridae places it among the most specialised theropod herbivores. The absence of skull material makes precise dietary inferences uncertain, though the broader family is well established as herbivorous based on better-known relatives.

## Anatomy & life

Therizinosaurs as a group are notable for being theropods that secondarily adopted herbivory, developing broad hips, reduced or toothless jaws in some taxa, and prominent claws likely used for pulling down vegetation. Nanshiungosaurus, based on its vertebral morphology, fits this ecological pattern, though specific feeding adaptations cannot be confirmed without skull material.

## Fossil record

Nanshiungosaurus brevispinus was described by Chinese palaeontologist Dong Zhiming in 1979. The genus name honours the Nanxiong Basin of Guangdong where initial material was found. The species name brevispinus refers to the relatively short neural spines on the dorsal vertebrae. A second species, N. bohlini, was named in 1997 but its referral to this genus remains debated.

Known remains consist primarily of dorsal, sacral and caudal vertebrae and pelvic elements. No skull material has been confidently assigned to Nanshiungosaurus, which limits comparison with other therizinosaurs. Vertebral anatomy, including opisthocoelous anterior dorsals, is consistent with therizinosaur affinities.

Partial vertebral column and pelvis without a skull.

- The type preserves vertebrae and pelvis but little basis for a precise whole-body reconstruction.
- Nanshiungosaurus bohlini from Gansu is poorly comparable and should not automatically extend the type genus.
- Plant-eating or omnivorous habits are inferred from therizinosaur anatomy; no direct gut contents are known.

## Where it lived

The occurrence of therizinosaurs across both Central Asian and Chinese Late Cretaceous deposits, represented by forms such as Nanshiungosaurus, Therizinosaurus, and Erlikosaurus, documents a geographically widespread radiation. Nanshiungosaurus from southern China represents a southern extent of this distribution and hints at faunal interchange across the continent.

## Open questions

- The type is incomplete and has not received a comprehensive modern redescription.
- The second named species may not belong to Nanshiungosaurus.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
