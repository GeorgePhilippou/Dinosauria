## Overview

Argentinosaurus huinculensis is one of the candidates for the largest land animal that has ever existed, from the Cenomanian of Patagonia (~96 Ma). Known from fragmentary remains — several dorsal vertebrae, a tibia and rib fragments — size estimates based on scaling from these elements suggest a body length of 30–40 metres and a mass of 70–80 tonnes or more, though the uncertainty ranges are very wide given the incompleteness of the material.

The fragmentary nature of Argentinosaurus makes precise size determination difficult, and it has been challenged for the "largest dinosaur" title by Patagotitan mayorum (described 2017, known from much more complete material) and possibly Notocolossus. What is clear is that Argentinosaurus represents the upper end of titanosaur gigantism, living in the same ecosystem as the giant theropod Giganotosaurus.

## Anatomy & life

Argentinosaurus was a titanosaur, one of the long-necked, four-legged sauropods that dominated the Cretaceous of South America. Its dorsal vertebrae are among the largest ever found, well over a metre across, and the leg bone recovered with them is about 1.55 metres long. These bones make it a genuine contender for the heaviest land animal known.

Everything beyond those bones is reconstruction. Length estimates of about 30 to 35 metres and masses of roughly 65 to 75 tonnes come from scaling the vertebrae and limb bone against more complete titanosaurs such as Patagotitan and Dreadnoughtus. Different methods give different answers, and the figures should be read as a range rather than a measurement.

As a sauropod, Argentinosaurus was a plant-eater that moved on all four limbs. A 2013 computer simulation by Sellers and colleagues, built on a museum skeletal reconstruction, suggested a slow walking gait of around two metres per second, but this rests on a largely hypothetical skeleton.

- Dorsal vertebrae more than a metre wide place it among the very largest sauropods.
- Its body length and mass are extrapolations from a handful of bones, not measurements of a skeleton.
- It lived in the Huincul Formation alongside the giant predator Mapusaurus.

**Feeding: Plant-eater.** No skull or teeth are known. Herbivory is secure for sauropods as a group, but the feeding height and preferred plants of Argentinosaurus are unknown.

**Movement: Quadrupedal.** The only limb element is a single lower-leg bone. Locomotor simulations use a reconstructed skeleton borrowed from other titanosaurs.

**Appearance: Largely reconstructed.** The generalised titanosaur outline is borrowed from relatives; skin, colour and the shape of the head are not preserved.

## Fossil record

Argentinosaurus rests on a single very incomplete postcranial skeleton from the Huincul Formation. No skull, neck, tail, forelimb or complete hindlimb is known, so its famous size is an extrapolation.

The holotype preserves several dorsal vertebrae, the front part of the sacrum, rib fragments and a right lower-leg bone. A giant femoral shaft from elsewhere in the formation is sometimes referred but was not found with the type.

The lower-leg bone was described as a tibia but is generally reinterpreted as a fibula; this matters because it changes how the leg, and therefore the whole animal, is scaled.

Gigantic vertebrae and limb bones are the basis for its extreme size estimates.

## Where it lived

Cretaceous Patagonian terrestrial ecosystems with large titanosaurs and theropods.

## Classification

Argentinosaurus is a titanosaurian sauropod, usually recovered among the giant lognkosaurs within Colossosauria alongside Patagotitan and Futalognkosaurus. Its position shifts between analyses because so few characters can be scored from the fragmentary holotype.

## Open questions

### Was it the largest dinosaur?

Possibly, but the claim cannot be settled. Its bones are among the largest known, yet estimates for Argentinosaurus, Patagotitan and other giants overlap within their error margins.

### Does the giant femur belong to it?

A huge femoral shaft from the region is often quoted with Argentinosaurus, but it was not found with the holotype and its assignment is not secure.

### What did the rest of the animal look like?

No skull, neck, tail or forelimb is known. Every full-body reconstruction fills those gaps with other titanosaurs.
