## Overview

Secernosaurus (meaning 'separated lizard') was a hadrosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Patagonia, Argentina, living approximately 72–66 million years ago (Maastrichtian). Named by Michael Brett-Surman in 1979, it is estimated at around 3 metres in length and is known from fragmentary postcranial remains from the Lago Colhué Huapi / Chubut Formation of Chubut Province. Secernosaurus holds the distinction of being the first hadrosaurid dinosaur discovered in South America.

Secernosaurus belongs to Hadrosauridae and is placed within Kritosaurini (Saurolophinae), suggesting affinities with North American hadrosaurs such as Kritosaurus. Its South American occurrence in the Maastrichtian has been interpreted as evidence of a faunal interchange between North and South America near the end of the Cretaceous, facilitated by emerging land connections across the Central American seaway. The fragmentary nature of the material means taxonomic details remain uncertain.

## Anatomy & life

The occurrence of a hadrosaurid in Maastrichtian South America is biogeographically remarkable, as hadrosaurs were previously thought to be exclusively Northern Hemisphere dinosaurs. Secernosaurus is interpreted as evidence for a faunal corridor opening between North and South America towards the end of the Cretaceous, allowing hadrosaurs to disperse southward into Gondwana.

Brett-Surman originally assigned Secernosaurus to Hadrosauridae without definitive tribal placement. Later phylogenetic analyses placed it within Kritosaurini (Saurolophinae) alongside North American hadrosaurs such as Kritosaurus and Gryposaurus. This affinity supports a North American origin for Secernosaurus's ancestors, consistent with the biogeographic interpretation of southward dispersal.

Patagonia during the Maastrichtian hosted a rich dinosaur fauna including the titanosaur Saltasaurus, the abelisaurid Carnotaurus, and various small theropods. The addition of Secernosaurus to this community indicates that by the latest Cretaceous, North American hadrosaurs had joined the indigenous South American fauna, presaging the Great American Biotic Interchange that would intensify in the Cenozoic.

## Fossil record

Secernosaurus koerneri was described by Michael Brett-Surman in 1979 from fragmentary postcranial remains collected in Chubut Province, Patagonia. The genus name 'separated lizard' refers to its geographic isolation in South America, far removed from the rest of the hadrosaurid world which was centred in North America and Asia. It was the first hadrosaurid recorded from the Southern Hemisphere.

Fragmentary cranial and postcranial bones from more than one locality.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous Patagonian continental deposits yielding the only known Secernosaurus material.
