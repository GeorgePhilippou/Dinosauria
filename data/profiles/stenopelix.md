## Overview

Stenopelix (meaning 'narrow pelvis') was a small ornithischian dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of Germany, living approximately 127–121 million years ago. Only a single fragmentary specimen lacking a skull is known, making precise identification and classification difficult. It is estimated at roughly 1.5 metres in length. The NHM places it within Pachycephalosauria; however, some researchers have suggested it may be a basal ceratopsian, and its exact affinities remain uncertain owing to the incomplete nature of the material.

Named by Meyer in 1857 from material found in the Wealden facies of Lower Saxony, Stenopelix valdensis is one of the oldest and most enigmatic marginocephalian dinosaurs known from Europe. The scarcity of well-preserved Wealden ornithischians from Europe makes any specimen from this interval scientifically important, even when it is fragmentary. Stenopelix has been referenced in discussions of early marginocephalian distribution and the Mesozoic fauna of Europe.

## Anatomy & life

The Wealden facies of northwestern Europe preserve a distinctive Early Cretaceous fauna of dinosaurs, crocodylomorphs, turtles, and early mammals. European Wealden dinosaurs include iguanodontians such as Iguanodon, spinosaurid theropods, and the sauropod Pelorosaurus. Stenopelix is among the few ornithischians other than iguanodontids from this assemblage, making it particularly valuable for reconstructing European Early Cretaceous biodiversity.

Even though Stenopelix is known only from incomplete postcranial remains, its Early Cretaceous age and European provenance make it significant for understanding the dispersal of marginocephalians — the group that includes pachycephalosaurs and ceratopsians — beyond their better-known Asian and North American records. If it is indeed a pachycephalosaur, it extends the group's geographic range considerably.

## Fossil record

Stenopelix valdensis was named by Hermann von Meyer in 1857 from a partial skeleton discovered in the Obernkirchen Sandstone of Lower Saxony, Germany. The genus name references the unusually narrow pelvic region preserved in the specimen. The species name valdensis refers to the Wealden (from the Old English 'weald', meaning forest) geological unit in which it was found. No skull material has ever been recovered.

Largely articulated postcranial holotype; skull absent.

## Where it lived

The only known specimen of Stenopelix was recovered from Barremian-age Wealden deposits in the Obernkirchen area of northwestern Germany.

## Classification

The phylogenetic placement of Stenopelix has been debated for over 150 years. Its narrow pelvis and general body form led early workers to classify it variously as a hypsilophodontid ornithopod, a pachycephalosaur, or even a ceratopsian. Modern analyses tend to place it within Marginocephalia, most commonly near the base of Pachycephalosauria, but the lack of skull — which provides the most diagnostic features for these groups — means uncertainty persists.

## Open questions
