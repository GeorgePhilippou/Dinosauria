## Overview

Carnotaurus sastrei was a large Late Cretaceous abelisaurid from Patagonia with two thick bull-like brow horns, an extremely short deep skull, and the most vestigial forelimbs of any large non-avian theropod. Exceptional skin impressions preserve a mosaic of large boss-like scales and smaller flat scales, with no feather evidence — making it one of the best-documented theropod integument samples.

The single known specimen, discovered by Bonaparte in 1984, is near-complete with skull, much of the vertebral column and extensive skin. As an abelisaurid it represents the South American apex predator lineage that evolved in Gondwanan isolation from the tyrannosaurs of the northern continents.

## Anatomy & life

Carnotaurus sastrei was a medium-large theropod with a short, deep skull topped by a pair of thick, forward-facing horns above the eyes. The jaws were relatively slender and the teeth small for an animal of its size, which has prompted debate over how it dealt with prey.

Its forelimbs were among the most reduced of any large theropod: the upper arm was short, the forearm tiny and the four fingers barely functional. In contrast, the hindlimbs were long and the tail carried enormous muscle attachments, which biomechanical studies interpret as evidence of powerful, fast running.

Because Carnotaurus is known from a single individual, the figures quoted for its length and weight describe that one animal, and the extent of variation in the species is unknown. Skin impressions preserved along much of the body show a covering of small scales without feathers.

- Paired brow horns, directly preserved on the skull, are unique among well-known theropods; their function is still debated.
- The forelimbs are extraordinarily short, with a tiny forearm and stubby fingers that could not have grasped prey.
- Extensive skin impressions show non-overlapping scales of varying size along the flanks, tail and neck, with no evidence of feathers.

**Feeding: Carnivore.** Blade-like teeth and a deep skull identify a meat-eater; whether it hunted large or small prey is a biomechanical interpretation.

**Movement: Bipedal runner.** Enlarged caudofemoral muscle attachments suggest strong running ability; exact speeds are modelled, not measured.

**Appearance: Scaly skin preserved.** Skin impressions show small scales of differing sizes; earlier claims of rows of large feature scales were not confirmed by re-study. Colour is not preserved.

## Fossil record

Carnotaurus sastrei rests on a single specimen, the holotype MACN-CH 894, which preserves the skull, most of the vertebral column, the girdles and forelimbs, the thigh bones and extensive patches of scaly skin. Only one individual is known.

A complete skull and lower jaws, the neck, back and sacrum, most of the tail, the shoulder girdle with both forelimbs, the pelvis and both femora, together with skin impressions from the right side of the body.

The skull was somewhat crushed during fossilisation, which affects reconstructions of its exact shape. The shins, feet and tail tip are not preserved and are restored from related abelisaurids.

Short deep skull, reduced arms, horns and preserved skin impressions make its anatomy unusually clear.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous Patagonian terrestrial environments.

## Classification

Carnotaurus is an abelisaurid ceratosaur and the name-giver of the subfamily Carnotaurinae, which groups the short-faced, often horned abelisaurids of Gondwana. Its closest relatives include Aucasaurus and Abelisaurus from Argentina and Majungasaurus from Madagascar.

## Open questions

### What were the horns for?

Head-butting, shoving contests and display have all been proposed on biomechanical grounds. No direct evidence of horn use survives.

### How fast could it run?

The huge tail-based leg muscles suggest a powerful runner, but published speed estimates are models built on one skeleton.

### Was every Carnotaurus like this one?

With a single specimen, differences between sexes, ages or individuals cannot be assessed. Its exact age within the La Colonia Formation is also debated.
