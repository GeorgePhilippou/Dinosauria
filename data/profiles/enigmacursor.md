## Overview

Enigmacursor (meaning 'mysterious runner') was a small herbivorous ornithischian dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic period, approximately 152–145 million years ago, in what is now Colorado, USA. Estimated at roughly 1.8 metres in length, it had long, slender hindlimbs suited for running and inhabited the floodplains of the Morrison Formation alongside iconic contemporaries such as Diplodocus, Allosaurus, and Stegosaurus. The type specimen is unusually complete for a small Morrison ornithischian, though the skull remains unknown.

Enigmacursor mollyborthwickae was described by Maidment and Barrett in 2025, making it one of the most recently named dinosaurs in this dataset. It is placed within Neornithischia but lacks assignment to a more specific family, reflecting genuine uncertainty about its relationships to other small ornithischians. Its discovery adds to the growing understanding of Morrison Formation neornithischian diversity, a group historically overshadowed by the formation's more spectacular sauropods and theropods.

## Anatomy & life

Small ornithischians from the Morrison Formation are notoriously rare and fragmentary, making Enigmacursor's relatively complete postcranial skeleton particularly valuable. The long hindlimbs and large feet suggest active bipedal locomotion and possibly fast running, consistent with the genus name. However, interpretations of locomotor ability depend on measurements that may be revised with further preparation of the material.

The Morrison Formation was home to one of the most diverse Jurassic dinosaur assemblages known, dominated by giant sauropods and large theropods. Small herbivorous ornithischians like Enigmacursor occupied lower trophic levels, likely subsisting on ferns, cycads, and low-growing vegetation. The discovery of Enigmacursor adds a new dimension to understanding ecosystem structure within this iconic formation.

## Fossil record

Enigmacursor mollyborthwickae was formally described by Maidment and Barrett in 2025, based on a partial skeleton collected from the Morrison Formation of Colorado. The genus name reflects the difficulties in placing the animal within the ornithischian family tree, and the species name honours Molly Borthwick. Despite the specimen's relative completeness for a small Morrison ornithischian, the skull has not been recovered.

## Classification

Enigmacursor is placed in Neornithischia but cannot be assigned to a specific family with confidence, partly reflecting the poor understanding of basal neornithischian relationships generally and partly the absence of diagnostic cranial features. Future work — particularly if skull material is found — could help determine whether Enigmacursor is an early ornithopod, a hypsilophodontid-grade animal, or a member of another neornithischian lineage.

## Open questions

- Only one incomplete individual is described.
- The skull and precise position within Neornithischia remain poorly known.
