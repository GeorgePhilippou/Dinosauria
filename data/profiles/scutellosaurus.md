## Overview

Scutellosaurus (meaning 'small shield lizard') was a small, lightly built herbivorous dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of Arizona, USA, living approximately 201–184 million years ago (Hettangian–Pliensbachian). Named by Edwin Colbert in 1981, it reached about 1.2 metres in length and is known from the Kayenta Formation of Coconino County. It is notable for possessing hundreds of small bony scutes (osteoderms) along its back and flanks, earning its name.

Scutellosaurus is classified within Thyreophora—the armoured ornithischians—as one of the most basal members of that clade, and is scientifically significant because it illuminates the early evolution of body armour in dinosaurs. Unlike the later, fully quadrupedal stegosaurs and ankylosaurs, Scutellosaurus appears to have been capable of bipedal locomotion based on limb proportions. Its discovery in the Early Jurassic of North America has helped anchor the origins of Thyreophora temporally and geographically.

## Anatomy & life

Limb proportions in Scutellosaurus suggest the animal may have been primarily bipedal or capable of switching between bipedal and quadrupedal postures—unlike the later, heavily built ankylosaurs and stegosaurs that were obligate quadrupeds. This has implications for understanding the locomotory transitions within Thyreophora as body armour became heavier over evolutionary time.

The Kayenta Formation represents a fluvial and aeolian environment in what is now the American Southwest. Scutellosaurus shared this Early Jurassic landscape with theropods including Dilophosaurus and Megapnosaurus, as well as early sauropodomorphs and a diverse small tetrapod fauna. The moderate diversity of this fauna provides a snapshot of post-Triassic dinosaur community assembly.

## Fossil record

Scutellosaurus lawleri was described by Edwin Colbert in 1981 from multiple specimens collected in Arizona, making it one of the better-represented Early Jurassic ornithischians from North America. The species name honours James Lawler, who contributed to the collection of material. Colbert recognised it as a very early member of the armoured dinosaur lineage.

Multiple partial skeletons and abundant osteoderms; no wholly complete individual.

- Long hindlimbs and tail support habitual bipedality.
- The exact distribution of its numerous small osteoderms is not preserved as a complete articulated armour covering.

## Where it lived

Early Jurassic terrestrial redbeds of the Colorado Plateau yielding multiple Scutellosaurus specimens alongside other dinosaurs and early mammals.

The Kayenta record is Early Jurassic and considerably younger/broader than 205-202 Ma.

## Classification

Scutellosaurus possessed over 300 small, low-keeled bony scutes embedded in the skin of the back and sides. These osteoderms are considered homologous to the much larger and more elaborate armour plates and spikes of stegosaurs and ankylosaurs. Scutellosaurus represents an early stage in the evolution of defensive armour in Thyreophora, when the elements were still small and numerous.

## Open questions

- Armour arrangement and formation-level age resolution remain incomplete.
