## Overview

Einiosaurus procurvicornis ("buffalo lizard") was a centrosaurine ceratopsid from the Two Medicine Formation of Montana (~74 Ma), immediately recognisable by its strongly downward-curved nasal horn — unique among ceratopsids — and two long spikes projecting upward from the top of the frill. It represents part of a remarkable stratigraphic succession of centrosaurines through the Two Medicine Formation, where different species appear in successive layers.

Massive bone beds preserve hundreds of Einiosaurus individuals — consistent with herding behaviour and catastrophic mass mortality events comparable to those in Alberta. The Two Medicine centrosaurine succession (Rubeosaurus → Einiosaurus → Achelousaurus) shows an apparent anagenetic or cladogenetic sequence of forms through time, providing one of the best records of ceratopsid evolution within a single formation.

## Anatomy & life

Three centrosaurine species appear in successive stratigraphic levels of the Two Medicine Formation over ~2.5 million years: Rubeosaurus ovatus (oldest), Einiosaurus procurvicornis (intermediate) and Achelousaurus horneri (youngest). Whether this represents true evolutionary succession within a single evolving lineage (anagenesis) or replacement by separate species (cladogenesis) is debated.

The remarkable diversity of centrosaurines — with different genera in different formations, different time slices and different geographic areas — may reflect rapid evolution driven by sexual selection. Different horn and frill configurations served primarily as species recognition billboards rather than weapons; divergence in display structures could drive rapid speciation in socially complex herding animals.

## Fossil record

Scott Sampson described Einiosaurus procurvicornis in 1995 from Two Medicine Formation material. The downward-curved nasal horn is the most diagnostic feature and gives the animal an immediately distinctive profile among ceratopsids. Bone beds with hundreds of individuals confirm its social, herding lifestyle.

Two multi-individual bonebeds with abundant cranial and postcranial remains across growth stages.

Bonebeds document mass mortality and herd living; the distinctive curved nasal horn is unique among centrosaurines.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous seasonal floodplain of Montana with nesting hadrosaurs and diverse ceratopsians.

## Open questions

- Bonebed disarticulation complicates individual association.
- Horn function, population structure and exact cause of death remain uncertain.
