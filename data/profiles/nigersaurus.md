## Overview

Nigersaurus taqueti was a small (9-metre), highly unusual rebbachisaurid sauropod from the Early Cretaceous of Niger (~115 Ma), notable for its extremely broad, straight-edged muzzle lined with hundreds of tiny leaf-shaped teeth — up to 600 teeth arranged in dense batteries at the front of the jaw like a lawnmower blade. This dental system was adapted for close-cropping ground-level vegetation.

The skull of Nigersaurus is among the most bizarre of any dinosaur: the jaws open forward (not downward) because the entire front of the skull faces down, positioning the tooth batteries at the soil surface. This extreme downward-facing muzzle is unique among sauropods and has been interpreted as an adaptation for grazing at ground level — unusual for an animal with a long neck.

## Anatomy & life

The teeth of Nigersaurus are arranged in columns — up to 50 columns per tooth row — with replacement teeth stacked behind each functional tooth. The total tooth count can reach 500–600 at any one time, with the fastest tooth replacement rate known for any sauropod: each tooth replaced approximately every 14 days. The teeth are unusually thin-walled for a sauropod, optimised for rapid replacement rather than durability.

CT scanning of the inner ear and skull orientation suggests that Nigersaurus habitually held its head facing downward toward the ground — different from most sauropods. This, combined with the forward-facing tooth battery, indicates it was a ground-level grazer, cropping ferns and horsetails close to the soil surface in the lush Early Cretaceous Saharan floodplains.

## Fossil record

Fragments of Nigersaurus were found by French palaeontologists in the 1950s and named by Sereno and colleagues in 1999 from new material collected during Sereno's Saharan expeditions. A thorough redescription in 2007 using CT scanning revealed the extraordinary internal skull structure and dental battery arrangement.

Multiple partial skeletons including exceptional skulls, jaws, neck and postcranial material.

The extraordinary dental battery with over 500 teeth and the wide straight-edged muzzle are unique among sauropods.

- Its broad muzzle carried dental batteries with hundreds of replacement teeth.
- The skull and neck support low browsing, but claims that it held its head permanently downward overstate biomechanical inference.
- Several individuals collectively document much of the skeleton; no single complete skeleton is known.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous North African river and floodplain ecosystem alongside Suchomimus and Sarcosuchus.

The Elrhaz Formation is Aptian-Albian; the age remains formation-level rather than a precise 115-105 Ma lifespan.

## Open questions

- Habitual head posture and exact vegetation preference remain debated.
- Skeletons are composite across individuals.
