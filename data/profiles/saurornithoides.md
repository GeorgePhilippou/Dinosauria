## Overview

Saurornithoides (meaning 'bird-like lizard') was a small carnivorous troodontid theropod from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia and Central Asia, living approximately 85–66 million years ago (Campanian–Maastrichtian). Named by Henry Fairfield Osborn in 1924, it is estimated at about 3 metres in length and is known from the Djadokhta, Nemegt, and related formations of Mongolia, as well as from Uzbekistan and possibly China and North America. It had relatively large eyes and a large brain for a dinosaur.

Saurornithoides belongs to Troodontidae within Paraves and is closely related to Troodon of North America. Troodontids are among the most encephalised (large-brained relative to body size) non-avian dinosaurs, and Saurornithoides has contributed to discussions of sensory capabilities and intelligence in theropods. Two species are currently recognised: the type S. mongoliensis (Campanian) and the later and larger S. junior (Maastrichtian).

## Anatomy & life

Two species of Saurornithoides are currently recognised. The type species S. mongoliensis, from the Djadokhta Formation (~75–71 Ma), is the smaller and older form. S. junior, from the Nemegt Formation (~72–66 Ma), is larger and is sometimes regarded as a separate genus. The relationship between these two species and other troodontids such as the North American Troodon continues to be studied.

Troodontids including Saurornithoides are notable for having the highest known brain-to-body mass ratios among non-avian dinosaurs, comparable in some measures to modern birds. Large orbits suggest well-developed vision, and enlarged olfactory regions imply a keen sense of smell. These features have led to speculation about relatively complex behaviours, though direct evidence is limited.

Saurornithoides was likely an agile pursuit predator of small prey—lizards, early mammals, eggs, and insects—using its stereoscopic vision and sickle-shaped second toe claw. Its ecology in the Djadokhta Formation has been compared to that of modern hawks and mongooses, occupying a small-to-medium predator niche in the Gobi Desert ecosystem.

## Fossil record

Saurornithoides mongoliensis was described by Henry Fairfield Osborn in 1924 from material collected by the American Museum of Natural History's Central Asiatic Expeditions led by Roy Chapman Andrews. It was one of the first small theropods described from Mongolia and contributed to early recognition of the diversity of Gobi Desert dinosaur faunas.

One principal partial skull and skeleton with limited referrals.

- Saurornithoides junior was transferred to the separate genus Zanabazar.
- Large eyes and teeth support predatory or omnivorous habits, but neither a categorical diet nor nocturnality is established for this genus.
