## Overview

Tsintaosaurus spinorhinus was a lambeosaurine hadrosaur from the Late Cretaceous Wangshi Formation of Shandong, China (~70 Ma), famous for what appeared to be a unicorn-like spike projecting upward from the skull. This spike was originally interpreted as a nasal crest; subsequent analysis of the skull showed that the spike is actually a solid nasal process forming a different crest shape from other lambeosaurines.

Asian hadrosaurs provide important data on the dispersal of hadrosaurs from their North American origin to Asia (via Beringia) and subsequently to Europe. Tsintaosaurus shows that lambeosaurines — the crested hadrosaur lineage — successfully colonised Asia, producing a distinct Asian clade that paralleled the North American radiation.

## Anatomy & life

Hadrosaurs originated in North America and dispersed to Asia across the Beringian land connection in the Late Cretaceous, then from Asia to Europe. Asian lambeosaurines like Tsintaosaurus, Pararhabdodon (Spain) and Magnapaulia (Mexico) represent the westward dispersal from the Asian lambeosaurine radiation. This transcontinental dispersal occurred at roughly the same time as the last land connections before sea-level rise isolated the continents.

The Wangshi Formation of Shandong preserves a diverse Late Cretaceous fauna including other hadrosaurs (Tanius, Shantungosaurus — one of the largest hadrosaurs at up to 16 m), theropods and pterosaurs. The region was a lowland plain near the Late Cretaceous sea margin of eastern Asia.

## Fossil record

Described by C. C. Young in 1958 from material found in Shandong Province (then transliterated as Tsingtao). The forward-projecting spine on the skull was reconstructed as an upright unicorn-like crest — a striking feature that made Tsintaosaurus famous among popular dinosaur books. Subsequent re-examination of the skull showed the crest is formed differently from other lambeosaurine crests, with a more complex nasal bone configuration.

Multiple skulls and partial skeletons, some historically reconstructed incorrectly.

The distinctive forward-projecting hollow crest was confirmed by later more complete specimens after initial scepticism.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous of eastern China, in fluvial and floodplain settings.
