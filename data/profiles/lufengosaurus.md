## Overview

Lufengosaurus was a large herbivorous massospondylid sauropodomorph dinosaur that lived during the Early Jurassic, approximately 200–190 million years ago, in the Lufeng Basin of Yunnan Province, southwestern China. Estimated at around 6 metres in length, it was one of the largest dinosaurs of its time in Asia and was primarily bipedal, though it may also have moved on all fours while feeding. The type species is Lufengosaurus huenei.

Lufengosaurus holds the distinction of being the first dinosaur to be mounted and displayed in China, making it an icon of Chinese palaeontology. It is known from multiple specimens, providing a relatively good understanding of its skeletal anatomy. A 2017 study reported the oldest fossilised collagen and blood vessel remnants from a Lufengosaurus rib, offering a rare glimpse into dinosaur soft tissue preservation.

## Anatomy & life

Lufengosaurus became the first dinosaur to be mounted and exhibited in China when a skeleton was displayed in the 1950s, making it a cultural and scientific landmark for Chinese palaeontology. Its image appeared on a Chinese postage stamp in 1958, further cementing its status as an iconic Chinese dinosaur. Numerous specimens from the Lufeng Basin have since been studied, providing one of the better skeletal datasets for any Early Jurassic sauropodomorph.

A landmark 2017 study published in Nature Communications reported the recovery of the oldest known fossilised collagen fibres and microstructures resembling blood vessels from a Lufengosaurus rib bone. Though claims of ancient protein preservation in dinosaur fossils remain scientifically debated, the Lufengosaurus specimens contributed important evidence to discussions about the limits of biomolecular preservation over geological time.

## Fossil record

Lufengosaurus huenei was named in 1940 by C. C. Young (Yang Zhongjian), the founder of Chinese vertebrate palaeontology, based on material from the Lufeng Basin. The genus name honours Lufeng County, while the species name honours the German palaeontologist Friedrich von Huene, who made major contributions to early dinosaur classification. Young later named a second species, L. magnus, based on larger specimens, though this species is considered dubious by some researchers.

## Classification

Lufengosaurus belongs to Massospondylidae, a family of early sauropodomorphs (informally called 'prosauropods') that were widespread in the Early Jurassic. It is closely related to Massospondylus from southern Africa, reflecting the faunal connections between Asia and Africa during this period when Pangaea was beginning to fragment. Like other massospondylids, Lufengosaurus was a facultative biped with a long neck suited to browsing on vegetation.

## Open questions

- The numerical age of Lower Lufeng units is imprecise.
- Taxonomic boundaries among Lufeng sauropodomorph specimens need continued revision.
- Parental care is not directly demonstrated.
