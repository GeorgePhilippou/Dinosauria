## Overview

Riojasaurus incertus was a large Late Triassic prosauropod from the Ischigualasto-Villa Unión Basin of Argentina (~216–203 Ma), reaching about 10 metres — making it one of the largest dinosaurs of the Triassic. Unlike Plateosaurus, which was a biped, Riojasaurus shows adaptations for quadrupedal locomotion: its limb bones are denser and more pillar-like, suggesting it habitually walked on all fours.

As one of the largest Early Mesozoic herbivores, Riojasaurus demonstrates that prosauropods rapidly evolved large body sizes after the origin of the dinosaur lineage — filling ecological roles vacated by the declining non-dinosaurian megaherbivores (rhynchosaurs, dicynodonts) of the Triassic.

## Anatomy & life

Riojasaurus limb bones are proportionally more robust and the humeral torsion less pronounced than in Plateosaurus, suggesting habitual quadrupedal locomotion. This makes it one of the earliest large dinosaurs to use all four legs routinely, anticipating the fully quadrupedal posture of the giant sauropods that would follow.

By the Late Triassic, prosauropods like Riojasaurus had become the dominant large herbivores, displacing the rhynchosaurs and dicynodonts that had previously dominated. Their long necks and large size allowed access to vegetation at multiple heights — a feeding strategy that sauropods would take to extremes. Riojasaurus lived alongside small early dinosaurs and the surviving non-dinosaurian archosaur fauna of the Triassic.

## Fossil record

Described by Bonaparte in 1969 from the Los Colorados Formation of La Rioja Province, Argentina. The type specimen is incomplete but includes much of the vertebral column and limb bones, enough to establish its large size and pillar-like limb anatomy. Multiple additional specimens have been recovered from the same unit.

Several partial postcranial skeletons with limited referred cranial material.

Dense limb bones and large prosauropod body plan document the early evolution of large-bodied plant-eating dinosaurs.

- Robust limb bones show a heavy-bodied animal capable of quadrupedal support.
- Whether adults were obligate quadrupeds or retained meaningful bipedal ability is biomechanically debated.
- Herbivory is strongly inferred from sauropodomorph anatomy, while precise feeding height and body mass remain estimates.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic terrestrial environment of the Ischigualasto-Villa Unión basin.

The Los Colorados Formation is Norian-Rhaetian; its exact numerical duration remains under refinement.

## Open questions

- Skull association and species-level variation need modern redescription.
- Habitual gait is unresolved.
