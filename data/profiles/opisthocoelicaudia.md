## Overview

Opisthocoelicaudia (meaning 'hollow-backed tail', referring to the opisthocoeolous — convex at the rear — caudal vertebrae) was a titanosaur sauropod from the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian, approximately 72–66 million years ago) of Mongolia. The single known specimen comes from the Nemegt Formation of Omnogovi and comprises a nearly complete postcranial skeleton from which the skull and neck are missing. It was about 12 metres in length.

Described by Borsuk-Białynicka in 1977, Opisthocoelicaudia skarzynskii is placed in Saltasauridae within Opisthocoelicaudiinae. Its highly distinctive caudal vertebrae — uniquely convex at both ends (amphicoelous-like, yet with a notable posterior convexity) — are unlike any other sauropod and give the genus its name. For many years it was debated whether Opisthocoelicaudia might be the body of Nemegtosaurus, whose skull was found in the same formation; current consensus treats them as separate taxa.

## Anatomy & life

The caudal vertebrae of Opisthocoelicaudia are opisthocoeolous — they are convex on the posterior face, fitting into cup-shaped sockets on the following vertebra, the reverse of the procoelous condition common in other titanosaurs. This arrangement would have created an unusually rigid tail, which some researchers interpret as an adaptation for tripodal rearing posture to reach high vegetation.

Since both Opisthocoelicaudia (headless body) and Nemegtosaurus (skull only) were found in the same Nemegt Formation deposits, it was proposed they represented a single species. Careful anatomical comparisons, including limb proportions and vertebral morphology, have led most researchers to conclude they are distinct genera, though this debate has been productive in highlighting the challenges of matching isolated skeletal elements.

The absence of the skull and neck from an otherwise well-preserved individual is intriguing. One hypothesis is that the head and neck were removed by scavengers before burial, or that they were washed away separately during deposition. The Nemegt Formation preserves a diverse Late Cretaceous community, and Opisthocoelicaudia would have been one of the largest herbivores in that ecosystem.

## Fossil record

Opisthocoelicaudia skarzynskii was described by the Polish palaeontologist Maria Borsuk-Białynicka in 1977. The specimen was collected during the Polish-Mongolian Palaeontological Expeditions to Mongolia in the 1960s–70s. The species name honours expedition member Skarzyński. The specimen is notable for its exceptional completeness — unusual for a large sauropod — minus the missing skull and cervical vertebrae.

## Where it lived

Maastrichtian fluvial deposits of the Gobi Desert where the near-complete postcranial holotype of Opisthocoelicaudia was excavated by a Polish-Mongolian expedition.

## Open questions

- Possible synonymy with Nemegtosaurus cannot be tested using overlapping type anatomy.
- The skull and neck remain unknown.
