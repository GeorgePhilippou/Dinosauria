## Overview

Pentaceratops sternbergii ("five-horned face") was a chasmosaurine ceratopsid from the Campanian of New Mexico (~76 Ma), named for what appeared to be five horns: the nasal horn, two brow horns, and two elongated cheek horns (epijugals). Its skull with frill — reaching an estimated 3 metres in length — rivals Torosaurus for the largest ceratopsid skull ever, though the frill is fully open to debate regarding preservation.

As a southern North American chasmosaurine, Pentaceratops fills an important biogeographic gap: its large body size and elaborate frill help document how chasmosaurine horn and frill complexity evolved between the earlier Chasmosaurus of Alberta and the later Triceratops of the same region, providing a morphological bridge in the lineage.

## Anatomy & life

A referred skull (OMNH 10165) estimated at 2.3 metres without frill and over 3 metres with frill has been cited as the largest known ceratopsid skull, though the completeness of this estimate is questioned. The frill parietal fenestrae are proportionally large, and the epiparietals (frill margin bones) are prominent.

The San Juan Basin of New Mexico preserves a diverse late Campanian fauna including hadrosaurs, ankylosaurs and various small dinosaurs. Pentaceratops is the dominant ceratopsid in this fauna, which differs in composition from the contemporaneous Dinosaur Park Formation fauna of Alberta — evidence for a biogeographic boundary (Laramidia North vs South) in Late Cretaceous North America.

## Fossil record

Named by Osborn in 1923 from Sternberg's New Mexico material. The "five horns" are the nasal horn, paired brow horns, and paired elongated epijugals (cheek projections). Strictly speaking, only the brow horns and nasal horn are true horns developed from nasal/frontal bones; the epijugals are enlarged processes of the jugal bone, present in all ceratopsids but greatly elongated in Pentaceratops.

Multiple partial skulls and skeletons; some referrals of disputed identity.

The enormous skull with five horn points is diagnostic; skull dimensions make it among the largest skulls of any land animal.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous forested floodplains of the American Southwest.

## Open questions

- The identity of OMNH 10165 remains disputed.
- Some historical species and referrals have been reassigned.
