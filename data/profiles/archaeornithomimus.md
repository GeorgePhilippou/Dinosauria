## Overview

Archaeornithomimus asiaticus was an ornithomimosaurian theropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Inner Mongolia, China, and Kazakhstan, living approximately 90–72 million years ago during the Coniacian to Campanian stages. Estimated at about 3.5 metres in length, it is known from material recovered from the Iren Dabasu Formation and the Dabrazhin Formation. As its name suggests — 'ancient bird mimic' — it is considered an early member of the ornithomimid lineage.

Named by Russell in 1972, Archaeornithomimus is classified within the family Ornithomimidae and is of significant interest as one of the more primitive and geologically older ornithomimids known. Its relatively early age and Asian distribution have made it important for reconstructing the evolutionary origins of the ornithomimid lineage, which subsequently diversified across both Asia and North America in the Late Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Archaeornithomimus shares the general ornithomimid body plan — toothless beaked skull, slender neck, compact torso, and long cursorial hind limbs — but retains some features considered more primitive than those of later members of the group. The forelimbs bear three clawed digits. The overall proportions are consistent with a fast-running lifestyle, and like other ornithomimids it was probably feathered.

Like other ornithomimids, Archaeornithomimus was probably an omnivore, using its toothless beak to process a variety of food sources including plant material and small animals. PBDB ecospace data characterises it as a ground-dwelling herbivore to omnivore. The gregarious habits attributed to ornithomimids by some researchers may also apply to this genus.

Archaeornithomimus is one of the geologically oldest members of Ornithomimidae, providing evidence that the family was established in Asia before the Campanian. Its presence in the Iren Dabasu Formation — an early Cenomanian to Turonian deposit — alongside early tyrannosaurs and hadrosauroids suggests that diverse coelurosaur lineages were already well differentiated in Asia by the mid-Late Cretaceous.

## Fossil record

Archaeornithomimus asiaticus was named by Dale Russell in 1972, based on material from the Iren Dabasu Formation of Inner Mongolia that had been collected during earlier Sino-Soviet expeditions. The generic name means 'ancient bird mimic', positioning it as a primitive precursor to later ornithomimids. The species name refers to its Asian origin.

Disarticulated postcranial bones from several individuals.

## Where it lived

The primary source of Archaeornithomimus material, this formation has also yielded other important Late Cretaceous dinosaurs including Bactrosaurus and early tyrannosauroids.
