## Overview

Giganotosaurus carolinii was a giant carcharodontosaurid predator from the Candeleros Formation of Patagonia. It is known from one incomplete skeleton with much of the skull and a referred partial lower jaw; no complete skeleton exists.

Giganotosaurus carolinii was a carcharodontosaurid theropod from the Cenomanian of Patagonia (~98 Ma), and with an estimated length of 12–13 metres it is among the longest carnivorous dinosaurs ever found. Discovered by amateur fossil hunter Rubén Carolini in 1993 in the Neuquén Group, it briefly held the title of "largest carnivore ever" before overlapping size estimates with Spinosaurus and Tyrannosaurus complicated the comparison.

As a carcharodontosaurid, Giganotosaurus was more closely related to Carcharodontosaurus of North Africa than to Tyrannosaurus — yet the two giants lived at almost the same time, representing parallel evolution of enormous predators on separate landmasses. Giganotosaurus shared its ecosystem with the titanosaur Argentinosaurus.

## Anatomy & life

Giganotosaurus was a huge theropod from the early Late Cretaceous of Patagonia. Length estimates of roughly 12–13 metres and mass estimates from about four to eight tonnes come from an incomplete skeleton and depend on how the missing parts are reconstructed, so comparisons with Tyrannosaurus and Spinosaurus are not settled.

Its skull was long and low with blade-like, serrated teeth suited to slicing flesh rather than crushing bone, and its braincase was small relative to body size. The skull bones are incomplete and distorted, so the published skull length of around 1.5–1.8 metres is a reconstruction.

It lived alongside giant titanosaurian sauropods such as Andesaurus and rebbachisaurids, and large sauropods are usually assumed to have been among its prey. Whether it hunted alone or in groups is unknown; the related Mapusaurus has been found in a multi-individual bonebed, but no such evidence exists for Giganotosaurus.

- Among the largest theropods known, though its exact size relative to Tyrannosaurus depends on reconstruction.
- Blade-like teeth and a long, low skull mark it as a carcharodontosaurid rather than a tyrannosaur-like bone-crusher.
- Discovered by an amateur fossil hunter, Rubén Carolini, whose name the species carries.

**Feeding: Meat-eater.** Serrated, laterally compressed teeth indicate slicing flesh from large prey; specific prey species are not documented by bite marks or gut contents.

**Movement: Bipedal.** Robust hindlimbs and small forelimbs indicate two-legged walking; speed estimates are model-based and vary widely.

**Appearance: Skeletal outline reconstructed.** Body proportions are reasonably constrained; the distorted skull, skin texture and colour are not directly known.

## Fossil record

Giganotosaurus rests on a single incomplete skeleton with much of the skull, pelvis and limbs, plus a referred partial dentary from a slightly larger animal. Its giant size is secure; its precise proportions are not.

The holotype includes much of the skull and lower jaws, the braincase, most of the vertebral column, ribs, the pelvis and the hindlimbs, and was estimated by its describers at about 70 per cent complete. A separate partial dentary about eight per cent larger was later referred to the genus.

Because a single individual supplies almost all the anatomy, size estimates and skull proportions carry more uncertainty than the animal's fame suggests.

Large skull bones and serrated teeth support its identity as one of the largest known theropods.

- Its skull is reconstructed from incomplete and distorted bones.
- It rivalled the largest theropods in length, but claims that it was definitively larger than Tyrannosaurus depend on uncertain reconstructions and mass models.
- Blade-like serrated teeth support predation on large vertebrates; specific prey and hunting groups are unknown.

## Where it lived

Early Late Cretaceous Patagonian ecosystems with giant sauropods.

The Candeleros Formation is early Cenomanian, approximately 99-97 Ma.

## Classification

Giganotosaurus is a carcharodontosaurine carcharodontosaurid, a member of the giant allosauroid radiation that dominated Gondwanan faunas in the mid-Cretaceous. It is closely related to Mapusaurus from slightly younger Argentine rocks and to Carcharodontosaurus from North Africa.

## Open questions

### How heavy was it?

Estimates range from about four to over eight tonnes depending on the model and on how the incomplete skeleton is reconstructed.

### What did the complete skull look like?

The preserved skull bones are incomplete and distorted, so total skull length and snout shape are reconstructed rather than measured.

### Did it hunt sauropods in groups?

Giant sauropods shared its habitat, but no bite marks, trackways or bonebeds document either its prey or any group behaviour.
