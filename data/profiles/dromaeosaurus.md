## Overview

Dromaeosaurus (meaning 'running lizard') was a small but formidable carnivorous theropod that lived during the Late Cretaceous period, approximately 76–74 million years ago, in what is now Alberta, Canada and Montana, USA. Estimated at about 1.8 metres in length and roughly 15 kilograms, it was the namesake genus of the family Dromaeosauridae — the group colloquially known as raptors — and possessed the characteristic sickle-shaped retractable claw on its second toe. It is one of the oldest and most taxonomically important members of the dromaeosaurid family.

Described by William Diller Matthew and Barnum Brown in 1922, Dromaeosaurus is known from fragmentary material including partial skull and jaw elements. Despite being the eponymous genus of Dromaeosauridae, its skeletal anatomy is less completely known than many later-described relatives such as Velociraptor or Deinonychus. Its heavily built skull with robust teeth suggests it may have targeted larger prey relative to its body size compared with other small dromaeosaurids.

## Anatomy & life

Dromaeosaurus had a relatively deep, robust skull compared to many smaller dromaeosaurids, with strongly built teeth suggesting it could generate considerable bite force. Some researchers have proposed that its skull proportions are convergently similar to those of larger predators, allowing it to tackle prey disproportionate to its modest body size.

Like all dromaeosaurids, Dromaeosaurus bore a hyperextensible second toe armed with an enlarged, curved claw. Functional analyses of this structure in dromaeosaurids suggest it was used for gripping and restraining prey rather than primarily slashing, potentially allowing the animal to pin struggling victims while delivering bites.

PBDB occurrence data documents Dromaeosaurus from multiple formations across North America as well as attributed teeth from Russia (Amur) and Uzbekistan (Navoi), though some referrals outside Canada and the USA should be treated cautiously given the fragmentary nature of disarticulated teeth. The North American record spans the Campanian to Maastrichtian.

## Fossil record

Dromaeosaurus albertensis was described by William Diller Matthew and Barnum Brown in 1922, based on material collected in the Red Deer River badlands of Alberta. It became the founding genus of Dromaeosauridae, a family name now applied to dozens of species worldwide. The holotype material includes a partial skull and lower jaws.

Partial skull and mandible with isolated referred teeth and fragmentary bones.

Principal locality; the Horseshoe Canyon and Oldman equivalents have produced most known material.
