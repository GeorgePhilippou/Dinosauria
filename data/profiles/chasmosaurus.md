## Overview

Chasmosaurus belli was a chasmosaurine ceratopsid from the Campanian of Alberta (~76 Ma), notable for its enormous frill relative to body size — larger proportionally than any other ceratopsid. The frill contained two large parietal fenestrae (openings) that reduced its weight while maintaining its impressive visual size, and a network of blood vessels supplied the skin covering the frill, enabling colour flushing for display.

Chasmosaurus is the namesake of Chasmosaurinae — the chasmosaurine lineage that ultimately produced Triceratops. Unlike the relatively short frills of centrosaurines, chasmosaurines evolved increasingly large, elongated frills. Chasmosaurus itself shows variation in skull and frill morphology between specimens, some of which may reflect sexual dimorphism.

## Anatomy & life

The chasmosaurine frill is structurally different from the centrosaurine frill: larger, with bigger parietal fenestrae covered by a thin skin membrane in life. Vascular channels in the frill bones indicate blood flow that could flush the skin with colour. The enormous display surface was almost certainly brightly patterned in life, used for species recognition, mate choice and possibly threat displays.

Two frill morphs are present in Chasmosaurus specimens from the same formation: one with a squamosal notch and oval frill (the B morph) and one without (the A morph). These may represent male and female, with the more elaborate frill being male. Similar sexual dimorphism has been proposed for other ceratopsids.

## Fossil record

Named by Lambe in 1914 from Dinosaur Park Formation material; Lambe's Monoclonius belli was later reassigned to Chasmosaurus. The genus is pivotal for understanding chasmosaurine evolution: it shows the large frill and relatively modest nasal horn that characterise early chasmosaurines, before the later evolution of elaborate brow horns and larger nasal horns in Triceratops-grade animals.

Numerous skulls and partial to nearly complete skeletons across growth stages, with some skin impressions.

Multiple skulls with different frill shapes document intraspecific or interspecific variation in the chasmosaurine lineage.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous coastal plain of Alberta alongside Corythosaurus, Parasaurolophus and Gorgosaurus.

## Open questions

- C. belli and C. russelli may overlap in variable characters.
- Some specimens historically assigned to Chasmosaurus are Vagaceratops or indeterminate.
