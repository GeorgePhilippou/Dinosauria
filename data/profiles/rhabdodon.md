## Overview

Rhabdodon (meaning 'rod tooth') was a medium-sized herbivorous ornithopod from the Late Cretaceous of Europe, living approximately 83–66 million years ago (Campanian–Maastrichtian). Named by Matheron in 1869, it reached about 4 metres in length and is known from Austria, France, Romania, and Spain, making it one of the most geographically widespread European ornithopods. It belongs to the family Rhabdodontidae within Iguanodontia, a family endemic to Late Cretaceous Europe.

Rhabdodon is scientifically significant as a representative of the insular European dinosaur fauna of the Late Cretaceous, when much of western Europe was an archipelago of islands separated from the main continental landmasses. Island-dwelling dinosaurs of this period, including Rhabdodon, are thought to have evolved at smaller body sizes or retained primitive features due to island conditions, a phenomenon related to insular dwarfism in some lineages.

## Anatomy & life

Rhabdodon had distinctive cylindrical, rod-like teeth (reflected in its name) suited to cropping vegetation. Its skeleton shows an ornithopod body plan with bipedal capabilities, though it likely adopted quadrupedal postures during feeding. It was substantially smaller than related mainland forms, which may in part reflect island constraints on body size.

With confirmed fossil material from Austria, France, Romania, and Spain, Rhabdodon has one of the widest geographic distributions of any Late Cretaceous European dinosaur. This distribution reflects either long-range dispersal across the European archipelago or a broad original range before subsequent sea-level changes isolated populations.

## Fossil record

Rhabdodon priscus was named by Philippe Matheron in 1869 from material collected in southern France. It was one of the first dinosaurs described from the Late Cretaceous of Europe and has since been recovered from multiple countries across the paleo-European archipelago, making it a cornerstone taxon for understanding Cretaceous European ornithopods.

Lectotype dentary plus abundant disarticulated cranial and postcranial referrals from southern France.

- A 2025 reassessment diagnosed R. priscus using the type material rather than the old continent-wide wastebasket.
- Romanian and Austrian material once called Rhabdodon belongs to Zalmoxes and Mochlodon.
- R. septimanicus may be distinct, so French material should not all be merged automatically.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous French continental deposits that have produced substantial Rhabdodon material.

The genus spans Campanian to lower Maastrichtian French units, not a narrow 76-70 Ma interval.

## Classification

Rhabdodon gives its name to the family Rhabdodontidae, a group of ornithopods apparently endemic to the Late Cretaceous European island system. Other members include Zalmoxes from Romania. These dinosaurs are thought to have evolved in geographic isolation as Tethys Sea transgression created a fragmented European archipelago during the Campanian and Maastrichtian.

## Open questions

- Species boundaries within French material remain under revision.
- Many postcranial referrals are not associated with diagnostic jaws.
