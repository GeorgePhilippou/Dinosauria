## Overview

Proceratosaurus (meaning 'before Ceratosaurus') was a medium-sized carnivorous theropod from the Middle Jurassic of England, living approximately 168–165 million years ago. Named by Arthur Smith Woodward in 1910, it is estimated to have reached about 4.1 metres in length and is known only from a single partial skull found in Gloucestershire, England, from the White Limestone / Great Oolite Formation. It bore a prominent nasal crest.

Proceratosaurus is now recognised as an early tyrannosauroid rather than a ceratosaur (the name was given before the skull's affinity was understood). Together with related genera such as Guanlong and Kileskus, it defines the family Proceratosauridae, an early radiation of crested tyrannosauroids. This discovery significantly extended the temporal and geographic range of the tyrannosauroid lineage back into the Middle Jurassic.

## Anatomy & life

Proceratosaurus was reclassified as a basal tyrannosauroid following work by Rauhut and colleagues, who identified tyrannosauroid features in the skull bones. It now anchors the Proceratosauridae, demonstrating that the tyrannosauroid lineage had already diversified by the Middle Jurassic, some 90 million years before Tyrannosaurus rex.

The skull bears a prominent crest over the nasal bones, which may have served a display function or aided in species recognition. Crests appear independently across several theropod lineages, suggesting convergent evolution of display structures in dinosaurs.

Because only the skull is known, full body reconstructions of Proceratosaurus are necessarily speculative and are informed by comparison with better-known relatives like Guanlong. Its overall size estimate of roughly 4 metres should be treated as approximate.

## Fossil record

The holotype skull of Proceratosaurus bradleyi was described by Arthur Smith Woodward in 1910 from material collected in Gloucestershire. It was initially misidentified as related to Ceratosaurus due to its nasal crest; reassignment to Tyrannosauroidea came much later following phylogenetic analyses in the 2000s.

Single partial skull and mandible; no secure associated postcranium.

- CT and redescription identified it as a proceratosaurid tyrannosauroid, not a close relative of Ceratosaurus.
- A low crest rises from the nasal region, but its complete shape and function are unknown.
- No secure postcranial skeleton exists, so body size and proportions are inferred from relatives.

## Open questions

- Only one partial skull is known.
- Body size, forelimbs and covering are not directly known.
