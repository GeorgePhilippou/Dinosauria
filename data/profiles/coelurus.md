## Overview

Coelurus was a small coelurosaur theropod from the Late Jurassic of North America, approximately 155–145 million years ago (Kimmeridgian to Tithonian), known from the Morrison Formation of Wyoming. Estimated at about 1.8 metres in length, it was a lightly built, bipedal carnivore with elongate legs suited to swift pursuit. Its name, meaning 'hollow tail', references the hollow vertebrae that distinguish it from heavier theropods.

Described by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1879 from a partial skeleton, Coelurus fragilis has had a convoluted taxonomic history and was historically confused with the contemporary Ornitholestes. Only one species is currently recognised as valid. As a basal coelurosaur from the Morrison Formation, Coelurus is relevant to understanding the early diversification of the coelurosaurian lineage that would later give rise to dromaeosaurids, birds, and other derived theropods.

## Anatomy & life

Coelurus had elongate hindlimbs proportioned for fast running, three-fingered hands with grasping claws, and a stiff tail used as a counterbalance. Its vertebrae had large pleurocoels (hollow side pockets), lightening the skeleton. The skull is not known from the type specimen, so inferences about its head shape and teeth rely on comparison with related coelurosaurs. It was a small, agile predator, likely hunting small vertebrates and invertebrates.

The Morrison Formation is one of the most famous dinosaur-bearing formations in the world, preserving a diverse Late Jurassic fauna that includes Allosaurus, Stegosaurus, Diplodocus, Camarasaurus, and many others. Coelurus occupied the small-predator niche in this ecosystem alongside contemporaries such as Ornitholestes and Tanycolagreus. Its fragmentary nature makes it one of the lesser-known members of this renowned fauna.

## Fossil record

Coelurus fragilis was described by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1879 from partial postcranial material discovered in the Morrison Formation of Como Bluff, Wyoming. The generic name refers to the hollow tail vertebrae, and the species name fragilis means 'fragile', reflecting the delicate, lightly built nature of the bones. The specimen is known from one partial skeleton, and subsequent referrals of other material have been largely rejected.

Coelurus has a long and complicated taxonomic history typical of early-described dinosaurs. Over the decades, several species were named and later transferred to other genera or considered nomen dubium. It was frequently confused with Ornitholestes, another small Morrison coelurosaur, until more rigorous anatomical comparison separated them. Currently only C. fragilis is accepted as valid, based solely on the original Morrison specimen.
