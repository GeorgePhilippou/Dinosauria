## Overview

Borogovia gracilicrus was a small troodontid theropod from the Nemegt Formation of Mongolia, dating to the late Maastrichtian (~72–66 Ma). Named by Halszka Osmólska in 1987, it is known only from partial hindlimb material — two tibiae, metatarsals, and foot elements — making detailed reconstruction speculative. Its species name gracilicrus means 'slender leg' in Latin, reflecting its notably elongate, lightly built lower leg bones. The genus name is a direct reference to the 'borogoves' of Lewis Carroll's nonsense poem Jabberwocky (1871), continuing the literary naming tradition occasionally used for Mongolian troodontids.

Despite the fragmentary nature of its remains, Borogovia is scientifically significant for a distinctive feature of its second toe: unlike most other troodontids and dromaeosaurids, whose second-toe claw is sharply recurved and sickle-shaped (the 'falciphoran condition'), Borogovia's second ungual is relatively straight and modest in size. This suggests it did not use an enlarged killing claw in the manner of Velociraptor, and may represent a different predatory strategy — or a shift toward a more omnivorous diet — within the Troodontidae.

## Anatomy & life

The most distinctive and scientifically significant feature of Borogovia is the morphology of its second toe ungual. In dromaeosaurids and most troodontids, the second toe bears a large, strongly curved 'sickle claw' held off the ground and used in prey restraint or killing — the so-called falciphoran condition. Borogovia's second toe ungual is comparatively small and straight, lacking the extreme curvature seen in Velociraptor or Troodon. This has led some researchers to suggest Borogovia occupied a different ecological niche from other paravians, possibly targeting smaller or less mobile prey, or supplementing its diet with plant material or invertebrates.

## Fossil record

The Borogovia material was collected during the Polish–Mongolian Palaeontological Expeditions to the Gobi Desert in the 1960s–70s, but was not formally described until 1987 when Osmólska published her analysis. The choice of name — from Carroll's 'Jabberwocky', where 'borogoves' are described as 'mimsy' and 'outgribing' creatures of uncertain nature — was a deliberate nod to the fragmentary and puzzling nature of the fossil material, following the earlier Mongolian troodontid Saurornithoides. The species name gracilicrus refers to the gracile (slender) lower leg proportions.

## Where it lived

The Nemegt Formation at the type locality yielded the partial hindlimb material that defines Borogovia; the same beds also preserve the much larger troodontid Tochisaurus and a diverse fauna including Tarbosaurus and Deinocheirus.

The type comes from the Maastrichtian Nemegt Formation.

## Classification

Because Borogovia is known only from hindlimb elements, its placement within Troodontidae is based on shared features of the tibia, metatarsals, and toe bones rather than the skull and forelimb characters often used to diagnose troodontids. Its assignment to the family is broadly accepted but the absence of cranial material means key phylogenetic questions — such as its exact relationship to Troodon, Saurornithoides, and the Asian troodontids — remain unresolved. Additional material from the Nemegt Formation could substantially clarify its position.
