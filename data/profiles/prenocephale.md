## Overview

Prenocephale (meaning 'sloping head') was a small herbivorous pachycephalosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 83–66 million years ago. Named by Maryańska and Osmólska in 1974, it reached about 2.4 metres in length and is known from the Nemegt and Baruungoyot Formations of the Omnogov province. It is distinguished by its strongly domed skull with a smooth, rounded cranial surface and clusters of small nodes around the dome's margin.

Prenocephale is a member of Pachycephalosauridae within Pachycephalosauria and belongs to the subgroup Eupachycephalosauria. Its thick cranial dome has been central to debates about head-butting behaviour in pachycephalosaurs—whether they engaged in intraspecific combat or used their skulls for flank-butting displays. PBDB records also tentatively indicate possible North American occurrences (New Mexico), though these may represent distinct taxa.

## Anatomy & life

The dome of Prenocephale is smooth and strongly vaulted, and the animal has been frequently cited in discussions of pachycephalosaur headbutting behaviour. Some researchers argue the dome was used in direct cranial impacts between rivals, while others favour flank-butting or display functions; biomechanical studies remain inconclusive.

The skull is notable for its high, rounded dome of compact bone above the braincase, bordered by small bony nodes and knobs. Unlike some other pachycephalosaurs, Prenocephale lacks a flat-headed morphology, placing it firmly within the domed pachycephalosaur lineage. Its teeth are small and leaf-shaped, consistent with plant-eating.

Prenocephale shared the Late Cretaceous Mongolian environment with large sauropods such as Nemegtosaurus, theropods including Tarbosaurus, and the ankylosaur Saichania. It was likely a browser of low vegetation and fruit, occupying an herbivore niche distinct from the larger hadrosaurs of the same region.

## Fossil record

Prenocephale prenes was described in 1974 by Polish palaeontologists Teresa Maryańska and Halszka Osmólska from material collected during the Polish-Mongolian Palaeontological Expeditions to the Gobi Desert. The genus name refers to the animal's characteristically sloped, domed skull roof.

Holotype skull and partial skeleton plus referred skull material.

## Open questions

- Ontogenetic synonymy with Homalocephale remains debated.
- Diet and dome function are unresolved.
