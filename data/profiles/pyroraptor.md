## Overview

Pyroraptor (meaning 'fire thief') was a small dromaeosaurid theropod from the Late Cretaceous of southern France and Spain, living approximately 84–66 million years ago. Named by Allain and Taquet in 2000, its discovery was precipitated by a forest fire in Provence that exposed the fossiliferous rocks. Only a handful of bones and teeth are known—including a distinctive large sickle-shaped pedal claw, arm and foot elements—making size estimates uncertain but probably comparable to Velociraptor.

Pyroraptor is classified within Dromaeosauridae and represents one of the very few dromaeosaurids known from Late Cretaceous Europe, where the group was rare. Its fragmentary nature means many of its anatomical details and its exact relationships within Dromaeosauridae cannot be determined with confidence. It is thought to have been a fleet-footed predator but gained wider cultural recognition through a fictionalised swimming depiction in the 2022 film Jurassic World: Dominion.

## Anatomy & life

Known elements include a large sickle-shaped second pedal claw (the hallmark of dromaeosaurids), arm and foot bones, and teeth. Because the material is so limited, full body reconstructions are largely extrapolated from related dromaeosaurids. Estimates of body size comparable to Velociraptor (roughly 1–2 metres long) are plausible but unconfirmed.

Dromaeosaurids are exceptionally rare in the Cretaceous of Europe compared to Asia and North America. Pyroraptor's discovery—along with that of Variraptor from the same region—demonstrated that the group had a presence in Late Cretaceous Europe, probably facilitated by intermittent land connections across the Tethys Sea archipelago.

Pyroraptor gained widespread public recognition through its appearance in Jurassic World: Dominion (2022), where it was depicted swimming under ice. While some dinosaurs may have been capable of swimming, this portrayal—including its colourful plumage and aquatic hunting behaviour—far exceeds what can be inferred from the sparse fossil record.

## Fossil record

Pyroraptor olympius was formally described by Ronan Allain and Philippe Taquet in 2000. The specimen was first noticed after a forest fire burned through scrubland in the Var region of Provence in 1992, exposing the underlying Cretaceous rocks. The genus name means 'fire thief', commemorating these unusual circumstances of discovery.

Several isolated teeth and limb bones, including a pedal ungual; no articulated skeleton.
