## Overview

Talarurus (meaning 'wicker tail') was an ankylosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 99–89 million years ago. It reached about 6 metres in length and is known from fairly complete skeletal material, including a tail club — the bony mass at the tail tip that is characteristic of ankylosaurids. Named by Maleev in 1952, Talarurus plicatospineus is one of the earliest described Mongolian ankylosaurs and remains among the better-documented members of the Ankylosauridae.

Talarurus is significant as one of the oldest well-known ankylosaurids from Central Asia, predating the more famous Late Cretaceous ankylosaurs of the Gobi Desert such as Tarchia and Saichania by tens of millions of years. Its relatively basal position within Ankylosauridae makes it useful for tracing the evolutionary history of the group. The holotype and referred specimens have allowed comparison of skull and body armour morphology with later, more derived forms.

## Anatomy & life

Like other ankylosaurids, Talarurus possessed a bony tail club formed by fused distal tail vertebrae encased in bone. The club could be swung laterally by tail musculature and is thought to have been used for active defence against predators, potentially capable of breaking the bones of attacking theropods. The club is relatively modest in Talarurus compared to some later ankylosaurids, consistent with its more basal phylogenetic position.

Talarurus was protected by a suite of osteoderms embedded in the skin over the dorsal surface of the body. These included broad flat plates, keeled scutes, and possibly lateral spines, though the precise arrangement is not fully known. As in other ankylosaurids, the skull surface was fused with bony ornamentation, creating a heavily reinforced head.

The Bayan Shireh Formation preserves a diverse mid-Cretaceous fauna including the therizinosaur Segnosaurus, hadrosaurs, the ornithomimosaur Garudimimus, and various small theropods. Talarurus was the primary large ankylosaur in this community, filling a herbivorous niche similar to that of later Gobi Desert ankylosaurs.

## Fossil record

Talarurus plicatospineus was described by Evgeny Maleev in 1952 from material collected during Soviet-Mongolian palaeontological expeditions to the Bayan Shireh Formation. The genus name references the wicker-like interlocking bones of the tail club; the species name plicatospineus means 'pleated spine', referring to the folded or ridged surface texture of the osteoderms. Maleev's description was one of several important 1950s publications on Mongolian ankylosaurs.

Several partial skeletons and skulls, mostly disarticulated, with tail-club elements.

## Where it lived

The holotype and most referred specimens were recovered from Cenomanian–Santonian Bayan Shireh Formation sediments in southern Mongolia during Soviet expeditions.

## Open questions

- Composite historical material and formation age remain sources of uncertainty.
