## Overview

Achelousaurus was a centrosaurine ceratopsid from Late Cretaceous Montana, named for the shape-changing Greek river deity Achelous. It had a thickened nasal boss rather than a long nasal horn, placing it among the unusual short-frilled ceratopsids close to Pachyrhinosaurus.

The genus is known from a small number of skull-dominated specimens from the Two Medicine Formation. Its anatomy is important because it may document part of an evolutionary sequence among centrosaurines in Montana, linking horned forms such as Einiosaurus with the boss-nosed condition seen in Pachyrhinosaurus.

## Anatomy & life

Achelousaurus is useful for studying horn and nasal-boss evolution in centrosaurines, one of the most visually specialised ceratopsid lineages.

Few dated occurrences are recorded, so claims about range, abundance or variation within the genus are not well supported.

## Fossil record

Several skulls and partial postcranial remains from multiple growth stages.

## Open questions

- Some referred juvenile material is less diagnostic.
- Relationships among derived centrosaurines differ among analyses.
