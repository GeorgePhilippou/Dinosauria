## Overview

Utahraptor ostrommaysorum was the largest dromaeosaurid theropod known, reaching 5–7 metres and weighing an estimated 300–500 kg. From the early Early Cretaceous Cedar Mountain Formation of Utah (~126 Ma), its discovery in 1991 — announced just as Jurassic Park was in production — provided real-world justification for the large "Velociraptors" depicted in the film.

A remarkable Utahraptor bonebed in the Doelling's Bowl area of Utah preserves multiple individuals, including young, trapped in a quicksand-like deposit together. Whether this represents pack behaviour — the animals dying while hunting together — or independent individuals drawn to a prey carcass and individually becoming mired is debated, but the multi-individual deposit is one of the most significant large theropod bonebeds from the Early Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Utahraptor was a heavily built dromaeosaurid, the family of sickle-clawed predators that includes Velociraptor and Deinonychus, from the Yellow Cat Member of the Cedar Mountain Formation in eastern Utah. It was named in 1993 from a huge foot claw and a few other bones found near Moab, and remains by far the largest member of its family.

The known bones show an animal several times heavier than Deinonychus, with a robust, deep-snouted premaxilla, thick limb bones and a curved second-toe claw whose bony core alone is over 20 centimetres long. Published lengths of five to seven metres and masses from about 280 to 500 kilograms or more are estimates scaled from relatives, not measurements of a complete skeleton.

In 2001 a sandstone block from Utahraptor Ridge, later dated to about 135 million years ago, was found to contain the remains of several Utahraptor individuals of different sizes together with an iguanodontian. Its slow preparation promises a far better picture of the animal, but most of that material is not yet published.

- The bony core of the second-toe claw is over 20 cm long, the largest known for any dromaeosaurid.
- Thick, robust limb bones and a deep premaxilla indicate a heavy animal rather than a scaled-up Velociraptor.
- A multi-individual block from Utahraptor Ridge includes juveniles, but is still being prepared.

**Feeding: Carnivore.** Serrated teeth in the premaxilla and the enlarged sickle claw indicate a predator. No bite-marked prey or gut contents are documented.

**Movement: Bipedal.** Hindlimb elements match the two-legged plan of other dromaeosaurids. Running speed and any pack hunting are not evidenced.

**Appearance: Largely reconstructed.** Utahraptor preserves no feathers or skin; the feathered look follows Microraptor and other dromaeosaurids that do.

## Fossil record

Utahraptor ostrommaysorum was named from a large pedal ungual and a small set of paratype bones from the Gaston Quarry. A sandstone block from Utahraptor Ridge containing multiple individuals has been under preparation since 2014 and remains largely unpublished.

The holotype pedal ungual; paratype bones including a premaxilla, a lacrimal, a tibia and a large manual ungual; and a growing collection of skull and postcranial elements from the Utahraptor Ridge block, which contains several individuals of different sizes.

Most of what is written about the shape of Utahraptor comes from scaling these pieces against Deinonychus and other relatives. Formal description of the block material is still awaited.

The enormous sickle claw and dromaeosaurid proportions at large size directly support its identity as the largest known dromaeosaurid.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous of the Colorado Plateau with iguanodontian ornithopods and sauropods.

## Classification

Utahraptor is a dromaeosaurid, usually placed in Dromaeosaurinae alongside Dromaeosaurus and the Mongolian Achillobator. Its exact position depends on which of its fragmentary bones are scored, and it may move once the block material is described.

## Open questions

### How big was a full-grown adult?

Estimates of 5–7 m and 280–500 kg or more rest on isolated bones. The largest elements in the block may revise both figures.

### Did it hunt in packs?

Several individuals occur together in the Utahraptor Ridge block, but they may have been trapped in a quicksand-like deposit rather than hunting together. Pack behaviour is not demonstrated.

### Which bones belong to which animal?

The holotype claw, the paratypes and the block specimens come from different sites and individuals. Until the block is fully prepared, the association of many elements is uncertain.
