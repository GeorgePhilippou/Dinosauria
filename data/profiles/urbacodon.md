## Overview

Urbacodon ('URBAC tooth') was a small theropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Uzbekistan, living approximately 95 million years ago during the Cenomanian stage. The genus and type species Urbacodon itemirensis are known only from a single left dentary (the tooth-bearing bone of the lower jaw) containing 32 small, unserrated teeth. It was a likely carnivore or insectivore, with an estimated length of around 1 metre. Its fossils were found in the Kyzylkum Desert of Central Asia.

Named by Averianov and Sues in 2007, Urbacodon is placed within Troodontidae or as a close relative of troodontids based on the distinctive morphology of its teeth and jaw. The acronym URBAC in its name stands for Uzbek–Russian–British–American–Canadian joint expeditions that conducted palaeontological fieldwork in the Kyzylkum Desert during the 1990s–2000s. As the only named dinosaur from these expeditions at the time of its description, Urbacodon contributes to the poorly known Cenomanian vertebrate fauna of Central Asia.

## Anatomy & life

The dentary of Urbacodon bears 32 teeth that are small, closely packed, unserrated, and distinctly constricted between the crown and root — characters that align it with Troodontidae or closely related coelurosaurs. Troodontid teeth are typically distinctive enough to identify the family from isolated specimens, and the dental formula and tooth morphology of Urbacodon were central to its classification.

Being known from only a single jaw element, Urbacodon represents one of the most fragmentary dinosaur genera in the formal record. While diagnostic dental features justify its description as a distinct taxon, virtually nothing can be said about its overall body size (estimated at approximately 1 metre by comparison with relatives), locomotion, or most aspects of its biology. Discovery of more complete material from the Kyzylkum would be transformative.

The Bissekty Formation is one of the few Cenomanian vertebrate-bearing units in Central Asia and has produced a diverse assemblage including multiple theropod taxa, turtles, lizards, and early eutherian mammals. Urbacodon thus contributes to a growing picture of the distinct and diverse Cenomanian fauna of Central Asia at a time when it was geographically positioned between the major Laurasian landmasses.

## Fossil record

Urbacodon itemirensis was described in 2007 by Alexander Averianov and Hans-Dieter Sues from a single left dentary found in the Bissekty Formation of Uzbekistan. The genus name is derived from the acronym URBAC, honouring the joint Uzbek–Russian–British–American–Canadian palaeontological expeditions that recovered the specimen. The species name references the Itemir locality in the Kyzylkum Desert.

Partial dentary with teeth and very limited referred elements.
