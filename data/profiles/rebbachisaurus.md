## Overview

Rebbachisaurus (meaning '[Aït] Rebbach lizard') was a large diplodocoid sauropod from the Early to Late Cretaceous of Morocco, living approximately 106–94 million years ago (Albian–Cenomanian). Named by René Lavocat in 1954, it is estimated to have been about 20 metres long and is known from dorsal vertebrae, a scapula, humerus, and sacrum from the Kem Kem and Gara Sbaa / Kem Kem Formations of the Drâa-Tafilalet region. It belonged to the family Rebbachisauridae within Diplodocoidea.

Rebbachisaurus gives its name to Rebbachisauridae, a family of Cretaceous diplodocoid sauropods with tall neural spines that may have supported a dorsal 'sail' or fat hump. The family is known from Africa, Europe, and South America, and Rebbachisaurus is significant as the namesake and a key African representative. Because the known material excludes the skull and much of the limbs, body reconstructions are largely inferential.

## Anatomy & life

The neural spines of Rebbachisaurus dorsal vertebrae are notably tall, raising the possibility that the animal carried a dorsal 'sail' of skin or a fatty hump over the back, similar to structures proposed for some other sauropods and hadrosaurs. Whether this was a sail, a hump, or simply tall muscle attachment points remains unresolved.

Rebbachisaurus is the type genus of Rebbachisauridae, a family of diplodocoid sauropods that flourished in the Cretaceous on Gondwanan landmasses and in Europe. Other members include Nigersaurus from Niger—notable for its wide, battery-like tooth arrangement—and Limaysaurus from Argentina, illustrating the wide geographic range of this family.

## Fossil record

Rebbachisaurus garasbae was named by René Lavocat in 1954 from vertebrae and limb girdle bones collected in what was then French Morocco. The genus name comes from the Aït Rebbach Berber people of the region. It was one of the first large Cretaceous sauropods described from North Africa.

Partial skeleton with articulated and disarticulated vertebrae plus scapula, humerus and ischium.

- The giant neural spine belongs to a dorsal vertebra but does not by itself establish a tall skin sail.
- Rebbachisaurus tessonei was removed to the genus Limaysaurus.
- The skull and most of the neck and limbs are unknown; body mass estimates are moderate for a sauropod, not reliably gigantic.

## Where it lived

The Kem Kem Formation is famous for preserving one of the most predator-heavy Cretaceous ecosystems known, including enormous theropods like Spinosaurus, Carcharodontosaurus, and Deltadromaeus alongside Rebbachisaurus. This concentration of large carnivores alongside Rebbachisaurus has prompted ecological debate about food availability and taphonomic biases in the deposit.

Cenomanian-age continental deposits of the Sahara renowned for diverse Cretaceous dinosaurs and the type Rebbachisaurus material.

The type is lower Upper Cretaceous Cenomanian, not Early Cretaceous.

## Open questions

- Some Kem Kem sauropod material cannot be referred securely.
- Soft-tissue profile over the neural spines is unknown.
