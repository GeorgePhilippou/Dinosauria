## Overview

Rhoetosaurus (meaning 'Rhoetan lizard', from Rhoetus, a Titan of Greek myth) was an early sauropod dinosaur from the Middle Jurassic of Queensland, Australia, living approximately 161–155 million years ago. Named by Longman in 1926, it is estimated to have exceeded 15 metres in length and is known from a partial skeleton including hind limbs, pelvis, and tail vertebrae collected near the Roma district of Queensland, from the Walloon Coal Measures / Injune Creek Group.

Rhoetosaurus is scientifically significant as the oldest and best-known sauropod from Australia, providing a rare window into the Jurassic dinosaur fauna of Gondwana's Australian landmass. Its placement within Eusauropoda but outside more derived clades makes it a basal sauropod, though the incompleteness of the skeleton limits detailed phylogenetic resolution. Australian Jurassic dinosaurs are poorly known overall, making Rhoetosaurus a crucial reference point.

## Anatomy & life

Australia's Mesozoic fossil record is patchy compared to other continents, and Jurassic dinosaurs from the region are especially rare. Rhoetosaurus fills an important gap as the country's earliest known sauropod, demonstrating that large herbivorous dinosaurs were established in Australia by the Oxfordian stage of the Jurassic.

Known elements include caudal and dorsal vertebrae, parts of the pelvis, a femur, and foot bones. The femur is robustly constructed and indicates a large, column-legged quadruped. The tail vertebrae show features typical of basal eusauropods. No skull material is known, making dietary reconstruction dependent on inference from relatives.

## Fossil record

Rhoetosaurus brownei was named by Heber Longman in 1926 from fragmentary material recovered near Roma in Queensland. The species name honours Arthur Brown on whose property the bones were found. Excavation of the site yielded bones representing portions of the hindquarters and tail, but the skull, neck, and much of the forelimb remain unknown.

Partial postcranial skeleton with about 40 vertebrae, pelvis and a well-preserved hind limb and foot.

## Where it lived

During the Jurassic, Australia formed part of the Gondwanan supercontinent and shared faunal connections with South America and Africa before fully separating. Rhoetosaurus may have close relatives among Gondwanan sauropods from South America, though establishing precise affinities requires more complete material from both regions.

Middle Jurassic fluvial and lacustrine deposits of central Queensland yielding the only known Rhoetosaurus skeleton.

## Open questions

- The skull and most of the neck and forelimb are unknown.
- Its exact position among basal sauropods varies.
