## Overview

Tylocephale ('swelling head') was a small pachycephalosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 75–72 million years ago during the Campanian stage. It reached about 1.4 metres in length and was a bipedal herbivore or omnivore. The type and only species, Tylocephale gilmorei, is known from a partial skull including the mandible, discovered in the Barun Goyot Formation of the Gobi Desert.

Tylocephale is notable for possessing one of the tallest domes among Mongolian pachycephalosaurs, which is reflected in its name. Named by Maryańska and Osmólska in 1974, it contributes to understanding the diversity of pachycephalosaurid dinosaurs in Asia during the Campanian and provides comparative data for evaluating the use of the dome in social behaviour.

## Anatomy & life

Tylocephale is distinguished by its particularly tall, narrow dome — higher relative to skull length than in most other Asian pachycephalosaurs. The known material includes the partial skull and mandible, which bear the typical features of Pachycephalosauridae: a solid dome of thickened bone over the braincase and small, leaf-shaped teeth. The function of the dome — whether for intraspecific combat, species recognition, or display — remains debated in the literature.

## Fossil record

Tylocephale gilmorei was named in 1974 by Teresa Maryańska and Halszka Osmólska from material collected during the Polish–Mongolian Palaeontological Expeditions of the 1960s–70s. The generic name means 'swelling head', reflecting the high, strongly domed skull. The species honours American palaeontologist Charles W. Gilmore.

Single partial skull and lower jaw.

## Where it lived

The Barun Goyot Formation represents a semi-arid dune and interdune environment in Late Cretaceous central Asia. Tylocephale would have been a small to medium-sized herbivore or omnivore within this ecosystem, potentially consuming seeds, insects, and plant material. Its modest size of around 1.4 metres suggests it occupied a different niche from larger contemporaneous herbivores such as protoceratopsids.

The Gobi Desert formation from which Tylocephale gilmorei was recovered.

## Classification

Tylocephale is one of several pachycephalosaurid genera known from the Campanian of Mongolia, alongside Prenocephale, Homalocephale, and Goyocephale. Phylogenetic analyses have placed it in varying positions within Pachycephalosauridae, and its exact relationships to these contemporaries are not fully resolved. Together, these taxa document considerable diversity in head-shape among Asian pachycephalosaurs.

## Open questions

- Body size, diet and dome function remain uncertain.
