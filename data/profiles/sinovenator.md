## Overview

Sinovenator (meaning 'Chinese hunter') was a small troodontid theropod from the Early Cretaceous of Liaoning Province, China, living approximately 127–121 million years ago. Estimated at about 1 metre in length, it is one of the earliest and most basal known troodontids, and partial skeletons and skulls have been recovered from the Yixian Formation. Its discovery shed important light on the origins of the Troodontidae and their relationships with other paravian theropods.

Named by Xu, Norell, Wang, Makovicky, and Wu in 2002, Sinovenator changii is significant because it preserves a combination of features shared with dromaeosaurids and birds alongside distinctly troodontid characteristics. It documents that troodontids were already present and diversifying in northeastern China during the Early Cretaceous, contemporaneous with some of the earliest birds. The presence of Sinovenatorinae as a subfamily designation reflects its basal position within the troodontid radiation.

## Anatomy & life

Sinovenator is one of the most primitive troodontids known. It retains several plesiomorphic (ancestral) features shared with other paravians, including relatively simple teeth, that contrast with the highly derived dental batteries of later troodontids like Troodon. Its basal position helps anchor the troodontid family tree and trace the acquisition of distinctive troodontid features through time.

Sinovenator lived alongside a remarkable diversity of feathered dinosaurs and early birds in the Yixian Formation ecosystem. Contemporaries included Sinornithosaurus, Sinocalliopteryx, Microraptor, and numerous enantiornithine birds. This dense assemblage makes the Jehol Biota one of the best windows into paravian diversification during the Early Cretaceous.

## Fossil record

Sinovenator changii was described in 2002 by Xu Xing, Mark Norell, Wang Xiaolin, Peter Makovicky, and Wu Xichuan from specimens collected in Liaoning Province. The species name honours Chang Meemann, a prominent Chinese palaeontologist. The holotype consists of partial skull and postcranial elements, with additional referred material providing further anatomical information.

Several partial skeletons, including cranial and postcranial elements.

## Where it lived

The holotype partial skeleton of Sinovenator was recovered from Yixian Formation deposits in Liaoning, part of the celebrated Jehol Biota.

## Classification

As a troodontid, Sinovenator was closely related to birds within the broader Paraves clade. Its skull shows features broadly intermediate between more basal coelurosaurs and avians, contributing to ongoing debate about which paravian lineage is most closely related to Aves. The presence of early troodontids alongside early birds in the Jehol Biota underscores the close ecological and phylogenetic proximity of these groups.
