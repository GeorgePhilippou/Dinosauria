## Overview

Protohadros (meaning 'first hadrosaur') was a large herbivorous ornithopod from the Late Cretaceous of Texas, USA, living approximately 100–94 million years ago (Cenomanian stage). Named by Jason Head in 1998, it is estimated at around 6 metres in length and is known from partial skull and postcranial remains collected from the Lewisville/Woodbine Formation. It represents one of the earliest and most primitive known members of Hadrosauridae.

Protohadros is scientifically significant as one of the oldest hadrosaurs known from North America, suggesting that the group may have originated in North America or undergone significant diversification there during the Cenomanian. Its skull shares primitive features with iguanodontids alongside derived hadrosaur features, making it important for understanding hadrosaur origins. Its Cenomanian age also makes it one of the oldest known hadrosaurids globally.

## Anatomy & life

The Cenomanian age of Protohadros, combined with its North American provenance, has been used to support hypotheses that hadrosaurs may have diversified in North America before migrating to Asia—the reverse of earlier models that favoured an Asian origin. This biogeographic interpretation remains debated.

## Fossil record

Protohadros byrdi was described by Jason Head in 1998 from fragmentary cranial and postcranial material from Denton County, Texas. It was named in recognition of its basal position within or close to the base of Hadrosauridae, and the species name honours the landowner who facilitated excavation.

Partial skull and jaw with sparse referred postcranial remains.

## Where it lived

The Woodbine Formation was deposited in a coastal to near-shore marine environment during the Cenomanian transgression of the Western Interior Seaway. Protohadros likely inhabited adjacent coastal floodplains and river systems, browsing on vegetation along the margins of the advancing seaway.

Cenomanian-age deposits of northern Texas yielding the only known Protohadros material.

## Classification

Head's original analysis placed Protohadros as one of the most basal hadrosaurs, predating the hadrosaurid radiation. Subsequent phylogenetic studies have broadly confirmed a very basal hadrosauroid or hadrosaurid position, though the precise placement relative to other primitive hadrosaurs such as Eotrachodon and Telmatosaurus continues to be refined.

## Open questions
