## Overview

Jingshanosaurus (meaning 'Jingshan lizard') was a large herbivorous prosauropod dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of China, living approximately 201–199 million years ago during the Hettangian stage. Known from a complete skeleton and skull from the Lufeng Formation of Yunnan Province, it reached an estimated 5 metres in length. It was named by Zhang Yihong and Yang Zhongjian in 1995. Jingshanosaurus is considered one of the last of the prosauropod lineage.

Jingshanosaurus is placed within Prosauropoda > Plateosauria, the group of large-bodied early sauropodomorphs that flourished in the Late Triassic and Early Jurassic before being replaced by true sauropods. The Lufeng Formation is one of the most important Early Jurassic dinosaur assemblages in the world, also yielding Lufengosaurus and other prosauropods. The complete nature of the Jingshanosaurus specimen makes it one of the most informative prosauropods for understanding the anatomy of the group at the transition into the Jurassic.

## Anatomy & life

Jingshanosaurus possessed a relatively large skull, leaf-shaped teeth, and robust limbs typical of prosauropods. At 5 metres it was a substantial animal for the group. Its Hettangian age (approximately 200 million years ago) makes it one of the youngest-known prosauropods, as true sauropods were already diversifying at this time, suggesting brief temporal overlap between prosauropods and early sauropods.

As one of the last surviving and largest prosauropods, Jingshanosaurus helps define the endpoint of the prosauropod lineage and the transition to sauropod dominance. Its complete skeleton provides a baseline for anatomical comparison with other plateosaurian prosauropods and early sauropods, informing models of how sauropodomorph body plans evolved.

## Fossil record

Jingshanosaurus xinwaensis was described in 1995 by Zhang Yihong and Yang Zhongjian from a complete skeleton collected from the Lufeng Formation of Yunnan. The genus name refers to Jingshan (Golden Hill), the discovery locality, and the species name references the Xinwa area. It remains one of the most complete Chinese prosauropods.

Skull and substantial partial postcranial skeleton.

## Where it lived

The Lufeng Formation records a semi-arid to sub-humid lake and river environment in Early Jurassic Yunnan, characterised by reddish mudstones. Jingshanosaurus shared this environment with Lufengosaurus and other sauropodomorphs, as well as early theropods and tritylodontid synapsids, providing a picture of Early Jurassic terrestrial ecosystems in South China.

A world-class Early Jurassic dinosaur site that has produced multiple prosauropod genera; the complete Jingshanosaurus skeleton was collected here.

## Open questions

- The Lower Lufeng numerical age remains debated.
- Postcranial anatomy still lacks a complete modern redescription.
