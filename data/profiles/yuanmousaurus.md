## Overview

Yuanmousaurus ('Yuanmou lizard') was a large eusauropod dinosaur from the Middle Jurassic of Yunnan Province, China, living approximately 180–159 million years ago during the Toarcian to Callovian stages. It reached an estimated 17 metres in length, making it one of the largest Middle Jurassic sauropods known from Asia. The type and only species, Yuanmousaurus jiangyiensis, was described from an incomplete skeleton collected near Yuanmou County.

Named by Lü and colleagues in 2006, Yuanmousaurus is placed within Eusauropoda, the vast majority of sauropods excluding the most primitive genera. Yunnan Province, where it was found, has long been recognised as a hotspot for Jurassic sauropod diversity, and Yuanmousaurus adds to the record of large Middle Jurassic sauropods from East Asia. Its size suggests that by the Middle Jurassic, Chinese sauropods had already achieved the enormous body dimensions characteristic of the group.

## Anatomy & life

The available skeleton of Yuanmousaurus, while incomplete, indicates an animal of very large size for a Middle Jurassic taxon, with an estimated length of around 17 metres. Its vertebrae show the typical eusauropod features of well-developed pleurocoels (hollows) and complex articulating surfaces, adaptations for lightening the vertebral column while maintaining structural integrity in very large animals. The limb proportions are consistent with a graviportal quadruped.

Yunnan Province has yielded a succession of sauropodomorphs spanning the Early to Middle Jurassic, including Lufengosaurus, Yimenosaurus, and various eusauropods. Yuanmousaurus fits into the broader pattern of sauropod gigantism developing through the Middle Jurassic in East Asia, a trend that would culminate in the enormous mamenchisaurids such as Mamenchisaurus and Omeisaurus known from the slightly later Upper Shaximiao Formation of Sichuan.

Yuanmousaurus is known from an incomplete skeleton lacking the skull, which limits confident determination of its diet and sensory capabilities. Its phylogenetic placement within Eusauropoda is broadly agreed upon, but more precise relationships to other Asian Middle Jurassic sauropods await either better material or a comprehensive phylogenetic analysis incorporating the full suite of Asian taxa from this interval.

## Fossil record

Yuanmousaurus jiangyiensis was described in 2006 by Lü Junchang, Li Tiangui, Ji Shu'an, Wang Zhenghui, Zhang Xingliao, and Dong Zhiming from an incomplete skeleton recovered near Yuanmou County, Yunnan Province. The genus name references Yuanmou, while the species name jiangyiensis refers to the locality Jiangyi. The specimen includes vertebrae, limb elements, and girdle elements sufficient for a preliminary phylogenetic assessment.

One incomplete postcranial skeleton without skull and with little neck.

## Where it lived

Middle Jurassic deposits of Yunnan Province from which Yuanmousaurus jiangyiensis was recovered.
