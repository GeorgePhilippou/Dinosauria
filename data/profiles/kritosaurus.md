## Overview

Kritosaurus (often translated as 'noble lizard' though more accurately 'separated lizard') was a large herbivorous hadrosaurine dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of North America, living approximately 74.5–66 million years ago. Known from the Kirtland and Fruitland formations of New Mexico and possibly equivalent deposits in Alberta and Texas, it reached an estimated 8 metres in length. It possessed a prominent nasal arch giving it a 'Roman nose' profile. It was named by Barnum Brown in 1910.

Kritosaurus is placed within Hadrosaurinae > Kritosaurini, a tribe that also includes Gryposaurus. The relationship between Kritosaurus and Gryposaurus has been debated, with some researchers considering them synonymous; the exact diagnostic features separating the genera remain contested. Kritosaurus species have also been reported from Argentina, though the South American material may represent a distinct related genus. The PBDB records 25 occurrences from North America, making it one of the better-documented Maastrichtian hadrosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

Kritosaurus is characterised by a prominently arched nasal region producing a distinctive 'Roman nose' or hump profile on the snout. This nasal arch may have served display or species-recognition functions. Like other hadrosaurines, it lacked a hollow cranial crest but had a sophisticated dental battery of hundreds of teeth for processing tough vegetation. It reached approximately 8 metres in length.

## Fossil record

Kritosaurus navajovius was named in 1910 by Barnum Brown from material collected in New Mexico. The genus name is often given as 'noble lizard' in popular literature, but the original Greek more accurately translates as 'separated lizard', in reference to the separated (arched) nasal bones. The species name references the Navajo people of the American Southwest.

Incomplete diagnostic skull with limited referred postcranial material.

## Where it lived

Kritosaurus inhabited the Late Campanian to Maastrichtian floodplains, river systems, and coastal areas of the southern Laramidian landmass. It coexisted with ceratopsids, ankylosaurs, and tyrannosaurids in a diverse dinosaurian community. The Wikipedia entry notes it lived from approximately 74.5–66 million years ago, placing it among the latest-surviving hadrosaurines.

The primary source of Kritosaurus navajovius material, from Late Campanian fluvial and coastal plain deposits of the San Juan Basin.

## Classification

The relationship between Kritosaurus and the very similar Gryposaurus is a long-running debate. Some researchers have considered them synonymous (with Kritosaurus having priority), while others maintain them as distinct genera. Material referred to Kritosaurus from Argentina has been separated by some workers into a related taxon, and this South American material complicates biogeographic reconstructions.

## Open questions

- The type skull is incomplete and postcranial referrals are limited.
- Species limits among North American kritosaurins continue to be revised.
