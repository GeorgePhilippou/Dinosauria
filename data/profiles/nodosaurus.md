## Overview

Nodosaurus (meaning 'node lizard') was a nodosaurid ankylosaur from the Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian, approximately 100–98 million years ago) of what is now Wyoming, USA. Known remains come from the Frontier Formation and possibly beds in Alberta. At about 5 metres in length it was a medium-sized ankylosaur, a quadrupedal herbivore covered in protective bony nodules and plates embedded in the skin.

Described by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1889, Nodosaurus is the type genus of Nodosauridae, the family of ankylosaurs that lack the tail club characteristic of ankylosaurids. Despite giving its name to the family, Nodosaurus is known from relatively fragmentary postcranial material and has historically been difficult to diagnose precisely. It is significant as one of the earliest-named North American ankylosaurs and as the anchor for a major armoured dinosaur lineage.

## Anatomy & life

Nodosaurids are distinguished from ankylosaurids by lacking a bony tail club and by often possessing prominent shoulder spines. The patterned nodular osteoderms of Nodosaurus, from which the genus takes its name, were arranged in rows across the back and flanks, providing passive defence against the large theropods that shared its Cenomanian ecosystem.

The Frontier Formation in which Nodosaurus was found represents marginal marine and coastal plain environments in the Western Interior Seaway region. Nodosaurus was among the herbivorous dinosaurs of this Cenomanian landscape, which also hosted early hadrosaurs and various sauropods, alongside large marine reptiles offshore.

## Fossil record

Nodosaurus textilis was named by Othniel Charles Marsh in 1889, based on material from Wyoming. The genus name refers to the distinctive bony nodes covering its body, while the species name textilis ('woven') alludes to the patterned arrangement of those osteoderms. Marsh recognised it as a heavily armoured herbivore, though the material available to him was fragmentary.

Known material consists largely of postcranial elements including vertebrae, ribs and osteoderms; skull material is very limited or absent. This incompleteness has made Nodosaurus difficult to diagnose rigorously, and some researchers consider it a nomen dubium. Nevertheless, it retains nomenclatural priority as the type genus of Nodosauridae and is conventionally retained in the literature.

Single partial postcranial skeleton with osteoderms; skull absent.

- The holotype preserves vertebrae, girdle and limb elements and dermal armour.
- No skull or tail club is known; head shape and much of the armour arrangement are reconstructed from relatives.
- It was a quadrupedal herbivore by nodosaurid anatomy, but precise size and feeding height are estimates.

## Where it lived

Cenomanian marine and marginal-marine deposits of Wyoming where Nodosaurus holotype material was found by Marsh.

The Frontier Formation type horizon is Cenomanian; an Early Cretaceous 110-100 Ma range is incorrect.
