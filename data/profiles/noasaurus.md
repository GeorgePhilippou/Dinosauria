## Overview

Noasaurus (meaning 'northwestern Argentina lizard', from the indigenous name Noa for that region) was a small ceratosaurian theropod from the Late Cretaceous (approximately 72–66 million years ago) of Salta Province, Argentina. It is known from skull fragments and some postcranial remains recovered from the Lecho Formation. Estimated at up to about 3 metres in length, it was a carnivore that walked on two legs.

Named by Bonaparte and Powell in 1980, Noasaurus is the type genus of Noasauridae, a family of small-bodied abelisauroid ceratosaurs with a Southern Hemisphere distribution. Its sickle-like foot claw, initially compared to that of dromaeosaurid raptors, was later reinterpreted as a more generalised feature. Noasaurids — including the better-known Masiakasaurus from Madagascar — show that small ceratosaurs filled a diversity of ecological niches in the Cretaceous of Gondwana.

## Anatomy & life

The holotype includes a pedal ungual (foot claw) that is strongly curved, which Bonaparte and Powell initially compared to the hyperextensible killing claws of dromaeosaurids. Later studies disputed this homology, interpreting the morphology as a different functional adaptation, possibly for prey restraint rather than slashing. This debate contributed to broader discussions of convergent evolution among theropods.

## Fossil record

Noasaurus leali was described by José Bonaparte and Jaime Powell in 1980 from fragmentary remains found in the Lecho Formation of Salta Province, Argentina. The genus name uses the indigenous 'Noa' for the north-western Argentine region (Noroeste Argentino), while the species honours the discoverer Leal.

Fragmentary skull, vertebral and limb elements from the holotype.

## Where it lived

The Lecho Formation in Salta represents a Late Maastrichtian fluvial to aeolian environment. Noasaurus shared this landscape with the titanosaur Saltasaurus and the sauropod Barrosasaurus, among others, indicating a diverse dinosaur fauna in north-western Argentina at the close of the Cretaceous.

Maastrichtian fluvial red-bed deposits in north-western Argentina that yielded the holotype Noasaurus material.

## Classification

Noasaurus is the founding member of Noasauridae, a clade of small to medium abelisauroids known mainly from Gondwana. Well-preserved noasaurids such as Masiakasaurus (Madagascar) have shown that some members had procumbent front teeth suggesting specialised diets including fish. How Noasaurus fits within this diversity is limited by its fragmentary preservation.

## Open questions

- Feeding specialisation is disputed.
- Many aspects of the skeleton are unknown.
