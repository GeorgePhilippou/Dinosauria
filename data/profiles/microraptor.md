## Overview

Microraptor was a small feathered dromaeosaurid from the Jiufotang Formation of Liaoning, known from hundreds of articulated specimens, especially M. gui; species limits within the genus remain debated.

Microraptor gui was the size of a crow but carried flight feathers on all four limbs — both arms and legs — making it the strongest evidence that the evolution of flight in dinosaurs may have passed through a four-winged stage. Iridescent black plumage has been reconstructed from melanosomes preserved in its feathers, making it one of the few dinosaurs whose colour can be described with confidence.

Hundreds of Microraptor specimens are known from the Yixian Formation of Liaoning, China (~125–120 Ma), making it among the most studied small dinosaurs. Stomach contents preserved in several specimens include fish, lizards and the remains of a small bird, revealing an opportunistic diet despite its tiny size.

## Anatomy & life

Microraptor was a small dromaeosaurid, about the size of a crow, with a long bony tail ending in a fan of feathers. Its most remarkable feature is a set of long, asymmetrical flight feathers on the hindlimbs as well as the arms, producing a four-winged layout with no living equivalent.

The plumage is directly preserved in many specimens. Analysis of fossil melanosomes in one skeleton indicates that the feathers were predominantly black with an iridescent sheen, comparable to a modern grackle or crow. Gut contents in different individuals include a bird, a mammal, a lizard and a fish, so its diet was clearly varied.

How Microraptor used its wings is still debated. Wind-tunnel and modelling studies agree that it could glide or parachute effectively, but whether it could launch from the ground or flap in powered flight remains an open question.

- Long flight feathers on the legs as well as the arms are preserved on many specimens, giving a four-winged body plan.
- Melanosome evidence indicates predominantly black, iridescent plumage in at least one individual.
- Preserved stomach contents from separate specimens document prey ranging from birds and mammals to lizards and fish.

**Feeding: Small-animal carnivore.** Different specimens preserve a bird, a mammal, a lizard and a fish in the gut region, showing an opportunistic diet.

**Movement: Gliding or flying.** Asymmetrical flight feathers and aerodynamic modelling support gliding at least; powered flight and take-off ability are inferred, not observed.

**Appearance: Feathers and colour preserved.** Plumage outline is directly preserved; the black iridescent colour comes from melanosome analysis of a single individual and may not represent every animal.

## Fossil record

Microraptor is one of the most abundantly sampled non-avian dinosaurs. Articulated, feather-bearing skeletons on limestone slabs preserve the plumage outline, melanosomes and stomach contents alongside the bones.

Complete skulls, vertebral columns, girdles and limbs from many individuals, together with the outlines of arm, leg and tail feathers, fossil melanosomes and, in several specimens, the remains of prey in the gut.

Jehol fossils are crushed flat on slabs, so three-dimensional skull and joint anatomy is harder to study than in uncrushed material. Many specimens were collected commercially and lack precise locality data.

Feather impressions and stomach contents provide direct evidence for plumage, gliding/flying ability and varied diet.

- Long flight feathers on arms and legs form a four-winged aerodynamic configuration.
- Aerodynamic studies support gliding or some aerial capacity, while launch and powered-flight performance remain debated.
- Gut contents document a varied carnivorous diet including birds, mammals, lizards and fish in different specimens.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous Jehol lake-and-forest ecosystem.

The main Jiufotang sample is Aptian, close to 120 Ma; species and localities should not be merged into a broader generic range without review.

## Classification

Microraptor is a dromaeosaurid within Microraptorinae, a group of small, often four-winged dromaeosaurs from the Early Cretaceous of China. Three species have been named, M. zhaoianus, M. gui and M. hanqingi, but many workers regard them as a single variable species.

## Open questions

### Could it fly under its own power?

Gliding is well supported. Whether the shoulder allowed a full flight stroke and whether it could launch from the ground remain contested.

### How many species are there?

Differences between M. zhaoianus, M. gui and M. hanqingi may reflect growth, individual variation or preservation rather than separate species.

### Was every Microraptor black?

The iridescent black colour is based on melanosomes from one specimen. Other individuals, sexes or ages might have differed.
