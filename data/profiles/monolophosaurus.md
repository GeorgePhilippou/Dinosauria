## Overview

Monolophosaurus was a large carnivorous theropod dinosaur that lived during the Middle Jurassic, approximately 170–160 million years ago, in what is now Xinjiang Province, northwestern China. It is estimated to have reached around 5.7 metres in length and is distinguished by a single prominent bony crest running along the top of its skull from the snout to the back of the head, a feature from which its name — 'single-crested lizard' — derives. The type species is Monolophosaurus jiangi.

Named in 1993 by Zhao Xijin and Philip Currie, Monolophosaurus is one of the better-known Middle Jurassic theropods from China. Its classification has been debated, with it placed in various positions within Tetanurae; it has been considered a tetanuran of uncertain placement, and some analyses have grouped it within Sinraptoridae or close to the base of Tetanurae. The single nasal crest, unusual in being a single midline structure rather than a paired ridge, was likely used for display or species recognition.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Monolophosaurus bears a large, prominent bony crest that extends as a single midline ridge from just above the nostrils to the posterior margin of the skull. This crest involved the nasal and lacrimal bones and was pneumatised (hollow), connected to the nasal passages. Possible functions include species recognition, sexual display, or thermoregulation, though which was primary cannot be determined from skeletal material alone.

Monolophosaurus is one of the better-known Middle Jurassic theropods from Asia, a time interval when theropod diversity and body plans were diversifying rapidly. Its co-occurrence with other theropods and sauropods in the Junggar Basin provides important data on the composition of Middle Jurassic ecosystems in Central Asia. The region is known for preserving an important but still relatively poorly sampled Middle Jurassic fauna.

## Fossil record

Monolophosaurus jiangi was described in 1993 by Zhao Xijin and Philip Currie from a nearly complete skull and partial skeleton from the Wucaiwan Formation of the Junggar Basin, Xinjiang. The genus name means 'single-crested lizard', referring to the distinctive single midline crest, while the species name jiangi honours Jiang Bingyin, the official who facilitated the Sino-Canadian palaeontological expeditions that led to its discovery.

Single partial skeleton with a nearly complete skull and substantial axial and appendicular remains.

## Where it lived

A nearly complete skull and partial postcranial skeleton of Monolophosaurus jiangi were recovered from Middle Jurassic continental deposits in the Junggar Basin.

## Classification

The classification of Monolophosaurus within Tetanurae has been debated since its description. It has been placed outside or at the base of Carnosauria by various analyses, and its exact relationship to sinraptorids and allosaurids is uncertain. Some phylogenies recover it as a basal tetanuran below the carnosaur–coelurosaur split. Ongoing work on Middle Jurassic theropod phylogenetics may clarify its position.

## Open questions

- Only one individual is securely known.
- Soft-tissue appearance and crest function are unknown.
