## Overview

Lycorhinus was a small heterodontosaurid ornithischian dinosaur that lived during the Early Jurassic (Sinemurian), approximately 196–190 million years ago, in what is now the Upper Elliot Formation of South Africa. It is known from very fragmentary material — ultimately reduced over time to a single tooth and the impression of a jaw — making detailed reconstructions uncertain. The type species is Lycorhinus angustidens, named in 1924 by Sidney Haughton.

The genus name, derived from Greek words for 'wolf' and 'nose', reflects the canine-like teeth that are characteristic of heterodontosaurids — a family of early ornithischians notable for possessing differentiated teeth including tusk-like caniniforms. The limited and degraded material of Lycorhinus has generated considerable taxonomic complexity, with several other heterodontosaurid taxa at various times considered synonyms or species of Lycorhinus, including Heterodontosaurus, Abrictosaurus, and Lanasaurus.

## Anatomy & life

Like other heterodontosaurids, Lycorhinus possessed differentiated teeth — an unusual feature among ornithischians. These included tusk-like caniniforms alongside more typical herbivore teeth, giving the skull a heterodont (mixed tooth type) appearance from which the family takes its name. The function of the tusks has been debated, with suggestions including display, combat, or feeding specialisation.

Lycorhinus co-existed with other Early Jurassic dinosaurs in the Elliot Formation of southern Africa, including the prosauropod Massospondylus and the basal ornithischian Lesothosaurus. Together these taxa document the early radiation of dinosaurs following the end-Triassic extinction, a period of rapid dinosaur diversification across the newly reorganised Pangaean ecosystems.

## Fossil record

Lycorhinus angustidens was named in 1924 by Sidney H. Haughton based on a partial jaw found in the Upper Elliot Formation of South Africa. Haughton initially interpreted it as a cynodont (mammal-like reptile) rather than a dinosaur, and its reclassification as an ornithischian dinosaur came later. The genus name means 'wolf nose', referring to the pronounced canine-like teeth, while angustidens means 'narrow-toothed'.

## Classification

The extreme fragmentation and subsequent degradation of the Lycorhinus material has generated substantial taxonomic confusion. At various times, the type species of Heterodontosaurus, Abrictosaurus, and Lanasaurus have been treated as species of Lycorhinus or its synonyms. The most recent comprehensive review retained Lycorhinus angustidens as valid and accepted the synonymy of Lanasaurus scalpridens with it, while treating the other taxa as distinct.

## Open questions

- Referral of the partial skull and Lanasaurus material remains disputed.
- The type dentary is incomplete and mostly an impression.
- Specific diet breadth is unknown.
