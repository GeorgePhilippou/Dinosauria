## Overview

Chindesaurus was one of the earliest dinosaurs, a small to medium saurischian from the Late Triassic of the American Southwest, approximately 227–206 million years ago (Norian), found in the Chinle Formation of Arizona and New Mexico. Reaching about 4 metres in length, it had long legs and a whip-like tail. No skull material is known, so its head shape and diet are inferred from comparisons with similar early dinosaurs, though it is generally considered a carnivore.

Named by Long and Murry in 1995, Chindesaurus bryansmalli has been placed within Herrerasauridae by some researchers, making it one of the earliest dinosaur lineages. Its classification is debated: others consider it an early saurischian of uncertain family placement. Located in the Chinle Formation—the same unit that preserves the famous Petrified Forest fauna—Chindesaurus offers a window into the earliest dinosaur communities of Late Triassic North America.

## Anatomy & life

Because no skull material of Chindesaurus has been found, its head morphology and diet remain speculative and are reconstructed by analogy with better-known herrerasaurids. The known material—partial limb bones and vertebrae—indicates long, gracile hindlimbs suggestive of bipedal locomotion and cursorial ability. A whip-like tail is inferred from the proportions of the recovered vertebrae.

The Chinle Formation fauna of the Late Triassic included a diverse assemblage of aetosaurs, phytosaurs, early crocodylomorphs, rauisuchids, and early dinosaurs. Chindesaurus would have been one of the earliest dinosaurian predators in North America, existing in an ecosystem still dominated by non-dinosaurian archosaurs. The Triassic-Jurassic extinction event shortly after would clear the way for dinosaurian dominance.

## Fossil record

Chindesaurus bryansmalli was described in 1995 by John Long and Phillip Murry from partial hindlimb and vertebral material recovered from the Chinle Formation of the Petrified Forest region of Arizona. The genus name refers to Chinde Point in the Petrified Forest, and the species name honours Bryan Small, who collected the original specimens. Additional material from New Mexico has been tentatively referred to the genus.

One incomplete postcranial skeleton, with uncertain additional referrals.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic fluvial and lacustrine formation of the Colorado Plateau, primary source of Chindesaurus material.

## Classification

Chindesaurus has been placed in Herrerasauridae alongside Herrerasaurus and Staurikosaurus, or alternatively treated as an early saurischian of uncertain affinities. Herrerasauridae itself has a contested position: some analyses place it as the earliest-diverging theropods, others as early saurischians outside both Theropoda and Sauropodomorpha. This reflects the broader difficulty of resolving the earliest branching events among dinosaurs.

## Open questions

- Its position as a herrerasaurid or early theropod differs among analyses.
- Several referred specimens may not belong to the genus.
