## Overview

Harpymimus (meaning 'harpy mimic') was an omnivorous theropod from the Early Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 110–100 million years ago. Known from a skull and partial skeleton recovered from the Khuren Dukh Formation of Dornogov Province, it reached an estimated length of about 2 metres. Notably, Harpymimus possessed small, blunt teeth in its lower jaw, a feature that distinguishes it from most later ornithomimosaurs, which were toothless.

Harpymimus is classified within Deinocheiridae, a family of large ornithomimosaurs, making it a relatively early member of this lineage. Its retention of teeth is considered a primitive feature among ornithomimosaurs and provides evidence for the evolutionary transition from toothed ancestors to the fully toothless condition seen in later ornithomimids. It was described by Barsbold and Perle in 1984.

## Anatomy & life

Unlike most ornithomimosaurs, Harpymimus retained small, blunt teeth in its dentary (lower jaw), whereas the premaxilla and maxilla were already edentulous (toothless). This mosaic condition is considered primitive and supports the idea that ornithomimosaurs evolved toothlessness gradually. The diet was likely omnivorous, combining small prey, insects, and plant material.

Harpymimus is important for understanding the early evolution of ornithomimosaurs, particularly the transition from toothed to toothless forms. Its placement within Deinocheiridae, alongside the enormous Deinocheirus, suggests the deinocheirid lineage was ancient and morphologically diverse. Its primitive dentition anchors the base of ornithomimosaur tooth-loss evolution.

## Fossil record

Harpymimus okladnikovi was described in 1984 by Rinchen Barsbold and Altangerel Perle from material collected in Mongolia. The genus name references the harpies of Greek mythology, reflecting the bird-like appearance of ornithomimosaurs. The species honours archaeologist Aleksei Okladnikov. Its skull and partial skeleton made it one of the better-preserved early ornithomimosaurs at the time.

Single partial skeleton with skull, jaws, vertebrae and limbs.

- The holotype is incomplete but associated and includes much of the skeleton.
- Small teeth are restricted to the lower jaw.
- It was bipedal; its exact diet cannot be reduced confidently to herbivory, carnivory or omnivory.

## Where it lived

The Khuren Dukh Formation of Mongolia records terrestrial environments of the Albian stage, approximately 110–100 million years ago. Harpymimus would have inhabited these environments alongside other early Cretaceous theropods and herbivorous dinosaurs, in an ecosystem predating the great diversity of the later Mongolian Late Cretaceous faunas.

The sole known source of Harpymimus material, an Albian-age deposit preserving early ornithomimosaur diversity.

The Albian range reflects the Khuren Dukh Formation; older broad Early Cretaceous ranges lack equivalent resolution.

## Open questions

- Only one incomplete individual is known.
- Diet and exact position among early ornithomimosaurs remain uncertain.
