## Overview

Patagotitan (meaning 'titan from Patagonia') was an enormous titanosaur sauropod from the Early Cretaceous (Albian, approximately 101 million years ago) of Chubut Province, Argentina. Fossils come from the Cerro Barcino Formation and were found at La Flecha Ranch. At approximately 37.5 metres in length and an estimated mass of around 57,000 kg (though mass estimates vary), Patagotitan mayorum is considered one of the largest animals ever to have walked on land, rivalled only by a handful of other giant titanosaurs.

Described by Carballido, Pol, Otero, Cerda, Salgado, Garrido, Ramezani, Cuneo and Krause in 2017, Patagotitan is scientifically exceptional because it is known from multiple specimens of at least six individuals, making size estimates far more reliable than for many other giant dinosaurs. The femur alone measured 2.38 metres. PBDB places it within Eutitanosauria. Its discovery at La Flecha Ranch, Patagonia, began in 2010 when a ranch worker spotted a bone protruding from the ground.

## Anatomy & life

With a femur 2.38 metres long, Patagotitan is one of the most robustly documented giant dinosaurs. Mass estimates based on different methods range from around 50 to 70 tonnes. At 37.5 metres in length and almost twice the height of Diplodocus, it dwarfs most other known dinosaurs. The NHM notes that multiple specimens make size estimates for Patagotitan more reliable than for competitors such as Argentinosaurus, known only from fragmentary remains.

Patagotitan belongs to Eutitanosauria, the derived clade within Titanosauria that includes both the largest and some of the smallest titanosaurs. The evolution of extreme body size in titanosaurs is likely linked to high-fibre plant diets, efficient gut fermentation and rapid growth rates documented through bone histology. Patagotitan represents the upper end of this size gradient.

## Fossil record

The first Patagotitan bone was noticed in 2010 by a ranch worker at La Flecha Ranch, Chubut. Scientific excavations in 2012, 2013 and 2015 recovered hundreds of bones from at least six individuals buried in three separate flood events. The species was formally named mayorum in 2017 to honour the Mayo family, owners of La Flecha Ranch. The NHM notes the 2017 authors are Carballido and a large team of colleagues.

Partial postcranial skeletons from at least six individuals, collectively preserving most body regions except the skull.

## Where it lived

The Cerro Barcino Formation represents an Early Cretaceous fluvial environment in Patagonia. The burial of multiple Patagotitan individuals in three discrete flood events suggests either mass death events or aggregated carcass transport. The site offers important taphonomic data on how large sauropod remains accumulate. Casts of Patagotitan are now displayed in the Natural History Museum of London and the American Museum of Natural History.

Early Cretaceous (Albian) continental deposits of Chubut Province where the remains of at least six Patagotitan individuals were excavated across three flood events.

## Open questions

- Skull and much of the neck are unknown.
- Mass and total length estimates remain method-sensitive.
