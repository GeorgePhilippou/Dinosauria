## Overview

Aragosaurus ischiadicus was a large sauropod dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of Spain, living approximately 130–143 million years ago during the Kimmeridgian to Tithonian stages. Estimated at up to 18 metres in length, it is known from a partial skeleton found in the Villar del Arzobispo Formation near the town of Galve in the Teruel province of Aragón. It was one of the first significant sauropods described from Spain and gives its name to the autonomous region of Aragón.

Described by Sanz, Buscalioni, Casanovas, and Sanafé in 1987, Aragosaurus is classified as a macronarian eusauropod, placing it within the lineage that ultimately gave rise to the giant titanosaurs of the Cretaceous. Its assignment to Eusauropoda reflects its relatively plesiomorphic anatomy compared to more derived titanosauriforms, and its Spanish provenance makes it a key reference taxon for the European Jurassic–Cretaceous sauropod record.

## Anatomy & life

The known material of Aragosaurus includes vertebrae, limb bones, and pelvic elements. The vertebrae show the relatively simple, non-pneumatised construction of a macronarian eusauropod without the more derived cavernous internal structure of titanosauriforms. Limb bone proportions suggest a large, robustly built animal. The overall anatomy places Aragosaurus as a relatively basal macronarian, more primitive than the later Brachiosaurus or titanosaurs.

Aragosaurus was among the first large dinosaurs formally described from Spain and helped establish the Iberian Peninsula as an important region for European Jurassic–Cretaceous dinosaur research. Its position within Macronaria as a relatively primitive member places it close to the evolutionary grade that gave rise to Titanosauriformes, illuminating the anatomy of ancestral large sauropods.

## Fossil record

Aragosaurus ischiadicus was described in 1987 by Jose Luis Sanz and colleagues from material collected near Galve, Teruel, in the Aragón region of Spain. The generic name honours Aragón, the autonomous community where the fossils were found. The species name refers to the ischium, one of the pelvic bones preserved in the specimen. Galve has since become a significant dinosaur locality yielding multiple Early Cretaceous taxa.

One associated incomplete postcranial skeleton.

## Where it lived

The Villar del Arzobispo Formation represents a coastal or lagoonal environment at the Jurassic–Cretaceous boundary in what is now northeastern Spain. During this period, the Iberian Peninsula occupied a position between the Tethys Sea to the east and the Atlantic to the west. The formation has yielded sauropods, theropods, and ornithopods, reflecting a diverse dinosaurian fauna in a subtropical coastal setting.

The only known Aragosaurus specimen was recovered from this Late Jurassic to Early Cretaceous formation near Galve, a site that has yielded an important diversity of European dinosaurs.

## Open questions

- The type locality is not directly radiometrically dated.
- Its exact position within early Titanosauriformes varies.
