## Overview

Dracorex (meaning 'dragon king') was a pachycephalosaurian dinosaur from the very end of the Cretaceous period, approximately 66 million years ago, found in the Hell Creek Formation of South Dakota, USA. Estimated at around 4 metres long, it is known primarily from a spectacular skull bearing prominent spikes, knobs, and frills that give it a distinctly dragon-like appearance, rather than the domed skull typical of most pachycephalosaurs. The type and only species is Dracorex hogwartsia, fancifully named after the fictional school in J.K. Rowling's Harry Potter series.

Dracorex sits at the centre of an ongoing scientific debate: several researchers, most notably Jack Horner and Mark Goodman, have proposed that Dracorex may represent a juvenile growth stage of the more derived Pachycephalosaurus wyomingensis, with the flat-skulled, spiky morphology transforming into the characteristic dome as the animal matured. If correct, Dracorex (and the intermediate Stygimoloch) would be synonymised with Pachycephalosaurus, significantly simplifying Late Cretaceous pachycephalosaur diversity.

## Anatomy & life

Unlike the characteristic dome of most pachycephalosaurs, the skull of Dracorex is flat-topped and adorned with an array of bony horns, spikes, and irregular knobs around its margins. This combination of features is unique among described pachycephalosaurs and is the primary basis for its recognition as a distinct taxon — though it may also reflect its young age.

Horner and Goodman (2009) argued that pachycephalosaur skull domes grew progressively with age: flat-skulled juveniles (Dracorex) transitioned to a thickened but still spiky stage (Stygimoloch) before developing the full dome of adult Pachycephalosaurus. Bone histology supports rapid remodelling of skull ornamentation in pachycephalosaurs, lending credence to the synonymy hypothesis, though it has not been universally accepted.

Whether a distinct genus or a growth stage of Pachycephalosaurus, this animal lived in the Hell Creek ecosystem alongside Tyrannosaurus rex, Triceratops, and Edmontosaurus in the closing millions of years before the end-Cretaceous extinction. Pachycephalosaurs are inferred to have been herbivores or omnivores, and their robust skulls may have functioned in intraspecific combat or display.

## Fossil record

Dracorex hogwartsia was formally described in 2006 by Robert Bakker and colleagues, based on a nearly complete skull and a few cervical vertebrae found in South Dakota. The whimsical species name was chosen partly to appeal to young people's interest in palaeontology, and the genus name references the skull's resemblance to European dragon iconography. The specimen is housed at the Children's Museum of Indianapolis.

## Open questions

- The synonymy is strongly supported but depends on an incomplete ontogenetic series.
- The precise growth stage of the holotype is not known.
