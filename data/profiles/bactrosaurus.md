## Overview

Bactrosaurus johnsoni was an early hadrosauroid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Inner Mongolia, China, and adjacent regions of Kazakhstan and Mongolia, living approximately 90–72 million years ago during the Turonian to Campanian stages. Estimated at about 6 metres in length, it is known from skull fragments and postcranial bones of at least 12 individuals including juveniles and adults, recovered from the Iren Dabasu Formation. It is regarded as one of the most primitive hadrosaurids known.

Named by Gilmore in 1933, Bactrosaurus is classified within Hadrosauroidea as a relatively basal member of the hadrosaurid lineage. Its early age and primitive anatomy make it important for understanding the origins of the family Hadrosauridae, which went on to become one of the most diverse and abundant groups of Late Cretaceous dinosaurs. It co-occurred with Archaeornithomimus and early tyrannosauroids in the Iren Dabasu ecosystem.

## Anatomy & life

Bactrosaurus is known from a relatively large sample including skull fragments and substantial postcranial remains from multiple growth stages. The skull material shows a relatively low profile without evidence of a crest, consistent with a basal hadrosaurid. It possessed the typical hadrosaurid dental battery — a complex of hundreds of teeth replacing one another continuously — suited to processing tough vegetation.

Bactrosaurus inhabited lowland plains and deltaic environments in the Late Cretaceous of East and Central Asia. It was a gregarious herbivore, processing large quantities of vegetation with its dental battery. The Iren Dabasu Formation fauna in which it occurs represents an important window into the mid-Cretaceous Asian dinosaur world, before the great diversification of the classic Campanian faunas.

## Fossil record

Bactrosaurus johnsoni was described by Charles Gilmore in 1933 from material collected during the American Museum of Natural History's Central Asiatic Expeditions of the 1920s. The generic name derives from Bactria, the ancient region of Central Asia corresponding to northern Afghanistan and neighbouring areas, referring broadly to the Asian origin of the fossils. The species name honours J. B. Johnson, a member of the expedition.

Disarticulated skull and skeleton elements from at least twelve individuals.

## Where it lived

Multiple Bactrosaurus individuals including juveniles and adults were recovered from this formation, providing important ontogenetic data for one of the earliest known hadrosaurids.

## Classification

Bactrosaurus has long been considered one of the more primitive members of Hadrosauridae, occupying a position close to the base of the family. Its relatively early geological age (Turonian) is consistent with this interpretation, predating the major diversification of lambeosaurines and hadrosaurines that occurred in the Campanian. This basal position makes it important for anchoring analyses of hadrosaurid evolution.

## Open questions

- The formation age is imprecise.
- Some historical referrals and named species are not secure.
- Its position close to Hadrosauridae varies.
