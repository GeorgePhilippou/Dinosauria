## Overview

Liaoceratops was a small basal neoceratopsian dinosaur that lived during the Early Cretaceous, approximately 130–125 million years ago, in what is now Liaoning Province, northeastern China. It was a diminutive animal, estimated at less than 1 metre in length, with a small frill and incipient horn-like structures that represent very early stages in the evolution of the elaborate head ornamentation seen in later ceratopsians. The type species is Liaoceratops yanzigouensis.

Described in 2002 by Xu Xing and colleagues, Liaoceratops is significant for illuminating the early evolutionary history of ceratopsian dinosaurs in Asia. The discovery of two nearly complete skulls provided unusually detailed anatomical information for such an early ceratopsian, and its characteristics suggest that the frill and horn-like features that define more derived ceratopsians began to evolve in Asia during the Early Cretaceous, long before the elaborately horned forms appeared in North America.

## Anatomy & life

The discovery of Liaoceratops reinforced the view that ceratopsians originated and underwent their early diversification in Asia before members of the group dispersed into North America. During the Early Cretaceous, a diverse array of basal ceratopsians including Psittacosaurus and various neoceratopsians lived in what is now China, providing a rich fossil record for studying the group's early evolution.

Liaoceratops was a very small animal, likely feeding on low-growing plants in the lush Early Cretaceous forests of Liaoning. The Yixian Formation is famous for preserving an exceptional diversity of Early Cretaceous life, including feathered dinosaurs, early birds, and early flowering plants, placing Liaoceratops in one of the best-documented ancient ecosystems in the fossil record.

## Fossil record

Liaoceratops yanzigouensis was described in 2002 by Xu Xing, Peter Makovicky, Wang Xiaolin, Mark Norell, and You Hailu based on two nearly complete skulls from the Yixian Formation of Liaoning Province. The genus name refers to the Liao River region, and the species name honours Yanzigou, the locality where the fossils were found. The exceptional preservation of two skulls made this one of the better-documented early ceratopsians at the time of its description.

## Where it lived

Two nearly complete skulls of Liaoceratops yanzigouensis were recovered from the famous Early Cretaceous Yixian Formation lagerstätte.

## Classification

Liaoceratops possesses a small frill and subtly differentiated jugal (cheek) bones that foreshadow the elaborate frills and horns of later ceratopsians. However, it lacked true horns and its frill was simple and relatively small. These features place it at an important transitional grade in ceratopsian evolution, showing that frill development preceded the elaboration of facial horns.
