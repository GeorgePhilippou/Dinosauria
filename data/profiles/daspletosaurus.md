## Overview

Daspletosaurus torosus ("frightful lizard") was a large tyrannosaurine from the Late Campanian of Alberta (~76–77 Ma), contemporaneous with Gorgosaurus in the Dinosaur Park Formation. The co-occurrence of two large tyrannosaurs in the same formation is unusual and has generated competing hypotheses about ecological partitioning, temporal replacement and niche separation.

Daspletosaurus was more robust and had proportionally larger teeth than Gorgosaurus, suggesting it may have specialised on larger, more heavily armoured prey such as ceratopsids. A recently described species, D. wilsoni, from the Oldman Formation represents an older member of the lineage, suggesting a continuous tyrannosaurine presence in the region.

## Anatomy & life

The two large tyrannosaurs differ in robustness: Daspletosaurus has a deeper, heavier skull and more powerful dentition consistent with regularly attacking heavily built prey. Gorgosaurus is more gracile, possibly pursuing lighter-bodied hadrosaurs. Whether they partitioned prey by size or used habitats differently remains an active research question.

## Fossil record

Russell described Daspletosaurus torosus in 1970 from a nearly complete skeleton collected by Charles Sternberg from the Dinosaur Park Formation. The species name "torosus" refers to its robust muscular build. Additional specimens have since been recovered, and D. wilsoni was described in 2022.

Multiple skulls and partial-to-substantial skeletons across several individuals.

A robust skull and strong teeth distinguish Daspletosaurus from the slimmer Gorgosaurus with which it coexisted.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous coastal plain of Alberta with ceratopsians, hadrosaurs and ankylosaurs.

## Classification

Daspletosaurus is placed in Tyrannosaurinae alongside Tarbosaurus and Tyrannosaurus, suggesting a northern Laurasian origin of this clade. Its presence in Alberta predating both Tarbosaurus in Asia and Tyrannosaurus in North America places it near the ancestral node of the iconic Late Cretaceous large tyrannosaur radiation.

## Open questions

- Species boundaries and the proposed anagenetic sequence remain contested.
- Social behaviour and precise mass estimates are indirect.
