## Overview

Yingshanosaurus ('Mt. Ying lizard') was a stegosaurid dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of Sichuan Province, China, living approximately 159–142 million years ago during the Oxfordian–Tithonian stages. It is notable for possessing unusually large, broad shoulder spines that may have served as display or defensive structures, a feature that distinguishes it from many other stegosaurids. The type species, Yingshanosaurus jichuanensis, was described from the Upper Shaximiao Formation.

Yingshanosaurus is considered a nomen nudum by some researchers because the original description was published in a Japanese popular dinosaur book in 1992 without the formal scientific rigour required for valid nomenclature under the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature. This ambiguous status means that its scientific validity and placement within Stegosauria are not fully accepted by all workers, though it continues to appear in the literature and databases.

## Anatomy & life

The most notable feature attributed to Yingshanosaurus is a pair of broad, plate-like shoulder (pectoral) spines or girdle plates, quite different from the back plates and tail spikes typical of most stegosaurids. If these features are correctly attributed to a single individual, they would represent an unusual morphology within Stegosauria, suggesting possible specialisation for display or defence. However, uncertainty about the completeness and association of the specimen limits confidence in this interpretation.

The Upper Shaximiao Formation of Sichuan is notable for its rich stegosaur fauna, including Chialingosaurus, Tuojiangosaurus, and Chungkingosaurus, making it one of the most diverse stegosaur assemblages in the world. If Yingshanosaurus is valid, it would add to this diversity and suggest that Late Jurassic China was a centre of stegosaurid evolution with multiple co-occurring species.

The nomen nudum status of Yingshanosaurus means that until a formal redescription meeting ICZN requirements is published, its systematic position within Stegosauria cannot be rigorously evaluated. A thorough re-examination of the original specimen and formal publication would be required to establish whether the shoulder structures are genuine and to determine its relationships to other stegosaurids.

## Fossil record

Yingshanosaurus jichuanensis was described in 1992 by Watanabe in a Japanese-language dinosaur book, without formal publication in a peer-reviewed scientific journal. This means the name is generally considered a nomen nudum — a name published without adequate description or in an inappropriate format to satisfy the requirements of the ICZN — and its scientific validity is disputed. Despite this, the specimen has been discussed in subsequent palaeontological literature.

Partial associated skeleton with incomplete skull, postcrania and dermal armour (CV 00722).

## Where it lived

Late Jurassic floodplain deposits of the Sichuan Basin from which Yingshanosaurus was reportedly described.

## Open questions

- The original description is difficult to access outside China and the specimen lacks a modern full redescription.
- Its diagnostic status and precise position within Stegosauria require renewed study.
- The age of the Upper Shaximiao Formation is still being refined.
