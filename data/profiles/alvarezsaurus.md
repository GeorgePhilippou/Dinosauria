## Overview

Alvarezsaurus calvoi was a small theropod dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Patagonia, Argentina, living approximately 86–84 million years ago. Approximately 2 metres in length, it is the namesake of the family Alvarezsauridae and was recovered from the Bajo de la Carpa Formation of Neuquén Province. It is thought to have been lightly built and fast-moving, possibly feathered in life.

Named by Bonaparte in 1991, Alvarezsaurus is a key taxon for understanding the early evolution of the alvarezsaurid lineage, a bizarre group of maniraptorans characterised by highly reduced forelimbs with a single robust claw. Alvarezsaurus itself appears relatively unspecialised compared to later members of the family, making it important for reconstructing the sequence of anatomical changes that produced the extreme forelimb reduction seen in forms like Mononykus.

## Anatomy & life

Compared with derived alvarezsaurids, Alvarezsaurus retains relatively elongated forelimbs, suggesting it represents a less-derived stage of the forelimb-reduction trend characteristic of the family. Its hind limbs are slender and elongated, consistent with a cursorial lifestyle. As with other maniraptorans of its size, it was likely feathered, though direct evidence of integument is not preserved.

Alvarezsaurus is critical for understanding the origin of alvarezsaurids because it predates the more extreme anatomical modifications of later relatives. Its relatively plesiomorphic forelimb anatomy places it as a basal member of the clade, helping researchers reconstruct the stepwise evolution of the bizarre single-clawed arm that defines more derived alvarezsaurids.

## Fossil record

Alvarezsaurus calvoi was described by José Bonaparte in 1991 from partial postcranial remains collected in the Bajo de la Carpa Formation of Neuquén Province, Argentina. The generic name honours historian Don Gregorio Álvarez, while the species name honours palaeontologist Jorge Calvo. It became the namesake of the family Alvarezsauridae when that group was formally defined.

One incomplete postcranial skeleton.

## Where it lived

The Bajo de la Carpa Formation represents a semi-arid, seasonally dry environment of Late Cretaceous Patagonia. Alvarezsaurus shared this landscape with abelisaurid theropods, titanosaurian sauropods, and various other vertebrates. The Neuquén Basin has proven extraordinarily rich for Late Cretaceous dinosaurs, yielding many important taxa from across the dinosaur family tree.

Partial skeletal remains of the only known Alvarezsaurus individual were recovered here, establishing the type locality of the genus.

## Open questions
