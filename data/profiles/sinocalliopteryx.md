## Overview

Sinocalliopteryx (meaning 'Chinese beautiful feather') was a large compsognathid theropod from the Early Cretaceous Yixian Formation of Liaoning Province, China, approximately 125 million years ago. The holotype skeleton is nearly complete and preserves filamentous integumentary structures ('protofeathers') covering much of the body, as well as remarkable gut contents including the leg of a dromaeosaurid dinosaur and gastroliths (stomach stones). At about 2.37 metres in length it was the largest known compsognathid.

Named by Ji, Ji, Lü, and Yuan in 2007, Sinocalliopteryx gigas is scientifically significant on multiple fronts: it demonstrates that even large compsognathids bore feather-like body coverings, and its preserved stomach contents provide direct evidence of predatory behaviour and prey choice among Cretaceous feathered theropods. The presence of bird bones alongside dromaeosaurid remains in the gut of a second specimen further illuminates theropod predator-prey relationships in the Jehol Biota.

## Anatomy & life

Both known specimens preserve filamentous structures interpreted as protofeathers covering much of the trunk and tail. This confirms that even relatively large theropods within Compsognathidae bore insulating body coverings, consistent with the hypothesis that feather-like structures were widespread among coelurosaurs rather than restricted to the lineage leading to birds.

The holotype preserves the partial leg of a dromaeosaurid dinosaur within the abdominal region, and a second specimen contains the remains of two Confuciusornis birds and a partial enantiornithine bird. These gut contents provide unusually direct evidence of what Sinocalliopteryx ate, demonstrating it was an active predator of other feathered dinosaurs and early birds.

As the largest compsognathid, Sinocalliopteryx occupied a mid-tier predator niche in the diverse Jehol Biota ecosystem. Its diet of dromaeosaurids and birds indicates it targeted prey of significant size relative to its own body. The Yixian Formation ecosystem also included fish, frogs, lizards, early mammals, and a profusion of feathered dinosaurs that together represent one of palaeontology's greatest fossil assemblages.

## Fossil record

Sinocalliopteryx gigas was described in 2007 by Ji Shu-An, Ji Qiang, Lü Junchang, and Yuan Chongxi from two specimens recovered from the Yixian Formation of western Liaoning. The genus name means 'Chinese beautiful feather', a reference to the preserved integumentary filaments, and the species name gigas ('giant') reflects its unusual size for a compsognathid.

Two articulated skeletons with filamentous integument and gut contents.

- Filamentous integument is preserved around the body.
- Gut contents include a dromaeosaurid leg and birds in different specimens, directly documenting carnivory but not hunting method.
- The monophyly and family-level name of compsognathid-like theropods remain analysis-dependent.

## Where it lived

Both known Sinocalliopteryx specimens come from the remarkable fossil-bearing lacustrine shales of the Yixian Formation, famous for feathered dinosaurs.

High-precision dating brackets the Yixian Formation at 125.755-124.122 Ma, within the Barremian on the current time scale.

## Open questions

- Gut contents do not distinguish predation from scavenging.
- Family-level placement is unsettled.
