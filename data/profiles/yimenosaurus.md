## Overview

Yimenosaurus ('Yimen lizard') was a large sauropodomorph dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of Yunnan Province, China, living approximately 195–190 million years ago during the Toarcian stage. It reached about 9 metres in length, making it one of the largest sauropodomorphs of its time in Asia, and is known from approximately 10 partial skeletons and at least one skull collected near Yimen County. The type species, Yimenosaurus youngi, was named in 1990 in honour of the eminent Chinese palaeontologist C.C. Young.

Yimenosaurus is placed within basal Sauropodomorpha (informally 'prosauropods'), though it occupies a relatively advanced position closer to true sauropods than many earlier members of the group. Its relatively complete material makes it one of the better-known Early Jurassic sauropodomorphs from China and provides valuable data on the morphological transition between prosauropods and true sauropods in Asia during the Jurassic.

## Anatomy & life

Yimenosaurus is known from approximately 10 partial skeletons, making it one of the better-represented Early Jurassic sauropodomorphs from China. Available material includes cranial and postcranial elements that reveal a large-bodied, long-necked animal retaining leaf-shaped teeth appropriate for herbivory. Its larger size relative to earlier sauropodomorphs like Lufengosaurus from the same region suggests either different ecological specialisation or temporal evolution toward the sauropod grade.

Yunnan Province is one of the most productive regions in the world for Early and Middle Jurassic dinosaur fossils, with formations spanning the Triassic–Jurassic transition through the Middle Jurassic. The successive sauropodomorph faunas of this region — from basal forms like Lufengosaurus through Yimenosaurus and on to the abundant sauropods of the later Jurassic — provide an unusually detailed record of sauropod origins.

## Fossil record

Yimenosaurus youngi was described in 1990 by Bai Zhikui, Yang Jianwei, and Wang Guofu from material collected in Yunnan Province, China. The genus name references Yimen County, the area of the type locality, while the species name honours the pioneering Chinese vertebrate palaeontologist Chung Chien Young (Yang Zhongjian), a founding figure of Chinese dinosaur science.

More than ten partial skeletons reported, including substantial cranial and postcranial material.

## Where it lived

Early Jurassic fluvial deposits of Yunnan Province from which Yimenosaurus youngi was described.

## Classification

Yimenosaurus occupies a relatively advanced position among basal sauropodomorphs, with some analyses placing it closer to true sauropods than Plateosaurus or Massospondylus. Yunnan Province had already produced abundant prosauropod material (notably the Lufeng Formation fauna including Lufengosaurus), and Yimenosaurus from slightly younger deposits represents a continuation of sauropodomorph evolutionary trends toward greater size and obligate quadrupedality.

## Open questions

- Abundant material is reported, but the genus still lacks a modern comprehensive first-hand redescription and phylogenetic test.
