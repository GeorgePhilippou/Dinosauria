## Overview

Brachytrachelopan was a dicraeosaurid sauropod from the Late Jurassic of Argentina, approximately 155–145 million years ago (Kimmeridgian to Tithonian), found in the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation of Chubut Province. Estimated at about 10 metres in length, it is remarkable for having the shortest neck, proportionally, of any known sauropod dinosaur. Its name, meaning 'short-necked Pan' (after the Greek shepherd deity), directly references this unusual feature.

Named in 2005 by Rauhut, Remes, Fechner, Cladera, and Puerta, Brachytrachelopan is known from partial neck and dorsal vertebrae, ribs, a partial pelvis, and limb bones. As a dicraeosaurid, it belongs to a clade of diplodocoid sauropods characterised by forked neural spines and relatively short necks compared to diplodocids—but Brachytrachelopan takes this to an extreme, suggesting ecological specialisation for low browsing.

## Anatomy & life

Brachytrachelopan's most striking feature is its proportionally short neck, which is the shortest known among sauropod dinosaurs. While dicraeosaurids as a group tended towards shorter necks than diplodocids, Brachytrachelopan takes this to an extreme degree. The short neck is interpreted as an ecological adaptation for low or ground-level browsing, contrasting sharply with the high-browsing strategy typically associated with long-necked sauropods.

## Fossil record

The only known specimen of Brachytrachelopan mesai was discovered in the Cañadón Calcáreo Formation of Chubut Province, Patagonia, Argentina, and described in 2005 by Oliver Rauhut and colleagues. The species name honours Daniel Mesa, the shepherd who found the fossil on his land. The specimen includes partial neck vertebrae, dorsal vertebrae, ribs, a partial pelvis, and hindlimb bones.

One incomplete, partly articulated postcranial skeleton without a skull.

## Where it lived

The Late Jurassic of Patagonia was a diverse ecosystem preserving multiple sauropod lineages alongside theropods and small ornithischians. The unusual body plan of Brachytrachelopan suggests niche partitioning among sauropods, with different taxa exploiting vegetation at different heights. Its discovery has contributed to understanding how ecological diversity was achieved among giant herbivores.

## Classification

Brachytrachelopan is placed within Dicraeosauridae, a family of diplodocoid sauropods also including Dicraeosaurus from Tanzania and Amargasaurus from Argentina. Dicraeosaurids are distinguished by their bifurcated (forked) dorsal neural spines, which in Amargasaurus were dramatically elongated. The dicraeosaurids appear to represent an ecological and morphological departure from the long-necked template of most sauropods.
