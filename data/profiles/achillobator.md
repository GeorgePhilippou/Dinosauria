## Overview

Achillobator was a large dromaeosaurid from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, known from the Baynshire Formation. At roughly five metres long in the NHM dataset, it was far larger than the small bird-like dromaeosaurs often used to represent the group.

The genus is important because it shows that dromaeosaurids were not just small agile predators: some lineages produced robust, large-bodied hunters. PBDB records a single sampled occurrence from Dornogov, so its abundance and precise ecological role remain uncertain.

## Anatomy & life

Achillobator broadens the ecological range of dromaeosaurs, showing that the sickle-clawed lineage included sizeable predators as well as small bird-like forms.

The known material is limited, so reconstructions depend heavily on comparison with better-known dromaeosaurids.

## Fossil record

One disarticulated partial cranial and postcranial assemblage.

## Open questions

- The association of every listed element has been questioned.
- The formation age and exact dromaeosaurid position remain broad.
