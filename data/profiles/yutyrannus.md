## Overview

Yutyrannus huali is the largest dinosaur with direct fossil evidence of feathers — a nine-metre tyrannosaur relative from the Early Cretaceous Yixian Formation of Liaoning, China (~125 Ma). Filamentous feather-like structures are preserved on three specimens including the adult holotype, demonstrating that a 1.4-tonne tyrannosaur could retain insulating body coverings.

The discovery transformed questions about feather distribution in large-bodied tyrannosauroids: if Yutyrannus bore extensive filaments in a cool Early Cretaceous climate, whether the much larger but later Tyrannosaurus retained feathers at any life stage becomes a genuinely open question rather than a settled matter.

## Anatomy & life

The Yixian Formation palaeoclimate was significantly cooler than many Late Cretaceous environments (mean annual temperatures estimated at ~10°C). Insulating filaments would have provided real thermoregulatory benefit at this body size in this climate. For larger, later tyrannosaurs living in warmer environments, feathers may have been lost or restricted to juveniles.

## Fossil record

Xu Xing and colleagues described Yutyrannus in 2012 from three individuals from the same Liaoning locality. The filamentous integument — preserved on the neck, arm and tail — consists of hair-like proto-feathers rather than pennaceous flight feathers. Finding them on a 1.4-tonne adult, not just juveniles, was the critical result.

Three articulated skeletons, including an adult and younger individuals, with feather impressions.

Large tyrannosauroid body fossils with feather impressions show large-bodied tyrannosauroids could be feathered.

- Long filamentous feathers are directly preserved on multiple body regions.
- The three associated individuals do not by themselves prove pack hunting or stable social groups.
- Its position within Tyrannosauroidea varies among analyses, so a narrower family assignment is not presented as settled.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous northeastern Chinese lake-and-volcanic ecosystems.

High-precision dating brackets the Yixian Formation at 125.755-124.122 Ma, within the Barremian on the current time scale.

## Classification

Despite its size, Yutyrannus is a proceratosaurid — a relatively basal tyrannosauroid related to Guanlong and Sinotyrannus — not a close relative of T. rex. It shows the nasal rugosities and paired crests typical of proceratosaurids. Its body plan anticipates the later giant tyrannosaurs: large skull, reduced forelimbs and powerful hindlimbs.

## Open questions

- Death-assemblage behaviour, exact feather coverage and finer tyrannosauroid placement remain uncertain.
