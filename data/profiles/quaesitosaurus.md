## Overview

Quaesitosaurus (meaning 'extraordinary lizard') was a large herbivorous sauropod from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, tentatively dated to approximately 86–72 million years ago, though the precise range is uncertain. Named by Kurzanov and Bannikov in 1983, it is estimated to have reached around 23 metres in length and is known only from a partial skull—primarily the back of the braincase and lower jaws—from the Baruungoyot Formation of Omnogov province. It belongs to Nemegtosauridae within Diplodocoidea.

Quaesitosaurus is one of two Mongolian nemegtosaurids known primarily from skulls (the other being Nemegtosaurus), and its skull shows a diplodocid-like elongated snout with pencil-shaped teeth suited for cropping vegetation. Its partial skull has been studied for evidence of inner ear morphology, suggesting it may have had good low-frequency hearing—possibly an adaptation for long-range communication in a social sauropod.

## Anatomy & life

The known cranial material includes the occiput, parts of the braincase, and lower jaw elements. These show the characteristic features of Nemegtosauridae: a long, slender skull with pencil-like teeth at the front of the jaws—a diplodocoid-style skull that contrasts markedly with the more robust skulls of titanosaurs found in the same deposits.

Analysis of the inner ear region of the Quaesitosaurus skull has suggested a cochlear morphology suited to low-frequency sound detection. If accurate, this may indicate that large Mongolian sauropods were capable of sensing or producing infrasound, possibly for long-distance communication between herd members—though these interpretations are speculative.

## Fossil record

Quaesitosaurus orientalis was named by Kurzanov and Bannikov in 1983 from a partial skull collected in the Baruungoyot Formation. The species name refers to its eastern provenance (Mongolia). It was one of several sauropod discoveries to emerge from Soviet-Mongolian expeditions to the Gobi Desert during the 1970s–1980s.

Single incomplete skull and mandible.

- The type skull has the elongate muzzle and slender teeth associated with nemegtosaurid titanosaurs.
- Postcranial anatomy, total length and armour are unknown for the type.
- Its distinction from Nemegtosaurus has been questioned but cannot be resolved confidently from current material.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous sandy formation in the southern Gobi where the only Quaesitosaurus skull was recovered.

The type comes from the late Campanian Baruungoyot Formation; it should not be assigned the age of the younger Nemegt Formation.

## Classification

Some researchers have suggested that Quaesitosaurus and Nemegtosaurus may represent the same animal at different ontogenetic stages or from different parts of the skeleton of the same genus. This has not been conclusively demonstrated, and both are currently retained as separate genera pending discovery of more complete material.

## Open questions

- Validity relative to Nemegtosaurus needs modern specimen-level reassessment.
- Almost the entire body is unknown.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
