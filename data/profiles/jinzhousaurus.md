## Overview

Jinzhousaurus (meaning 'Jinzhou lizard') was a herbivorous iguanodontoid ornithopod dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of Liaoning Province, China, living approximately 125–120 million years ago during the Barremian-Aptian transition. Known from an almost complete skull and some postcranial remains from the Yixian Formation, it is estimated to have been a medium-sized ornithopod. It was named by Wang and Xu in 2001.

Jinzhousaurus is placed within Iguanodontoidea, related to iguanodonts and basal hadrosaurs, and represents part of the extraordinarily rich Jehol Biota of Liaoning — one of the world's most important Early Cretaceous fossil assemblages. The Jehol Biota is famous for preserving feathered dinosaurs, early birds, and flowering plants in exceptional detail. Jinzhousaurus provides comparative ornithopod data for this otherwise theropod-dominated palaeontological assemblage.

## Anatomy & life

The skull of Jinzhousaurus shows features intermediate between basal iguanodonts and more derived hadrosauroids, including a prominent oral beak and dentition adapted for processing plant material. Its iguanodontoid placement means it is closely related to the ancestors of hadrosaurs. Postcranial material is less complete but confirms a bipedally capable, medium-sized ornithopod.

The Yixian Formation preserves one of the world's great fossil assemblages, the Jehol Biota, known for extraordinary preservation including feathers, soft tissues, and colour patterns. Jinzhousaurus is one of the larger herbivores in this assemblage, which is dominated by diverse feathered theropods, early birds like Confuciusornis, and early flowering plants.

Jinzhousaurus contributes to understanding of Early Cretaceous ornithopod diversity in East Asia and the evolutionary transition from iguanodonts to hadrosaurs. Its position within Iguanodontoidea, alongside its Asian provenance, informs biogeographic models of how hadrosaur-line ornithopods dispersed and diversified globally during the Cretaceous.

## Fossil record

Jinzhousaurus yangi was described in 2001 by Wang Xiaolin and Xu Xing from material collected in Liaoning Province, named after the city of Jinzhou near the discovery site, with the species name honouring palaeontologist Yang Zhongjian (Young C.C.). The specimen includes a nearly complete skull, making it one of the better cranially known Early Cretaceous ornithopods from Asia.
