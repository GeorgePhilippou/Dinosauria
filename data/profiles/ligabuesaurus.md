## Overview

Ligabuesaurus was a large herbivorous sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Early Cretaceous, approximately 130–120 million years ago, in what is now Neuquén Province, Argentina. It is known from fragmentary remains including jaw elements, vertebrae, and limb bones, making precise size estimates uncertain, though it appears to have been a sizeable titanosauriform sauropod. The type species is Ligabuesaurus leanzai.

Named in 2006 by José Bonaparte, González Riga, and Apesteguía, Ligabuesaurus is named in honour of Giancarlo Ligabue, an Italian ethnologist and explorer who contributed to palaeontological research in Patagonia. Its fragmentary nature limits what can be said about its anatomy and ecology, but it contributes to the documented diversity of Early Cretaceous sauropods in South America, a time period when titanosaurian sauropods were rapidly diversifying across Gondwana.

## Anatomy & life

Because Ligabuesaurus is known only from incomplete material without a complete skull or associated full skeleton, many aspects of its anatomy remain unknown. The vertebrae and limb bones suggest it was a substantial sauropod, but precise body length and mass estimates are unreliable. Its teeth, preserved in the maxilla, provide some dietary and taxonomic information but are insufficient to fully characterise the genus.

The Neuquén Basin of Patagonia has yielded an extraordinary diversity of Cretaceous sauropods, ranging from the enormous Argentinosaurus to numerous smaller titanosaurs. Ligabuesaurus adds to this diversity from the Early Cretaceous portion of the record, a time period less well-documented than the later Cretaceous in the basin.

## Fossil record

Ligabuesaurus leanzai was described in 2006 by José Bonaparte, Bernardo González Riga, and Sebastián Apesteguía. The genus honours Giancarlo Ligabue, an Italian scientist and explorer who supported palaeontological work in Patagonia, while the species name leanzai acknowledges Héctor Leanza of CONICET for his contributions to Argentine geology. The holotype consists of a partial maxilla with teeth, several cervical and dorsal vertebrae, and girdle and limb bones.

Incomplete cranial and postcranial remains from a small number of individuals.

## Where it lived

The holotype fragmentary remains of Ligabuesaurus leanzai were recovered from Early Cretaceous continental deposits in this Patagonian formation.

## Classification

Ligabuesaurus has been placed within Titanosauriformes, the broad group of sauropods that includes brachiosaurs and titanosaurs. Its exact position within this group is not well-resolved given the fragmentary material. Its Early Cretaceous age from South America is consistent with the time when titanosaurian diversity was increasing significantly across Gondwana.

## Open questions

- Some referred material is incomplete and association is imperfect.
- Placement near Titanosauria and adult size vary among analyses.
