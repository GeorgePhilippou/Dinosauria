## Overview

Minmi paravertebra was a small (~3 m) ankylosaur from the Early Cretaceous Bungil Formation of Queensland, Australia (~119–113 Ma), named for a locality near Roma and for the small extra bone plates (paravertebrae) found alongside the vertebrae — unique among ankylosaurs. It is the most completely known ankylosaur from the Southern Hemisphere.

A second Minmi specimen found in the Toolebuc Formation preserves extensive skin impressions and even stomach contents — plant fragments confirming its herbivorous diet. As one of the earliest-diverging ankylosaurs from Gondwana, Minmi provides important information about how the group diversified outside of the Northern Hemisphere.

## Anatomy & life

The Toolebuc Formation specimen preserves plant fragments in the digestive region including leaves, stems and seeds from flowering plants and conifers — direct evidence of the diet of a Gondwanan ankylosaur. The preservation of this material in marine deposits indicates the animal drifted out to sea after death and sank to the seafloor before scavengers reached it.

Minmi represents one of the earliest records of ankylosaurs from the Southern Hemisphere and helps establish that the group had a global distribution by the Early Cretaceous. Gondwanan ankylosaurs are poorly known compared to their Laurasian relatives; Minmi's relatively complete record makes it a critical reference for understanding ankylosaur dispersal and evolution across the southern continents.

## Fossil record

Ralph Molnar described Minmi paravertebra in 1980 from Queensland material. The paravertebrae — small accessory ossifications alongside the main vertebral column — are unique to Minmi among all known ankylosaurs. Their function is unclear; they may have stiffened the back or provided attachment points for armour tendons.

Partial postcranial holotype with vertebrae, limbs and osteoderms; skull poorly represented.

Armour plates and postcranial material define this small Gondwanan ankylosaur, though its most famous, most complete specimen now belongs to the separately diagnosed Kunbarrasaurus.

- The holotype includes vertebrae, ribs and limb and armour elements but no well-preserved skull.
- Paravertebral ossifications are prominent in the type.
- Upper Albian Australian ankylosaur material once discussed under Minmi belongs to Kunbarrasaurus or other ankylosaurs and must not extend the range or anatomy of Minmi paravertebra.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous Australian environment, then a high-latitude landmass with seasonal conditions.

The Minmi paravertebra holotype is from the lower Aptian Bungil Formation. Upper Albian Toolebuc and Allaru material belongs to Kunbarrasaurus or other ankylosaurs, not securely to Minmi.
