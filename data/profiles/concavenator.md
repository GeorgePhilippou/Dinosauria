## Overview

Concavenator corcovatus is an unusual Early Cretaceous carcharodontosaurid from Cuenca, Spain (~130 Ma), bearing a triangular hump above the hips formed by two enormously elongated neural spines — a feature with no parallel in other theropods. The holotype is the most complete large theropod from Europe and preserves remarkable anatomical detail.

Quill nodes — small bumps on the ulna bone that anchor display or flight feathers in birds — have been reported on the Concavenator forelimb, suggesting it may have borne proto-feathers on its arms. If confirmed, this would extend evidence of feather-related structures into a lineage otherwise not known for integumentary structures, though the interpretation remains debated.

## Anatomy & life

Two elongated neural spines above the hips (vertebrae 4 and 5 of the dorsal series) project far above the vertebral column. Whether this formed a soft-tissue sail, a fatty hump or a display ridge is unclear. No analogous structure in any other non-avian dinosaur or living archosaur provides a direct functional model, though display and/or fat storage have been proposed.

Small bumps on the ulna were interpreted as quill nodes homologous with those anchoring wing feathers in modern birds. Critics argue these bumps may be taphonomic artefacts or pathological features unrelated to feathers. The debate highlights the difficulty of inferring soft tissue from bony correlates when the anatomy is atypical.

## Fossil record

Described by Ortega, Escaso and Sanz in 2010 from the Las Hoyas quarry — a Konservat-Lagerstätte (exceptional preservation site) in Cuenca Province. The specimen is nearly complete with both skull and postcranium preserved in articulation. Its discovery established that large carcharodontosaurids were present in Early Cretaceous Europe.

One nearly complete articulated skeleton with limited skin impressions.

The hump vertebrae and possible quill-knob structures on the arm bones are unique among large theropods.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous Spanish wetland ecosystem with diverse fish, turtles, crocodilians and early birds.

## Open questions

- The ulnar bumps may not be homologous with avian quill knobs.
- Soft tissue over the dorsal spines is unknown.
- Its exact carcharodontosaurian position varies.
