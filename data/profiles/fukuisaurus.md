## Overview

Fukuisaurus (meaning 'Fukui lizard') was a herbivorous iguanodontian dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous of Japan, living approximately 121–113 million years ago during the Aptian stage. Estimated at around 4.7 metres in length, it is known only from skull material — the rest of the skeleton is reconstructed by comparison with similar dinosaurs. Fukuisaurus tetoriensius is particularly significant as it represents one of the few ornithopod dinosaurs described from Japan, broadening understanding of Early Cretaceous plant-eater diversity in East Asia.

Described by Kobayashi and Azuma in 2003 from the Kitadani Formation of Fukui Prefecture, Fukuisaurus is placed within Hadrosauroidea based on aspects of its skull anatomy. Its discovery alongside Fukuiraptor from the same formation helps characterise the Early Cretaceous dinosaur community of Japan. Unusually, Fukuisaurus shares some features with both iguanodontids and hadrosauroids, placing it at an interesting evolutionary grade in ornithopod evolution.

## Anatomy & life

The skeleton of Fukuisaurus beyond the skull remains unknown from direct fossil evidence; reconstructions rely on comparison with related ornithopods. This limitation means that body mass, exact length, and many aspects of locomotion remain inferred. The skull itself is informative: it shows the widened, flattened snout typical of hadrosauroids and indicates a dental battery capable of processing tough plant material.

Fukuisaurus is one of several Early Cretaceous ornithopods from East Asia that collectively suggest significant diversity in the region before the later diversification of hadrosaurid dinosaurs. Related taxa from China and Mongolia indicate that Asia was an important evolutionary centre for the ornithopod lineage during the Early Cretaceous, potentially contributing to the stock that gave rise to true hadrosaurs.

## Fossil record

Fukuisaurus tetoriensius was described by Kobayashi and Azuma in 2003 from skull material recovered from the Kitadani Formation of Fukui Prefecture, Japan. The genus name honours Fukui Prefecture and the species name references the Tetori Group, the broader stratigraphic unit that includes the Kitadani Formation. The specimen consists of cranial bones and represents the first ornithopod dinosaur formally described from Japan.

Diagnostic cranial type material plus referred partial postcranial remains.

## Where it lived

The Kitadani Formation preserves a snapshot of an Early Cretaceous island environment in what is now Japan, a region that was at the time part of the eastern margin of the Asian continent. Fukuisaurus shared this environment with the theropod Fukuiraptor, crocodilians, turtles, and various small vertebrates, in a fluvial lowland setting characterised by river channels and floodplains.

The only known locality; skull material discovered alongside theropod fossils including Fukuiraptor, documenting the Early Cretaceous ecosystem of Fukui.

## Open questions

- The postcranial hypodigm has not received one comprehensive modern description.
- Mass and exact hadrosauroid position vary by reconstruction.
