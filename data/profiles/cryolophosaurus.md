## Overview

Cryolophosaurus was a large theropod dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of Antarctica, approximately 200–182 million years ago (Sinemurian to Pliensbachian), found in the Hanson Formation. Estimated at about 8 metres in length and around 500 kilograms in weight, it was one of the earliest large carnivorous dinosaurs known. Its name, 'frozen crested lizard', references both its Antarctic provenance and the distinctive curved, transverse bony crest on top of its skull.

Named by William Hammer and William Hickerson in 1994 from a partial skeleton recovered during a 1991 Antarctic expedition, Cryolophosaurus elliotti is placed within Tetanurae and possibly within an early branch of Carnosauria or as a basal tetanuran. Its discovery demonstrated that large theropods had already evolved complex cranial ornamentation by the Early Jurassic, and it remains one of the most important theropod discoveries from the Southern Hemisphere.

## Anatomy & life

Cryolophosaurus bore a distinctive, anteroposteriorly oriented (transverse) bony crest above its eyes, unlike the parallel crests of dilophosaurids or the sagittal crests of other crested theropods. This unique crest morphology has been compared to a pompadour hairstyle, earning the animal the informal nickname 'Elvisaurus'. The crest was likely used in display or species recognition rather than as a weapon, as its thin construction would not withstand combat stresses.

During the Early Jurassic, Antarctica was positioned further north and had a temperate climate rather than a polar ice cap. It was part of the supercontinent Gondwana, and its fauna would have been accessible from other Gondwanan landmasses. The presence of a large theropod at this early date confirms that theropods had diversified and reached large body sizes across Gondwana by the Sinemurian.

## Fossil record

Cryolophosaurus elliotti was described in 1994 by William Hammer and William Hickerson from a partial skeleton discovered during a 1991 expedition to the Beardmore Glacier region of Antarctica, at an elevation of about 4,000 metres. The specimen was found frozen in rock at one of the most remote fossil sites on Earth. The species name honours David Elliot, a geologist who contributed to Antarctic research.

One principal partial skull and incomplete skeleton, with additional material not yet fully published.

- The crest is directly preserved, but colour and display function are unknown.
- It has sometimes been called an allosauroid, yet modern analyses place it variably among early neotheropods rather than securely within Allosauridae.

## Where it lived

Early Jurassic continental formation on the Transantarctic Mountains, yielding the primary Cryolophosaurus skeleton.

The Hanson Formation type horizon is Pliensbachian, approximately 191-182 million years old.

## Classification

The phylogenetic position of Cryolophosaurus within Tetanurae has been debated. Some analyses place it within Carnosauria, others as a basal tetanuran outside the allosauroid-coelurosaur split. The Early Jurassic age of the specimen means it comes from a period when major theropod lineages were first diverging, making its precise placement consequential for reconstructing theropod evolutionary history.

## Open questions

- Only one principal specimen is fully described.
- Additional material is incompletely published.
- Its position among early neotheropods varies strongly.
