## Overview

Patagosaurus (meaning 'Patagonian lizard') was a large eusauropod from the Middle Jurassic (Toarcian, approximately 183–168 million years ago) of Chubut Province, Patagonia, Argentina. Fossils come from the Cañadón Asfalto Formation. At about 18 metres in length it was a substantial quadrupedal herbivore. Multiple skeletons have been recovered, making it one of the better-known South American Jurassic sauropods.

Described by Bonaparte in 1979, Patagosaurus fariasi is placed in Cetiosauridae, a family of early non-neosauropod sauropods. PBDB confirms it within Cetiosauridae. The multiple specimens from Cañadón Asfalto have provided important anatomical information and Patagosaurus is regarded as a key taxon for understanding the diversity and palaeobiogeography of early sauropods in Gondwana.

## Anatomy & life

Patagosaurus was a medium-large cetiosaurid with a moderately long neck, robust limbs and a relatively broad body. Its vertebral morphology shows the opisthocoelous cervical vertebrae typical of eusauropods. The limb proportions are graviportal, consistent with a slow-moving quadruped that relied on its large body to process large quantities of plant matter.

## Fossil record

Patagosaurus fariasi was described by José Bonaparte in 1979 from material found in the Cañadón Asfalto region of Chubut, Argentina. The genus name reflects the Patagonian provenance. The species name honours the Farias family, who owned the ranch where the fossils were discovered. Bonaparte described it from multiple individuals, providing a relatively rich sample.

Partial holotype postcranium plus disarticulated remains from multiple individuals.

## Where it lived

The Cañadón Asfalto Formation has yielded a diverse Middle Jurassic fauna. Patagosaurus shared its environment with the theropod Piatnitzkysaurus, crocodyliformes, and other vertebrates. The depositional environment was a complex of rivers and lakes within a tectonically active rift basin. This South American Jurassic fauna differs substantially from contemporaneous North American and African assemblages, reflecting Gondwanan isolation.

As one of the best-documented Middle Jurassic sauropods from South America, Patagosaurus is important for reconstructing Gondwanan sauropod diversity before the radiation of neosauropods. Phylogenetic analyses that include Patagosaurus have been informative for calibrating the timing of major sauropod divergences and testing hypotheses of vicariance versus dispersal in early sauropod biogeography.

Middle Jurassic fluvial and lacustrine deposits of Chubut Province, Patagonia, yielding multiple Patagosaurus specimens including articulated material.

## Open questions

- The exact age of individual collecting horizons is not uniform.
