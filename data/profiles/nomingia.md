## Overview

Nomingia (named after the Nomingiin Gobi, a region of southern Mongolia) was a caenagnathid oviraptorosaurian theropod from the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian, approximately 72–66 million years ago). Fossils come from the Nemegt Formation of Omnogovi Aimag, Mongolia. With an estimated length around 1.6 metres, it was a moderately small oviraptorosaur with an omnivorous diet and ground-dwelling habits.

Described by Barsbold, Osmólska, Watabe, Currie and Tsogtbaatar in 2000, Nomingia is scientifically notable for preserving a pygostyle — a fused terminal series of tail vertebrae previously known only in birds and closely related avialans. This discovery provided direct evidence that at least some non-avian oviraptorosaurs bore fan-shaped tail feathers, profoundly influencing understanding of the evolution of tail plumage in the theropod lineage.

## Anatomy & life

Caenagnathids and their relatives among oviraptorosaurs are generally considered omnivores, with robust beaks capable of processing a variety of food items including seeds, invertebrates, eggs and small vertebrates. PBDB ecospace data classifies Nomingia as omnivorous. It inhabited the Nemegt river-plain ecosystem alongside large theropods, hadrosaurs and sauropods.

## Fossil record

Nomingia gobiensis was described in 2000 by a team led by Rinchen Barsbold and including Halszka Osmólska, Mahito Watabe, Philip Currie and Khishigjav Tsogtbaatar. The holotype is a partial postcranial skeleton lacking the skull, found in the Nemegt Formation. The genus name refers to the Nomingiin Gobi Desert locality.

The most significant anatomical feature of Nomingia is its pygostyle — the last several caudal vertebrae are co-ossified into a single plate-like structure. Until the description of Nomingia, pygostyles were known only in birds and Caudipteryx. The presence of a pygostyle in Nomingia, a caenagnathid, suggests that fan-shaped tail feathers evolved independently in oviraptorosaurs or were present in the common ancestor of oviraptorosaurs and birds.

## Where it lived

Maastrichtian fluvial deposits of the Gobi Desert yielding the partial postcranial holotype of Nomingia.

## Classification

Nomingia is placed within Caenagnathinae within Caenagnathidae, a North American and Asian clade of oviraptorosaurs. Caenagnathids are distinguished from oviraptorids by features of the pelvis and hindlimb. The phylogenetic placement of Nomingia has been largely stable since its description, consistently recovering it as a derived caenagnathid.
