## Overview

Aralosaurus tuberiferus was a hadrosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of what is now Kazakhstan, living approximately 86–83 million years ago during the Santonian stage. Estimated at approximately 8 metres in length, it is known from a partial skull and some postcranial bones found in the Bostobe Formation, northeast of the Aral Sea. Its name means 'Aral Sea lizard', reflecting its discovery in the region around this Central Asian inland sea.

Described by Rozhdestvensky in 1968, Aralosaurus is placed within Lambeosaurinae based on PBDB classification, though its phylogenetic position has been debated — the distinctive nasal morphology, described as a tuber-like swelling, has been interpreted as a rudimentary crest. As one of the few hadrosaurids known from Central Asia, it provides important evidence for the geographic spread of hadrosaurs across the ancient Tethyan region.

## Anatomy & life

Aralosaurus is known primarily from the posterior portion of the skull, which reveals a distinctive upward-rising nasal bone that forms a small tuber-like protuberance above the orbits. This feature has been interpreted as either a rudimentary crest precursor or a unique nasal elaboration without direct homology to the hollow crests of other lambeosaurines. Postcranial material is fragmentary and does not significantly depart from the general hadrosaurid body plan.

Aralosaurus is one of the few hadrosaurids known from Central Asia and provides important biogeographic data about the dispersal of hadrosaurid dinosaurs across the ancient Eurasian landmass. Its age at approximately 83.6 million years makes it among the earlier lambeosaurines known from Asia, suggesting that crested hadrosaurs were already diversified across Asia by the Santonian stage.

## Fossil record

Aralosaurus tuberiferus was described by Anatoly Konstantinovich Rozhdestvensky in 1968 from a posterior half of a skull and fragmentary postcranial bones. The generic name means 'Aral Sea lizard', referring to the geographic location of the find. The species name tuberiferus, meaning 'bearing a tuber', describes the prominent nasal swelling visible in the skull material.

One fragmentary skull with jaws and limited referred cranial material.

## Where it lived

The Bostobe Formation in Kazakhstan represents coastal to floodplain environments bordering the Tethys Sea during the Santonian–Campanian transition. Central Asia during this interval was a mosaic of lowland plains, river deltas, and shallow coastal settings. The region's dinosaur fauna, though incompletely known, includes hadrosaurids, ceratopsians, and theropods, suggesting ecological connections with both Asian and European dinosaur communities.

The only known Aralosaurus specimen was recovered from this formation northeast of the Aral Sea, making it the type and sole locality for the genus.
