## Overview

Barapasaurus was a large, primitive sauropod dinosaur from the Early Jurassic period, approximately 199–184 million years ago (Sinemurian to Pliensbachian), found in the Kota Formation of what is now India. Its name means 'big leg lizard' in Telugu, reflecting its massive limbs. Reaching an estimated 14 metres in length, it is among the earliest well-known true sauropods and represents an important early phase in the evolution of giant, long-necked herbivores.

Named in 1975 by Jain, Kutty, Roy-Chowdhury, and Chatterjee, Barapasaurus is scientifically important as one of the earliest and most completely known sauropods from Gondwana. It was placed within the Cetiosauridae and within the broader Gravisauria, helping researchers understand the early radiation of sauropods out of Gondwana. Its discovery in India—a continent that was part of southern Gondwana in the Jurassic—has informed biogeographical models for early sauropod dispersal.

## Anatomy & life

Barapasaurus reached approximately 14 metres in length, making it one of the largest Early Jurassic dinosaurs known. It had spoon-shaped teeth suited to cropping vegetation, a long neck, and columnar limbs typical of sauropods. While relatively primitive, it already displayed the body plan that would characterise giant sauropods throughout the Jurassic and Cretaceous.

## Fossil record

Barapasaurus tagorei was described in 1975 by S.L. Jain, T.K. Kutty, T. Roy-Chowdhury, and S. Chatterjee from multiple partial skeletons discovered in the Kota Formation of the Pranhita-Godavari Basin, India. The specific name honours the poet and polymath Rabindranath Tagore. The material represents one of the best Early Jurassic sauropod collections from any single site.

About three hundred mostly disarticulated bones from at least six individuals.

## Where it lived

The Kota Formation preserves a fluvial environment with diverse fauna including fish, crocodylomorphs, and early mammals alongside Barapasaurus. As a large herbivore in an ecosystem with relatively low plant diversity compared to later periods, it likely fed on ferns, cycads, and conifers at varying heights.

The primary source of Barapasaurus material, yielding multiple partial skeletons from Lower Jurassic fluvial sediments.

## Classification

As one of the earliest known sauropods from Gondwana, Barapasaurus is central to understanding how sauropod body size increased rapidly during the Early Jurassic. Its placement within Cetiosauridae and Gravisauria indicates that large-bodied sauropods had already differentiated from smaller sauropodomorphs by the Sinemurian age, earlier than once thought.

## Open questions
