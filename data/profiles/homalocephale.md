## Overview

Homalocephale (meaning 'level head') was a small herbivorous pachycephalosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 72–66 million years ago. Known from a complete skull and nearly complete postcranium collected from the Nemegt Formation of Omnogov, it reached an estimated length of about 1.5 metres. Unlike dome-headed pachycephalosaurs, Homalocephale had a flat, decorated skull roof, which contributed to early debates about the function of pachycephalosaur head structures. It was named by Maryańska and Osmólska in 1974.

Homalocephale is placed within Homalocephaloidea, a group of flat-headed pachycephalosaurs. Whether the flat roof represents a true evolutionary state or a juvenile stage of dome-headed forms has been debated: some researchers have proposed that flat-headed pachycephalosaurs like Homalocephale are juveniles of dome-headed taxa, though this interpretation remains controversial. The genus is closely related to Prenocephale. Its well-preserved postcranial material makes it one of the better-documented pachycephalosaurs anatomically.

## Anatomy & life

Homalocephale's flat skull roof has been at the centre of debate about pachycephalosaur ontogeny. Some workers have proposed that flat-headed genera like Homalocephale are juveniles of dome-headed taxa, but the adult size and postcranial proportions of Homalocephale suggest it may be a genuinely flat-headed adult. This debate remains unresolved and has broad implications for pachycephalosaur diversity.

The well-preserved postcranium of Homalocephale, including details of the pelvis and hindlimb, has made it one of the most informative pachycephalosaurs for understanding the locomotory anatomy of the group. It also serves as a key reference point in debates about ontogenetic change within Pachycephalosauria.

## Fossil record

Homalocephale calathocercos was named in 1974 by Teresa Maryańska and Halszka Osmólska from a complete skull and postcranium from the Nemegt Formation of Mongolia. The genus name refers to its notably flat skull roof, in contrast to the domed heads of many pachycephalosaurs. The species name translates roughly as 'basket-tailed', referring to features of the tail vertebrae.

One incomplete, probably immature skeleton with partial skull.

- The individual was not fully mature, complicating diagnosis from skull shape.
- The famous broad pelvis does not demonstrate live birth or any particular reproductive posture.
- Herbivory or omnivory remains a broad inference rather than direct dietary proof.

## Where it lived

The Nemegt Formation preserves a lush, river-dominated Late Cretaceous landscape, quite different from the desert environments of the overlying Djadokhta Formation. Homalocephale coexisted with the giant hadrosaur Saurolophus, ankylosaurs, the theropod Tarbosaurus, and other diverse fauna in this productive floodplain ecosystem.

The rich Maastrichtian Nemegt Formation has yielded both skull and postcranial material of Homalocephale, alongside diverse Late Cretaceous fauna.

The Nemegt Formation spans the latest Campanian or early Maastrichtian into the late Maastrichtian; precise correlation remains debated.

## Open questions

- Ontogeny may account for some diagnostic features.
- Only one incomplete individual is known and genus-level validity remains debated.
