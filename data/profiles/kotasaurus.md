## Overview

Kotasaurus (meaning 'Kota lizard') was an early herbivorous sauropod dinosaur from the Early Jurassic of India, living approximately 199–184 million years ago during the Sinemurian to Pliensbachian stages. Known from extensive postcranial material from multiple individuals recovered from the Kota Formation near Yamanpalli, Telangana, it reached an estimated 9 metres in length. No skull material is known, so head shape is inferred from related early sauropods. It was named by P. Yadagiri in 1988.

Kotasaurus is placed near the base of Sauropoda, making it one of the most primitive sauropods known and an important taxon for understanding the earliest stages of sauropod evolution. The Kota Formation is one of the few Early Jurassic formations globally that preserves sauropod material, and Yadagiri reported postcranial elements from more than twelve individuals, providing a substantial sample for a primitive sauropod. Its early age and Indian provenance contribute to debates about where and how sauropods first diversified from their prosauropod ancestors.

## Anatomy & life

Kotasaurus is known only from postcranial elements including vertebrae, limb bones, and girdle material. These suggest a relatively primitive sauropod with a more robust build than many later forms. No skull is preserved, which limits reconstruction of the head and dietary apparatus. PBDB places it in the Sinemurian-Pliensbachian, approximately 199–184 million years ago, making it among the earliest sauropods by age.

As one of the earliest sauropods, Kotasaurus is critical for understanding the basal radiation of the group. Its Indian provenance adds to evidence for Gondwanan origins of sauropods, and the multiple-individual assemblage offers potential for palaeoecological and ontogenetic studies, if further preparation and analysis are undertaken. Its phylogenetic position near the base of Sauropoda makes it a key reference taxon.

## Fossil record

Kotasaurus yamanpalliensis was described in 1988 by P. Yadagiri from material collected in the Kota Formation near Yamanpalli, Telangana (then Andhra Pradesh). The genus name refers to the Kota Formation, and the species name honours the type locality. Yadagiri's description noted postcranial elements from over twelve individuals, suggesting the Yamanpalli site was a mass-death or accumulation site.

Disarticulated postcranial remains from at least twelve individuals.

- The assemblage is multi-individual, not a complete skeleton.
- Robust limbs support habitual quadrupedal locomotion.
- The age of the Kota Formation and its position near the base of Sauropoda are both actively discussed.

## Where it lived

The Kota Formation records a freshwater fluvial environment of Early Jurassic Gondwana in what is now southern India. The accumulation of multiple Kotasaurus individuals at a single site may reflect seasonal drought deaths near water sources, consistent with other large sauropod bone assemblages. Associated fauna includes fish and other aquatic vertebrates.

Postcranial material from more than twelve individuals was recovered from this Early Jurassic fluvial deposit near the village of Yamanpalli.

The lower Kota Formation is usually treated as Early Jurassic and often Toarcian, but radiometric control is limited.

## Open questions

- Cranial anatomy is essentially unknown.
- Formation age, element association and exact basal sauropod position remain uncertain.
