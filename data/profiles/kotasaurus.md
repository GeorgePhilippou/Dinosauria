## Overview

Kotasaurus was one of the first true sauropods, the group of long-necked, four-legged plant-eaters that later produced the largest land animals of all time. It lived in central India during the Early Jurassic, when India was still joined to Africa, Antarctica and Australia as part of Gondwana.

The genus was named in 1988 by the Indian palaeontologist P. Yadagiri from a bonebed near the village of Yamanpalli. The site preserves the jumbled skeletons of at least a dozen animals, which together give a good picture of the body even though no single skeleton is complete. A composite skeleton is mounted at the Birla Science Centre in Hyderabad.

## Anatomy & life

Kotasaurus already had the column-like limbs and heavy build of a sauropod, and it walked on all fours. At the same time it kept several features of its smaller, earlier relatives: the thigh bone retains a distinct lesser trochanter, a muscle-attachment crest that later sauropods lost, and the vertebrae lack the deep air-filled hollows of more advanced forms.

Its few known teeth are broad and spoon-shaped, suited to cropping vegetation. Nothing is known of the shape of its head, and the length of the neck is inferred from related early sauropods rather than from a complete series of neck vertebrae.

- One of the earliest sauropods known, from the Early Jurassic of Gondwana.
- A mix of advanced sauropod limbs and older, prosauropod-like features.
- Known from a single bonebed of many individuals rather than one skeleton.

## Fossil record

All known Kotasaurus material comes from one bonebed at Yamanpalli, where hundreds of disarticulated bones accumulated in an ancient river channel. The name-bearing specimen is a single hip bone; the other bones are referred to the species because they come from the same deposit and show consistent anatomy.

Because the bones are mixed together, individual skeletons cannot be reassembled, and the association of every element with Kotasaurus rather than another animal has to be assumed. The skull is represented by little more than isolated teeth.

## Where it lived

The Kota Formation formed in rivers and lakes in the Pranhita–Godavari Basin. It is generally regarded as Early Jurassic, and often as Toarcian, but its age rests mainly on fossil fishes, ostracods and plants rather than on radiometric dates. The same rocks have yielded the larger sauropod Barapasaurus, fishes, lizards, pterosaurs and some of the earliest mammals known from India.

## Classification

Kotasaurus is a sauropod that falls outside Eusauropoda, the group containing nearly all later sauropods. Most analyses place it near other early forms such as Vulcanodon and Barapasaurus, although its exact position among them changes from study to study because of the limited cranial material and the mixed nature of the bonebed.

## Open questions

### How old is the Kota Formation?

Estimates range across much of the Early Jurassic, from the Sinemurian to the Toarcian. Without radiometric dates, it is unclear how Kotasaurus compares in age with early sauropods elsewhere.

### Where exactly does it fit among early sauropods?

Its position relative to Vulcanodon, Barapasaurus and other early forms varies between analyses.

### What did its head look like?

No informative skull bones are known, so any reconstruction of the head is based on relatives.
