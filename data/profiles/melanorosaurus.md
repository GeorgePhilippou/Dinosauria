## Overview

Melanorosaurus was a large, early sauropodomorph dinosaur that lived during the Late Triassic to earliest Jurassic, approximately 227–210 million years ago, in what is now the upper Elliot Formation of South Africa. It is estimated to have reached around 8–12 metres in length, making it one of the largest land animals of its time, with a body plan that was transitioning toward the fully quadrupedal sauropods. The type species is Melanorosaurus readi.

Melanorosaurus is phylogenetically significant as one of the most primitive taxa positioned close to the origin of true sauropods. Its robust limb bones and large body size suggest it was approaching obligate quadrupedality, unlike the more slender, facultatively bipedal early sauropodomorphs. It is thus an important taxon for understanding the evolutionary transition from small, bipedal prosauropods to the giant, fully quadrupedal sauropods that dominated Jurassic and Cretaceous ecosystems.

## Anatomy & life

Melanorosaurus is considered one of the most derived non-sauropod sauropodomorphs, positioned close to the evolutionary origin of true sauropods. Its limb bones are robust and columnar, more consistent with a quadrupedal posture than the slender, cursorial limbs of earlier prosauropods. This makes it an important transitional taxon for understanding how the giant quadrupedal body plan of sauropods first evolved.

Melanorosaurus gives its name to the family Melanorosauridae, a group of large, late Triassic to early Jurassic sauropodomorphs that included other large-bodied forms such as Riojasaurus from Argentina and Eucnemesaurus from South Africa. Whether Melanorosauridae is a natural (monophyletic) group or a grade of early sauropodomorphs convergently acquiring similar features is debated among researchers.

The Elliot Formation of South Africa preserves one of the best records of Late Triassic and earliest Jurassic dinosaurs in Africa, documenting the early diversification of major dinosaur lineages. Melanorosaurus shared its environment with early ornithischians and smaller prosauropods, as well as non-dinosaurian reptiles. Its large body size may reflect early exploitation of megaherbivore ecological niches shortly after dinosaurs first evolved.

## Fossil record

Melanorosaurus readi was named in 1924 by Sydney Haughton based on partial limb and vertebral material from the Elliot Formation of South Africa. The genus name means 'Black Mountain lizard', named after the Thaba Nyama (Black Mountain) region of the Transkei where the fossils were found. The species name honours Read, who collected the original material. Additional specimens including a nearly complete skeleton were described in the 2000s.

Fragmentary name-bearing postcranial material, with additional historical referrals of unequal security.

- A modern redescription designated a lectotype from the original syntype material.
- The well-known skull once assigned to Melanorosaurus was not part of the type and should not be used uncritically in reconstructions.
- Its robust limbs support habitual terrestrial quadrupedal capacity, while exact gait remains biomechanically debated.

## Where it lived

The holotype and several referred specimens of Melanorosaurus readi were recovered from Late Triassic redbeds in the Karoo Basin.

The type material is from the lower Elliot Formation and is treated as Norian; older generic ranges assembled from referrals are not accepted.

## Open questions

- The referral of several historical specimens remains unresolved.
- Its exact position close to Sauropoda varies among matrices.
