## Overview

Chungkingosaurus was a small stegosaurid dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of Sichuan Province, China, approximately 168–162 million years ago (Bathonian to Callovian), found in the Shaximiao Formation. Reaching about 4 metres in length, it was a quadrupedal herbivore bearing pairs of dorsal plates and tail spikes typical of stegosaurs. Its name means 'Chongqing lizard', after the major city (formerly spelled Chungking) near its discovery site.

Named by Dong Zhiming, Zhou Shiwu, and Zhang Yihong in 1983, Chungkingosaurus jiangbeiensis is placed within Stegosauridae, subfamily Stegosaurinae. Known from a partial skull and partial skeleton, it is one of several stegosaurs from the Middle to Late Jurassic of Sichuan, a region remarkable for its diverse stegosaur fauna that also includes Tuojiangosaurus and Huayangosaurus. Its discovery contributed to the understanding of early stegosaur diversity in Asia.

## Anatomy & life

Like other stegosaurines, Chungkingosaurus bore pairs of bony plates along its back and tail spikes (thagomizer). Its plates were relatively narrow and tall compared to the broader plates of Stegosaurus. The partial skull indicates a small head with leaf-shaped teeth suited for cropping vegetation. At 4 metres long, it was a smaller member of the stegosaur family.

The Shaximiao Formation of Sichuan is globally significant for its stegosaur diversity. Alongside Chungkingosaurus, the formation has yielded Tuojiangosaurus and the more primitive Huayangosaurus, as well as diverse sauropods and theropods. This stegosaur assemblage suggests that the group diversified extensively in Asia during the Middle to Late Jurassic before spreading to other continents.

## Fossil record

Chungkingosaurus jiangbeiensis was described in 1983 by Dong Zhiming, Zhou Shiwu, and Zhang Yihong from material collected in the Shaximiao Formation near Chongqing (then Romanised as Chungking), Sichuan. The species name refers to the Jiangbei district of Chongqing. The material includes a partial skull and partial postcranial skeleton.

One partial skull and incomplete postcranial skeleton with dermal armour, plus uncertain referrals.

- The taxon remains recognised in recent reassessments, but its anatomy and position among stegosaurs are incompletely resolved.
- Tail spikes are preserved in the assemblage, although their exact number and arrangement on the living animal are uncertain.

## Where it lived

The Shaximiao Formation ecosystem was a warm, well-watered floodplain environment during the Middle to Late Jurassic. Chungkingosaurus shared this landscape with large sauropods like Omeisaurus and Datousaurus, smaller ornithopods, and theropods such as Gasosaurus. As a small stegosaur, it likely fed on low-growing vegetation and used its tail spikes defensively against predators.

Middle to Late Jurassic fluvial formation in Sichuan yielding the type material of Chungkingosaurus and associated stegosaurs.

The upper Shaximiao Formation is Late Jurassic, commonly Oxfordian.

## Open questions

- The holotype mount contains substantial restoration.
- Some referred specimens may represent other stegosaurs.
- A fully peer-reviewed modern monograph is lacking.
