## Overview

Marshosaurus was a medium-sized carnivorous theropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic (Kimmeridgian to Tithonian), approximately 155–148 million years ago, in what is now Utah and Colorado, USA. It was a moderately built predator estimated at around 4–5 metres in length, living alongside many of the famous dinosaurs of the Morrison Formation including Allosaurus, Stegosaurus, and large sauropods. The type species is Marshosaurus bicentesimus.

Named in 1976 by James Madsen in commemoration of the United States Bicentennial celebration, Marshosaurus is known from partial skull and postcranial material recovered from the Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry in Utah. Its classification has been debated, with it placed at various times within Megalosauria and later within Tetanurae. Its fragmentary material limits detailed interpretation, but it contributes to our understanding of the diversity of medium-sized predators that coexisted with the dominant Allosaurus in the Morrison ecosystem.

## Anatomy & life

The Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry, where most Marshosaurus material was found, is notable for an extraordinary concentration of Allosaurus remains alongside many other Morrison dinosaurs. The cause of this bone accumulation — whether a predator trap, drought-related mortality, or some other taphonomic process — has been debated for decades. Regardless of its origin, the quarry provides an exceptional sample of Late Jurassic theropod diversity.

## Fossil record

Marshosaurus bicentesimus was described by James Madsen Jr. in 1976, based on material from the Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry in Utah. The genus is named after the famous American palaeontologist Othniel Charles Marsh, one of the central figures in the nineteenth-century 'Bone Wars', while bicentesimus commemorates the bicentennial of the United States in 1976. The material includes pelvic bones, a partial skull, and other postcranial elements.

Holotype partial ilium plus fragmentary referred skull, vertebral and limb material.

## Where it lived

Marshosaurus inhabited the Late Jurassic Morrison Formation, one of the richest dinosaur-bearing deposits in the world. The ecosystem included apex predators such as Allosaurus and Torvosaurus, as well as other medium-sized theropods. Marshosaurus likely occupied a mesopredator niche, possibly targeting smaller prey or juveniles of large dinosaurs, though its diet cannot be directly inferred from available material.

The Cleveland-Lloyd Dinosaur Quarry is one of the most productive Jurassic dinosaur sites in the world; Marshosaurus material was recovered from its dense bone accumulation.

## Classification

The classification of Marshosaurus has been revised multiple times since its original description. It was initially placed in Megalosauria but later analyses suggested a tetanuran position outside Megalosauria proper. The fragmentary nature of the material means that its precise phylogenetic position remains poorly resolved. Some reviews have questioned whether all material assigned to Marshosaurus actually belongs to the same taxon.

## Open questions
