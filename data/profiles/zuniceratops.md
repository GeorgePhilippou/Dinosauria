## Overview

Zuniceratops ('Zuni horned face') was a ceratopsian dinosaur from the Turonian stage of the Late Cretaceous in what is now New Mexico, United States, living approximately 94–89 million years ago. The type and only species, Zuniceratops christopheri, represents one of the oldest known ceratopsians with true brow horns, predating the North American horned ceratopsids by several million years. Its remains come from the Moreno Hill Formation.

Named by Wolfe and Kirkland in 1998, Zuniceratops is placed within Neoceratopsia, close to the origin of the large-frilled Coronosauria that includes Centrosaurinae and Chasmosaurinae. Its discovery was significant because it demonstrated that brow horns evolved in North American ceratopsians well before the classic 'horned dinosaur' radiation of the Campanian. Zuniceratops was described after discovery by eight-year-old Christopher Wolfe, after whom the species is named.

## Anatomy & life

The Moreno Hill Formation preserves one of the few windows into Turonian (approximately 94–89 million years ago) dinosaur faunas of North America, a poorly sampled interval between the well-known Cenomanian and Campanian faunas. Alongside Zuniceratops, the formation has yielded early hadrosaurs and other taxa, suggesting that the major North American dinosaur groups of the Late Cretaceous were already establishing themselves during the Turonian.

## Fossil record

Zuniceratops christopheri was named in 1998 by Douglas Wolfe and James Kirkland, based on specimens discovered in the Moreno Hill Formation of New Mexico. Remarkably, the first specimen was found in 1996 by Christopher Wolfe, the eight-year-old son of palaeontologist Douglas Wolfe, and the species name christopheri honours this discovery. The genus name references the Zuni people, whose ancestral homelands include the New Mexico region where the fossils were found.

Several partial skulls with sparse postcranial referrals.

- It had well-developed brow horns but no large nasal horn.
- The frill shows an early stage toward ceratopsid anatomy; it is close to, but outside, Ceratopsidae in most analyses.

## Classification

Zuniceratops is notable as one of the earliest ceratopsians to possess genuine brow (supraorbital) horns, derived from the postorbital bones. This places it as an important data point for understanding when and where the elaborate cranial ornamentation of the large ceratopsids evolved. Its horns were relatively modest compared to later forms like Triceratops or Styracosaurus, suggesting a gradual increase in horn elaboration through the later Cretaceous ceratopsian radiation.

Zuniceratops is placed within Coronosauria, the ceratopsian subgroup that includes the large-frilled and multi-horned Ceratopsidae. Its phylogenetic position just outside or at the base of Ceratopsidae makes it informative for reconstructing the ancestral body plan of the major ceratopsid radiation that characterises the Campanian of North America. It lacks the elaborate nasal horn of centrosaurines and the long parietal frills of chasmosaurines, representing a more generalised condition.

## Open questions

- Postcranial anatomy and exact position close to Ceratopsidae remain uncertain.
