## Overview

Juravenator (meaning 'Jura hunter') was a small carnivorous theropod from the Late Jurassic of Bavaria, Germany, living approximately 152–149 million years ago. Known from a nearly complete skeleton of a juvenile individual recovered from the Painten Formation (Weißjura group) of Bavaria near Kelheim, it reached an estimated 75–80 centimetres in length for the known specimen (adult size uncertain). It was named by Göhlich and Chiappe in 2006 and is placed within Compsognathidae.

Juravenator is notable for preserving impressions of scaly skin along its tail, which was remarkable because closely related compsognathids from the same Solnhofen limestone environment were known to bear feathers. This initially suggested Juravenator lacked feathers, but subsequent study indicated that feathers may have been present elsewhere on the body and that the tail preserved only scaly integument. The fossil represents the second non-avian theropod found in the Solnhofen region after Compsognathus.

## Anatomy & life

The holotype preserves impressions of scales along parts of the tail — an unexpected finding for a compsognathid, given that feathers are known in related theropods. Later analysis suggested that feathers may have been present on parts of the body not preserved, and that scaly tail integument coexisted with feathered regions. This mosaic condition raises questions about the distribution of integument types across the body in small coelurosaurs.

Juravenator contributed substantially to debates about the distribution and evolution of feathers in non-avian theropods. Its skin impressions show that not all small coelurosaurs were wholly feathered, or at least that feather distribution varied across the body. As a Solnhofen compsognathid, it also adds to the broader picture of small theropod diversity in Late Jurassic Europe.

## Fossil record

Juravenator starki was described in 2006 by Ursula Göhlich and Luis Chiappe from a nearly complete, articulated skeleton of a juvenile animal found near Schamhaupten in Bavaria. The genus name refers to the Jura mountain range and the species is named after the Stark family, who owned the quarry. It is only the second non-avian theropod known from the Solnhofen region.

One nearly complete juvenile skeleton with soft-tissue impressions.

## Where it lived

The Solnhofen limestone environment of Late Jurassic Bavaria was a shallow tropical archipelago with lagoons, subject to seasonal drying. This anaerobic environment enabled exceptional preservation of soft tissues. Juravenator inhabited the surrounding landmasses and was likely a predator of small vertebrates and invertebrates in this island-rich setting.

Part of the Solnhofen limestone complex that has produced Archaeopteryx and Compsognathus; the Juravenator specimen was recovered from Schamhaupten quarries.

## Open questions

- Adult anatomy is unknown.
- Its exact coelurosaur position and distribution of integument over the body remain debated.
