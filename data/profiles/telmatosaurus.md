## Overview

Telmatosaurus (meaning 'swamp lizard') was a hadrosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Europe, living approximately 84–66 million years ago. It reached about 5 metres in length and is known from fragmentary remains collected across France, Romania, and Spain. Named by Nopcsa in 1900, Telmatosaurus transsylvanicus is notable as one of the best-represented hadrosaurs from Europe and has been interpreted as a dwarf form, reduced in size compared to its North American and Asian relatives due to the island conditions that characterised Late Cretaceous Europe.

Telmatosaurus is significant because it is regarded by some researchers as a relatively primitive (basal) hadrosaurid, retaining features that more derived hadrosaurs lost. Its European distribution, combined with its potentially basal phylogenetic position, has contributed to hypotheses about hadrosaur migration routes from Asia or North America into Europe via island-hopping during the Late Cretaceous. It is also notable for evidence of a tumour (a benign multilobular osteochondroma) found on a jaw fragment — one of the earliest cancer-like conditions reported in a dinosaur.

## Anatomy & life

At approximately 5 metres, Telmatosaurus is considerably smaller than its closest continental relatives, which routinely exceeded 10 metres. This size reduction is attributed to insular dwarfism — the evolutionary reduction of body size in island populations where resource availability is limited and predation pressure may be relaxed. Nopcsa himself was the first to propose this explanation for the Hateg fauna, a remarkably prescient insight for the early 20th century.

## Fossil record

Telmatosaurus transsylvanicus was described by Franz Baron Nopcsa in 1900 from material collected in Transylvania (now Romania). Nopcsa, an aristocratic Hungarian-Romanian palaeontologist, was a pioneer in studying European dinosaurs and was among the first to propose that many Hateg Basin dinosaurs were island dwarfs. The genus name references its presumed swampy habitat; the species name refers to Transylvania, the historical region where the fossils were found.

Several partial skulls and skeletons from multiple individuals.

## Where it lived

A jaw fragment attributed to Telmatosaurus has been identified as bearing a multilobular osteochondroma — a benign cartilage-capped bone tumour still seen in humans and other vertebrates today. This represents one of the earliest known occurrences of a tumour-like growth in a non-avian dinosaur, and contributes to a growing body of evidence that dinosaurs suffered from many of the same pathological conditions as living animals.

The most complete Telmatosaurus material comes from the Maastrichtian Hateg Basin of Romania, famous for its community of island-dwelling dwarf dinosaurs.

## Classification

Telmatosaurus has been placed as a basal hadrosaurid in several phylogenetic analyses, meaning it branched off near the root of the family before the saurolophine and lambeosaurine subfamilies diverged. This basal position, combined with its European location, suggests that Telmatosaurus may represent an early dispersal of hadrosaurs into Europe before the group's later diversification. However, some analyses place it within or close to Hadrosaurinae, and its exact position remains debated.

## Open questions

- Species referrals and the degree of island dwarfism remain debated.
