## Overview

Isisaurus (meaning 'ISI lizard', honouring the Indian Statistical Institute) was a large herbivorous titanosaur sauropod from the Late Cretaceous of India, living approximately 72–66 million years ago. Known from the Lameta Formation of Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh, it is notable for its unusual proportions: its neck was relatively short and directed more nearly vertically compared to most sauropods, while its forelimbs were long, giving it a giraffe-like posture. It was reclassified by Wilson and Upchurch in 2003 from the original genus Titanosaurus colberti. Size is not firmly established but it was a large animal.

Isisaurus is placed within Saltasauroidea > Lithostrotia, a derived group of titanosaurs. Its distinctive vertical neck posture has been compared to that of modern giraffes, enabling it to browse at considerable height. Coprolites (fossilised dung) attributed to Isisaurus or related taxa from the Lameta Formation have been found to contain fragments of rotted wood, fungus, and plant material, providing rare direct evidence of dietary content in Cretaceous sauropods. It was closely associated with Indosuchus in the same formation.

## Anatomy & life

Isisaurus is distinguished among titanosaurs by its unusually short, nearly vertically oriented neck and long forelimbs. This posture has been likened to a modern giraffe, and would have allowed it to browse at treetop height. This contrasts with most sauropods whose necks were held more horizontally, suggesting diverse feeding strategies among Late Cretaceous titanosaurs.

Coprolites associated with titanosaur sauropods from the Lameta Formation have been reported to contain fragments of decaying wood, fungus, conifers, and palms. If attributable to Isisaurus or a close relative, this would make the Lameta coprolites among the best direct dietary evidence for any non-avian dinosaur, indicating a broad plant diet including coarse woody material.

Isisaurus is one of the better-known titanosaurs from the Indian subcontinent, contributing to understanding of sauropod diversity during the final stages of the Cretaceous on the Indian plate. Its phylogenetic position within Saltasauroidea links it to South American forms, providing evidence for continued faunal connections across Gondwana even as India drifted northward.

## Fossil record

Material now assigned to Isisaurus was originally described as Titanosaurus colberti by Jain and Bandyopadhyay in 1997, honouring vertebrate palaeontologist Edwin Colbert. Wilson and Upchurch reclassified it as Isisaurus colberti in 2003, creating a new genus to honour the Indian Statistical Institute (ISI), which has been a major contributor to Indian palaeontology.

One associated, substantial but incomplete postcranial skeleton with very limited cranial evidence.

## Open questions

- The skull, distal tail and parts of the skeleton are missing.
- Relationships within Lithostrotia and the precise Lameta horizon remain uncertain.
