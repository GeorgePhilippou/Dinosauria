## Overview

Dubreuillosaurus was a large carnivorous theropod dinosaur from the Middle Jurassic of Normandy, France, living approximately 169–164 million years ago during the Bathonian stage. Placed within the family Megalosauridae, it is one of relatively few well-attested large theropods from Jurassic Europe, a landmass that at the time consisted of a series of shallow island archipelagos. The exact body size of Dubreuillosaurus remains uncertain because the known material — partial skull and skeleton — is insufficient for confident length estimates.

Dubreuillosaurus valesdunensis was formally described by Ronan Allain in 2005 after the original material had been previously referred to Poekilopleuron. PBDB data places it within Eustreptospondylinae (family Megalosauridae), aligning it with the broadly similar European megalosaurids that inhabited the Jurassic islands of what would become western Europe. Its description illuminated the diversity of mid-Jurassic theropods in Europe.

## Anatomy & life

During the Bathonian, western Europe was a mosaic of shallow tropical seas and low-lying islands, and the large theropods that evolved there showed some degree of insularity. Dubreuillosaurus shared this archipelago environment with sauropods, smaller ornithischians, and marine reptiles. The ecology of these Jurassic island ecosystems remains only partially understood.

Unlike many theropods, the total body length of Dubreuillosaurus cannot be confidently estimated from available material. NHM notes the specimen quality is insufficient to determine overall size, though the presence of large limb bones indicates it was a substantial animal. Future discoveries of more complete material would significantly improve understanding of this taxon.

## Fossil record

The material that would become Dubreuillosaurus was initially described as Poekilopleuron by earlier researchers, but a re-examination by Ronan Allain (2005) established that it represented a distinct taxon. The genus name honours the Dubreuillosaurus quarry near Valen-des-Unies in Calvados, and the species name valesdunensis refers to the locality. The holotype includes a partial skull and fragmentary postcranial material.

One associated partial skull and substantial incomplete postcranial skeleton.

## Where it lived

The only known locality for Dubreuillosaurus; Bathonian-age limestone deposits near the town of Valen-des-Ducs in Normandy.

## Classification

Dubreuillosaurus is placed within Eustreptospondylinae, a subfamily of Megalosauridae that includes the slightly younger English theropod Eustreptospondylus. Megalosaurids were among the dominant large predators of the Middle Jurassic and represent an important grade of tetanuran theropods before the rise of carcharodontosaurids and tyrannosaurids in the Cretaceous.
