## Overview

Panoplosaurus (meaning 'fully-armoured lizard') was a nodosaurid ankylosaur from the Late Cretaceous (middle Campanian, approximately 76–75 million years ago) of Alberta, Canada, with some material tentatively recorded from Montana and Texas. It was approximately 5–7 metres long and weighed around 1,600 kg, a quadrupedal herbivore covered in bony osteoderms, scutes and plates. Its skull was short-snouted with a domed surface and bony plates directly covering the cheeks. Fossils come primarily from the Dinosaur Park Formation.

Named by Lawrence Lambe in 1919, Panoplosaurus mirus has had a complex synonymy history; at times it was considered conspecific with Edmontonia, and some specimens originally referred to Panoplosaurus have been reidentified. The Wikipedia extract confirms it is distinct from Edmontonia based on features including a fused shoulder blade, lumpy skull osteoderms, and regularly shaped neck and body plates lacking prominent spines. It is an important taxon for understanding the diversity of North American nodosaurids.

## Anatomy & life

Panoplosaurus had one of the most extensive armour systems among nodosaurids. The skull was covered in lumpy osteoderms, the neck bore circular groups of plates, and the body was almost completely covered in scutes and ossicles of varying sizes. Unlike ankylosaurids, it lacked a tail club, and prominent body spines were also absent, distinguishing it from some other nodosaurids such as Edmontonia.

For much of the twentieth century, material referred to Panoplosaurus was considered synonymous with or closely related to Edmontonia. More recent analyses treating Panoplosaurus as a valid taxon distinct from Edmontonia are based on differences in skull osteoderm texture, shoulder anatomy and plate morphology. The Wikipedia entry notes this controversy and documents the distinct diagnostic characters now used to separate the two genera.

In the Dinosaur Park Formation ecosystem, Panoplosaurus was one of several large armoured herbivores coexisting with hadrosaurs, ceratopsians and large theropods such as Gorgosaurus. Its extensive passive armour would have been effective against attacks from such predators. Body mass estimates around 1,600 kg indicate it was a substantial animal despite its relatively modest length.

## Fossil record

Panoplosaurus mirus was discovered in 1917 and named by Lawrence Lambe in 1919. The genus name means 'fully-armoured lizard' in Greek, describing the extensive osteoderm covering. Lambe based the species on material from the Dinosaur Park Formation of Alberta during the productive early-twentieth-century period of Canadian dinosaur collecting.

Complete holotype skull with partial skeleton and armour; further referred skeletons.

- CT redescription supports separation from the related Edmontonia.
- It carried extensive osteoderms but no ankylosaurid-style tail club is known.
- Quadrupedal herbivory is secure; exact armour layout and body mass remain reconstructed.

## Where it lived

Middle Campanian floodplain deposits of southern Alberta, the primary source of confirmed Panoplosaurus material.

The Dinosaur Park Formation type and secure referrals are late Campanian.
