## Overview

Ampelosaurus atacis was a titanosaurian sauropod from the Late Cretaceous of southern France and Spain, living approximately 71–66 million years ago. Estimated at about 15 metres in length, it is known from teeth, postcranial bones, and notably osteoderms — bony dermal armour — preserved in the Marnes Rouges Inférieures Formation of the Aude region. It inhabited the coastal lowlands of the Ibero-Armorican island landmass during the final stages of the Cretaceous.

Named by Le Loeuff in 1995, Ampelosaurus is classified within Lirainosaurinae, a group of European titanosaurs. The presence of osteoderms in an otherwise modestly-sized titanosaur makes it a valuable taxon for studying the evolution of armour in sauropods. It is one of the best-known European Late Cretaceous sauropods, with material recovered from multiple sites in France and Spain.

## Anatomy & life

Ampelosaurus was a medium-sized titanosaur that lived in southern France around 71 million years ago, when much of Europe was an archipelago of islands. It had the classic sauropod build of a long neck and tail, a deep body and pillar-like legs, and its skin was studded with bony osteoderms of several shapes.

It is known from the Bellevue bonebed near Campagne-sur-Aude, where hundreds of bones from many individuals accumulated. Vertebrae, girdle bones, limb bones, teeth and osteoderms are all represented, and cranial material including a braincase has been attributed to the genus.

The wealth of material makes Ampelosaurus one of the best-sampled European titanosaurs, but the bones are disarticulated and may not all belong to one species. Bone histology shows slow, prolonged growth, and mature size remains an estimate that depends on which isolated limb bones are associated together.

- Hundreds of bones from the Bellevue bonebed document most regions of the skeleton.
- Plate-, bulb- and spine-shaped osteoderms show that the skin carried bony armour of more than one form.
- Bone microstructure records slow, extended growth compared with many other sauropods.

**Feeding: Plant-eater.** Slender, peg-like teeth and the long neck fit a browsing herbivore. The specific plants eaten are not preserved.

**Movement: Quadrupedal.** Robust, columnar limb bones show habitual four-legged walking.

**Appearance: Armoured skin confirmed.** Osteoderm shapes are known, but none has been found in place on the body, so their layout is reconstructed.

## Fossil record

Ampelosaurus is anchored by three articulated dorsal vertebrae from Bellevue. The same bonebed has yielded hundreds of disarticulated bones, teeth and osteoderms from multiple individuals, which together document most of the skeleton without preserving any single complete animal.

Vertebrae from the neck, back and tail, girdle and limb bones, teeth, cranial elements including a braincase, and osteoderms of plate, bulb and spine form, all recovered from the Bellevue bonebed.

Because the bones were not found in association, assigning every element to Ampelosaurus, and to a particular individual, depends on interpretation rather than direct observation.

## Where it lived

The type locality of Ampelosaurus, yielding teeth, vertebrae, limb bones, and dermal osteoderms that define the genus.

## Classification

Ampelosaurus is a lithostrotian titanosaur, usually placed among the European forms grouped as Lirainosaurinae. Its exact relationships within Lithostrotia vary among analyses.

## Open questions

### Is there more than one titanosaur at Bellevue?

Later studies have suggested that the bonebed may contain a second morphotype, so not every bone can be assumed to be Ampelosaurus.

### Which bones belonged together?

The bones are disarticulated and come from many individuals. Reconstructing one animal means associating elements that were not found connected.

### How were the osteoderms arranged?

Several osteoderm shapes are known, but their positions on the body have not been observed and remain reconstructed.
