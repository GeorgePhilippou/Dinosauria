## Overview

Omeisaurus (meaning 'Omei lizard') was a large sauropod dinosaur from the Middle to Late Jurassic (approximately 175–143 million years ago) of Sichuan Province and adjacent regions of China. Fossils are known from the Shaximiao Formation and related deposits of Sichuan, Gansu and Chongqing. Multiple species have been named. Estimates of body length reach about 20 metres. It was a quadrupedal herbivore that moved in gregarious groups.

Described by Young (C.C. Young) in 1939, Omeisaurus is placed in Mamenchisauridae, a family of East Asian sauropods characterised by very elongated necks. PBDB confirms its family placement as Mamenchisauridae. Omeisaurus is among the most abundant sauropods from the Chinese Jurassic fossil record and has been important for understanding East Asian sauropod diversity, with multiple well-preserved specimens from the Dashanpu quarry site near Zigong, Sichuan.

## Anatomy & life

Like other mamenchisaurids, Omeisaurus had an exceptionally long neck relative to its body, formed by elongated cervical vertebrae. The neck may have accounted for nearly half the total body length in some species, potentially serving as an adaptation for browsing at heights or angles unavailable to shorter-necked herbivores. Neck length estimates vary among species.

Several species have been assigned to Omeisaurus over the decades, including O. junghsiensis, O. tianfuensis, O. luoquanensis and others. Their validity and interrelationships are subjects of ongoing study. Some phylogenetic analyses suggest the genus is paraphyletic as currently defined, and future revision may affect how many distinct species are recognised.

## Fossil record

Omeisaurus junghsiensis was described by the Chinese palaeontologist C.C. Young in 1939 from material recovered near Mount Emei (Omei Shan) in Sichuan. Subsequent excavations at Dashanpu, Zigong, have produced multiple additional species and some of the most complete sauropod skeletons known from China, substantially expanding knowledge of the genus.

Multiple partial to substantial skeletons assigned to several species; some cranial material.

- Material attributed to different species collectively covers much of the skeleton, including skulls in some species.
- The tail-club often shown in reconstructions is based on referred material whose species association is uncertain.
- Long neck and quadrupedal herbivory are well supported broadly, but measurements must remain species-specific.

## Where it lived

Omeisaurus co-existed with other iconic Chinese Jurassic dinosaurs at Dashanpu, including the stegosaur Huayangosaurus, the sauropod Datousaurus, the ornithopod Xiaosaurus, and the theropod Gasosaurus. The Dashanpu quarry has been called the richest Jurassic dinosaur locality in Asia. This diverse fauna offers a detailed picture of a Middle Jurassic ecosystem.

Middle Jurassic deposits of the Sichuan Basin, including the Dashanpu quarry, yielding numerous Omeisaurus specimens.

The reviewed range follows Middle Jurassic type and core species; later referrals are not used to extend the genus automatically.

## Open questions

- The genus is probably oversplit or non-monophyletic.
- Several historical specimens lack rigorous species-level reassessment.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
