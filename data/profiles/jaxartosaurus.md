## Overview

Jaxartosaurus (meaning 'Jaxartes River lizard') was a herbivorous lambeosaurine hadrosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Kazakhstan and China, living approximately 90–84 million years ago. Known only from a skull roof and braincase, it is one of the more fragmentarily represented hadrosaurs from Central Asia. Its estimated length of around 9 metres is based on comparisons with related lambeosaurines. It was named by Anatoly Nikolaevich Riabinin in 1939 from material found in the Dabrazhin Formation (Coniacian) of Kazakhstan.

Jaxartosaurus is placed within Lambeosaurinae, the hollow-crested hadrosaurs, suggesting it may have borne a crest similar to those of Parasaurolophus or Corythosaurus. However, the limited material makes the precise crest morphology unknown. Its Central Asian location is significant for understanding hadrosaur biogeography: as hadrosaurs diversified across Laurasia in the Late Cretaceous, Central Asian forms like Jaxartosaurus may represent early members of lineages that later dispersed into both Asia and North America.

## Anatomy & life

Only a skull roof and braincase are known for Jaxartosaurus, making detailed anatomical reconstruction impossible. The elements present indicate a large hadrosaur and are consistent with lambeosaurine affinities, potentially suggesting a cranial crest, though its form remains unknown. Body size estimates of around 9 metres are extrapolated from related taxa.

Jaxartosaurus is one of the few lambeosaurine hadrosaurs documented from Central Asia, and its geographic position is informative for understanding hadrosaur dispersal routes between Asia and North America during the Late Cretaceous. Despite its fragmentary nature, it remains a biogeographically important taxon in studies of Cretaceous hadrosaur diversity.

## Fossil record

Jaxartosaurus aralensis was described in 1939 by A.N. Riabinin from skull material found in Kazakhstan. The genus name refers to the Jaxartes River (modern Syr Darya), near which the fossils were found, and the species name references the Aral Sea region. Additional material has been tentatively referred to Jaxartosaurus from Xinjiang, China.

Incomplete type skull with fragmentary postcranial material from the same dinosaur-bearing deposit.

## Where it lived

The Coniacian deposits of Kazakhstan record Late Cretaceous terrestrial environments on the western margins of the Asian continent. Jaxartosaurus would have inhabited river systems and floodplains in this region, in an ecosystem that likely included other ornithopods, ankylosaurs, and theropods of Late Cretaceous Asia.

The source of the type skull roof and braincase, from Coniacian-age deposits near the ancient Jaxartes (Syr Darya) River.

## Open questions

- The type is fragmentary and lacks a modern comprehensive redescription.
- Crest form, referred postcrania and precise lambeosaurine relationships require specialist review.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
