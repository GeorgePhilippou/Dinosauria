## Overview

Leptoceratops was a small ceratopsian dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian), approximately 68–66 million years ago, in what is now Alberta, Canada, and parts of Montana and Wyoming, USA. Reaching about 3 metres in length, it was a basal ceratopsian that retained the capacity for bipedal locomotion alongside quadrupedal movement, in contrast to the large fully quadrupedal ceratopsids like Triceratops with which it coexisted. It had a small neck frill but lacked the elaborate horns of its larger relatives.

Leptoceratops is the eponymous genus of the family Leptoceratopsidae and is one of the best-known leptoceratopsids, represented by more than ten individuals including complete and nearly complete skeletons. This wealth of material makes it scientifically important for understanding the anatomy of basal ceratopsians and for studying how the more derived horned ceratopsids evolved from smaller, more gracile ancestors.

## Anatomy & life

Unlike the large quadrupedal horned dinosaurs such as Triceratops, Leptoceratops retained hindlimbs proportionally longer than its forelimbs, suggesting it could move bipedally as well as on all fours. It had a small parrot-like beak for cropping vegetation and a modest neck frill without the elaborate ornamentation of derived ceratopsids. Its teeth were well-suited for processing tough plant material.

## Fossil record

Leptoceratops was first found in Alberta in 1910, and the type species Leptoceratops gracilis was named in 1914 by Barnum Brown for partial skull and skeletal material from the Scollard Formation. Charles M. Sternberg uncovered additional specimens in 1947, including one complete and two mostly complete skeletons found together. The genus name means 'slender horned face', reflecting its gracile build relative to other ceratopsians.

Several specimens from Montana originally referred to Leptoceratops have since been transferred to separate genera: Montanoceratops and Cerasinops. This taxonomic revision clarified the true geographic and morphological range of Leptoceratops gracilis. The species is now understood to be restricted to Maastrichtian deposits of Alberta, Montana, and Wyoming.

Secure occurrences are late Maastrichtian in the Scollard, Lance and equivalent latest Cretaceous units.

## Classification

As the eponymous member of Leptoceratopsidae, Leptoceratops occupies an important node in ceratopsian phylogeny, representing the basal grade of neoceratopsians that persisted alongside the much larger, more specialised ceratopsids into the final stages of the Cretaceous. Its survival to the very end of the Cretaceous makes it a contemporary of some of the largest horned dinosaurs.

## Open questions

- Exact locomotor proportions and species-level referral of isolated material remain debated.
- Detailed plant preferences are unknown.
