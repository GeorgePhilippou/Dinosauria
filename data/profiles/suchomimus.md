## Overview

Suchomimus tenerensis was a baryonychine spinosaurid from the Elrhaz Formation of Niger, known from a partial skeleton representing much of the body but assembled from disarticulated material.

Suchomimus tenerensis was a huge spinosaurid from the Early Cretaceous Sahara, with a skull shaped almost exactly like a modern gharial — long, narrow and packed with over 100 interlocking conical teeth suited to gripping fish. A low but distinct neural-spine sail ran along its back. Paul Sereno's team discovered it in Niger's Elrhaz Formation in 1997.

As a close relative of Baryonyx, Suchomimus shares the same fish-specialist bauplan: a long narrow snout, large thumb claw and robust forelimbs. It shared its river-delta world with Nigersaurus, the giant crocodilian Sarcosuchus and a diverse fish fauna in what is now the central Sahara.

## Anatomy & life

Suchomimus was a large predatory dinosaur from what is now the Ténéré Desert of Niger, described in 1998 from bones collected at Gadoufaoua in the Elrhaz Formation. The main skeleton belonged to an animal roughly ten metres long that was not fully grown; published body masses range from about two and a half to more than five tonnes, and none is well constrained.

Its most striking feature is the skull: a very long, narrow snout with a bulbous tip, an upward-kinked jaw line and dozens of conical, finely serrated teeth, all of which suit gripping slippery prey rather than slicing flesh. The forelimbs were powerful, with an enlarged sickle-shaped claw on the thumb, and tall neural spines along the back and hips formed a low ridge or sail.

Suchomimus belongs to Baryonychinae, the branch of Spinosauridae that also includes Baryonyx from England. Its anatomy is so close to Baryonyx that some researchers have questioned whether the two are distinct genera, and it may also be the same animal as Cristatusaurus, named from fragmentary jaws from the same region.

- A crocodile-like snout more than a metre long with conical teeth and a rosette of enlarged teeth at the tip.
- A large, strongly curved claw on the thumb and robust forelimb bones.
- Tall neural spines over the back and hips, forming a low ridge rather than the tall sail of Spinosaurus.

**Feeding: Carnivore.** Conical teeth, a narrow snout and enlarged thumb claws match the piscivorous toolkit of Baryonyx, which preserved fish scales. No gut contents are known for Suchomimus itself.

**Movement: Bipedal.** Hindlimb and pelvic bones support two-legged walking. Whether it swam or waded regularly is debated; the evidence from bone density and limb proportions is contested.

**Appearance: Skull and body outline supported.** The snout, spine ridge and claws are real. Skin, any soft crest, and colour are inferred.

## Fossil record

Suchomimus tenerensis is known chiefly from the holotype, a partial skeleton excavated at Gadoufaoua in 1997, supplemented by a referred snout and other skull pieces from the same locality. Together they cover much of the animal, but no single individual is complete or articulated.

The holotype includes parts of the braincase and jaws, cervical, dorsal, sacral and caudal vertebrae with tall neural spines, ribs, the pectoral girdle, forelimbs with the enlarged thumb claw, pelvis and hindlimb bones. A referred specimen preserves the front of the snout.

The published estimate of about two thirds of the skeleton refers to the holotype as a whole; its bones were scattered, so their association is taphonomic rather than articulated.

Long jaws, conical teeth and large claw support spinosaurid fish-eating adaptations.

- Long jaws, conical teeth and large thumb claws support spinosaurid feeding specialisation.
- Fish consumption is likely by comparison and anatomy, but a 5,000 kg point mass is unsupported.

## Where it lived

Early Cretaceous African river and floodplain settings.

The Elrhaz Formation is generally correlated to about 115-112 Ma around the Aptian-Albian transition; a 125 Ma lower bound is too old.

## Classification

Suchomimus is a baryonychine spinosaurid, a tetanuran theropod closely related to Baryonyx. Some analyses place the two so close together that they may represent a single genus; its relationship to the poorly known Cristatusaurus from the same rocks is also unresolved.

## Open questions

### Is it the same as Cristatusaurus or Baryonyx?

Cristatusaurus was named from jaw fragments from the same formation, and Suchomimus differs from Baryonyx only in a few features. Whether either name should be merged is still argued.

### How heavy was it?

Estimates run from about 2.5 to over 5 tonnes. The skeleton is disarticulated and immature, so no single figure is reliable.

### Did it hunt in the water?

Its anatomy suits catching fish from the shoreline, but recent analyses disagree on whether spinosaurids like Suchomimus were regular swimmers or wading generalists.
