## Overview

Spicomellus (meaning 'spiked collar') was an ankylosaur from the Middle Jurassic of Morocco, living approximately 167–163 million years ago. Known from a partial skeleton lacking a skull and leg bones, it is estimated at around 4 metres in length. Spicomellus afer holds the remarkable distinction of being both the first ankylosaur discovered in Africa and the oldest ankylosaur yet found anywhere in the world, establishing the group's presence on the African continent over 165 million years ago.

Named in a study in which Natural History Museum scientists played a key role, Spicomellus afer was found in the El Mers III Formation in Morocco's Middle Atlas Mountains. The species name afer means 'African' in Latin, reflecting its geographic significance. In the Jurassic, the site was not a highland but part of a vast wetland network. The body would have been covered in spikes of varying sizes, a distinctive feature that sets it apart from many other ankylosaurs known from this interval.

## Anatomy & life

At approximately 168 million years old, Spicomellus is the oldest known ankylosaur. This pushes the origin of the group back considerably and confirms that ankylosaurs were already diversifying by the Middle Jurassic. Its location in Africa also demonstrates that the group had a broad early geographic distribution, challenging the long-held assumption that ankylosaurs originated primarily in Laurasia.

The preserved material of Spicomellus includes osteoderms (armour plates) bearing spikes of varying sizes arranged across the back and flanks. This is consistent with the ankylosaur body plan of a broad, low-slung quadruped with passive bony defences. The absence of skull material prevents determination of whether it was a nodosaurid or ankylosaurid, placing it as an indeterminate ankylosaur at present.

During the Middle Jurassic, the El Mers III area was part of a large wetland and river network in what is now Morocco. Spicomellus shared this environment with sauropod dinosaurs and other vertebrates. The African Gondwanan landmass was still connected to Laurasia via narrow land bridges during this period, potentially explaining how ankylosaurs could have reached Africa from their presumed origin elsewhere.

## Fossil record

Spicomellus afer was named in a study published in 2021 in which Natural History Museum London scientists participated in describing the material. The fossil was found in Morocco's Middle Atlas region in the El Mers III Formation. The genus name references the spiked dog collar (mellus) of Roman antiquity, evoking the spiny armour of the animal; the species name afer celebrates its African provenance.

Holotype rib with fused spikes; second specimen with substantial axial, appendicular and armour material.

- The 2021 holotype is a partial rib with dermal spikes fused directly to its surface.
- A more complete 2025 specimen preserves vertebrae, ribs, limb elements and diverse armour.
- Tail handle vertebrae show that a tail weapon evolved much earlier in ankylosaurs than previously documented.
- Display and defence are plausible functions of the armour, but behaviour is not directly preserved.

## Where it lived

The holotype partial skeleton of Spicomellus was recovered from these Middle Jurassic deposits in the Moroccan Middle Atlas, an environment that was then a wetland plain.

Both described specimens are from the Middle Jurassic El Mers Group of Morocco; the age is formation-level rather than a direct date on each bone.

## Open questions

- The specimens do not preserve a complete skull or skeleton.
- The external arrangement and behavioural functions of every armour element remain partly reconstructed.
