## Overview

Malawisaurus was a medium-sized titanosaurian sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Early Cretaceous (Aptian), approximately 120–110 million years ago, in what is now Malawi in southeastern Africa. It is estimated to have reached around 9 metres in length, making it a modest-sized titanosaur. Unusually among titanosaurs, it is known from cranial material as well as postcranial bones, making it one of the better-documented African titanosaurs. The type species is Malawisaurus dixeyi.

Originally described in 1928 as a species of Gigantosaurus and later reassigned to Tornieria, Malawisaurus was given its current name in 1992 by Jacobs and colleagues, who recognised it as a distinct titanosaur. The recovery of skull material is particularly valuable because titanosaur skulls are extremely rare in the fossil record. Malawisaurus provides important data on African titanosaur evolution and the spread of this clade across Gondwana during the Cretaceous.

## Anatomy & life

Malawisaurus is one of a small number of titanosaurs for which skull material is known, making it scientifically valuable for understanding titanosaur head anatomy. The skull is relatively small and box-like, consistent with the general titanosaur pattern. Additional postcranial bones include vertebrae, ribs, and limb elements, together providing a reasonably comprehensive anatomical picture despite being from multiple individuals.

Africa's record of Cretaceous titanosaurs is patchier than that of South America, but genera like Malawisaurus, Aegyptosaurus, and Paralititan indicate that the continent supported a diverse titanosaur fauna during the Cretaceous. Malawisaurus' Early Cretaceous age makes it one of the earlier well-documented African titanosaurs, helping to document the timing of titanosaur diversification across the fragmenting Gondwana landmass.

## Fossil record

Material now assigned to Malawisaurus was first described in 1928 by Sydney Haughton as a species of Gigantosaurus (now an invalid genus), then later reassigned to Tornieria. In 1992, Louis Jacobs, Dale Winkler, W. R. Downs, and Emma Gomani erected the new genus Malawisaurus for this material, with the genus name honouring Malawi. The species name dixeyi commemorates F. Dixey, a geologist who collected the original fossils.

Composite cranial and postcranial remains from multiple individuals, with reported osteoderms.

- Cranial remains include braincase and jaw elements, unusual completeness for an early titanosaur.
- Associated osteoderms have been reported, although their arrangement is unknown.
- Its placement close to the base of Lithostrotia makes it important for titanosaur evolution.

## Where it lived

During the Early Cretaceous, the region now occupied by Malawi was part of a semi-arid to sub-humid Gondwanan environment. The Dinosaur Beds of Malawi preserve a fauna that, while not as diverse as some other African Cretaceous sites, provides important data on the distribution of large herbivores in sub-equatorial Africa during this period.

Multiple partial skeletons and cranial elements of Malawisaurus dixeyi have been recovered from these Early Cretaceous terrestrial sediments along the western shore of Lake Malawi.

The Dinosaur Beds are treated as Aptian; a point range narrower than formation dating is avoided.

## Open questions

- Bones derive from several individuals and quarry associations are incomplete.
- Precise placement within early Lithostrotia varies.
