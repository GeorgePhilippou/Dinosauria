## Overview

Stegoceras (meaning 'horn roof') was a small dome-headed pachycephalosaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of North America, living approximately 77.5–74 million years ago. It reached about 2.4 metres in length and is characterised by a thick, domed skull roof up to several centimetres thick. Fossils are known from Alberta, Canada, and New Mexico, USA. The type species Stegoceras validum was described by Lambe in 1902, making it one of the first pachycephalosaurs to be scientifically named.

Stegoceras is one of the best-known pachycephalosaurs and has been central to debates about whether the domed skull was used for head-butting behaviour analogous to modern mountain goats and musk oxen. Biomechanical studies have produced conflicting results, with some researchers arguing the structure was well-suited to absorbing impact while others contend the head shape was better adapted for flank-butting or display. The validity of a second species from New Mexico has also been debated.

## Anatomy & life

The most famous question about Stegoceras is whether its thick dome was used in head-to-head combat. Proponents note that the dome's internal structure — with interlocking bony columns — could absorb compressive forces. Critics point out that the dome's outer surface and neck anatomy are not optimally oriented for head-on impacts, suggesting flank-shoving or display as alternative functions. Current consensus is that some form of intraspecific combat or display is likely but precise mechanics remain debated.

Studies of Stegoceras growth series show that the skull dome increased in height and thickness as individuals aged, becoming most pronounced in adults, possibly males. This pattern of ontogenetic change complicates species identification from isolated dome fragments and has contributed to taxonomic revisions within pachycephalosaurs more broadly.

As a herbivore, Stegoceras likely fed on low to mid-level vegetation including leaves, seeds, and possibly fruit. Its teeth were small and leaf-shaped. Stegoceras shared the Dinosaur Park Formation ecosystem with numerous hadrosaurs, ceratopsians, ankylosaurs, and large tyrannosaurs like Gorgosaurus, representing one of the most diverse dinosaur communities ever reconstructed.

## Fossil record

Stegoceras validum was first described by Lawrence Lambe in 1902 from isolated skull domes recovered in Alberta, Canada. Early workers struggled to place pachycephalosaurs correctly within the dinosaur family tree, and for a time the domes were thought to belong to lizards. The genus name means 'horn roof', referencing the thickened skull cap. Over the following decades many additional specimens were collected from the Dinosaur Park Formation.

Numerous skull roofs and several partial skeletons spanning growth stages.

## Where it lived

The majority of Stegoceras material, including the holotype, comes from this prolific Campanian formation in the Red Deer River badlands of Alberta.
