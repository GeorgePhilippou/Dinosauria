## Overview

Sarcosaurus (meaning 'flesh lizard') was a small carnivorous theropod from the Early Jurassic of England, living approximately 201–193 million years ago (Hettangian–Sinemurian). Named by Andrews in 1921, it is estimated to have reached about 3 metres in length and is known from fragmentary remains—including a partial pelvis, femur, and vertebrae—from the Blue Lias and related Early Jurassic formations of Leicestershire and possibly Scotland. It is classified within Ceratosauria.

Sarcosaurus is one of the few theropod body fossils known from the earliest Jurassic of Britain and contributes to understanding early theropod diversity following the end-Triassic mass extinction. Its classification within Ceratosauria is based on pelvic features, but because the material is so fragmentary the assignment is provisional. It was one of several early Jurassic predators that filled niches left by Triassic archosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

Sarcosaurus lived shortly after the end-Triassic mass extinction event (~201 Ma), during a period of rapid ecosystem recovery. Early Jurassic theropods like Sarcosaurus were among the predators recolonising European environments as new faunal assemblages took shape following the extinction of many Triassic archosaur lineages.

Britain's Early Jurassic fauna is known primarily from marine deposits of the Blue Lias and related formations, with terrestrial dinosaur body fossils being rare. Sarcosaurus is thus a valuable, if frustratingly incomplete, representative of the land-dwelling predator community that hunted alongside early crocodylomorphs and small mammals in Hettangian–Sinemurian coastal environments.

## Fossil record

Sarcosaurus woodi was described by Charles William Andrews in 1921 from fragmentary material collected in Leicestershire. The holotype consists of a partial pelvis, femur, and vertebrae. The genus name reflects its role as a predatory 'flesh lizard' of the Early Jurassic.

Fragmentary pelvis and proximal femur with limited referred bones.

## Where it lived

Early Jurassic marine and marginal marine deposits of central England where the Sarcosaurus type material was found.

## Classification

Sarcosaurus is placed within Ceratosauria on the basis of certain pelvic features, but with such limited material the assignment is provisional. Some studies have questioned whether the material is diagnostic at genus level, and no skull or forelimb is known. Additional Early Jurassic British theropod material may eventually clarify its relationships.
