## Overview

Udanoceratops ('Udan Sayr horned face') was a large protoceratopsid or neoceratopsian dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 81–75 million years ago during the Campanian stage. It is known from a partial skull and some postcranial elements collected in the Gobi Desert, and is estimated to have been among the largest members of the protoceratopsid or non-ceratopsid neoceratopsian group. Unusually for a 'ceratopsian' (horned dinosaur), Udanoceratops had a reduced or absent head frill.

Named by Kurzanov in 1992, Udanoceratops tschizhovi is scientifically interesting as a large-bodied basal neoceratopsian from Asia, which contrasts with the more derived and often large-frilled ceratopsid dinosaurs known from North America. Its reduced frill challenges assumptions about frill evolution within Ceratopsia, and its large size for a non-ceratopsid neoceratopsian makes it notable in understanding the diversity of Asian horned dinosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

One of the most notable features of Udanoceratops is its very poorly developed or entirely absent head frill, despite being a member of Ceratopsia, the group most famous for elaborate frills and horns. This suggests that frill elaboration was not a universal trend in Ceratopsia, and that different lineages evolved varying degrees of cranial ornamentation. Udanoceratops may have relied on other means of display or species recognition.

Udanoceratops appears to have been relatively large for a non-ceratopsid neoceratopsian, potentially reaching 3–4 metres in length, though the incomplete material makes precise estimates uncertain. Its body plan would have been broadly similar to Protoceratops — a stocky, quadrupedal herbivore with a parrot-like beak for cropping vegetation — but substantially larger.

The Campanian of Mongolia is remarkably rich in ceratopsian dinosaurs, including the abundant Protoceratops andrewsi and the horned Magnirostris and Turanoceratops from Central Asia. Udanoceratops adds to this diversity by representing a large-bodied form with unusual cranial proportions. Understanding the relationships among these Asian taxa is important for reconstructing the origin and dispersal of ceratopsians into North America.

## Fossil record

Udanoceratops tschizhovi was named by Sergei Kurzanov in 1992 from specimens collected in the Gobi Desert of Mongolia. The generic name references the Udan Sayr locality where it was found, and the species name honours a Russian palaeontologist. The material includes a partial skull that reveals several informative anatomical features despite its incompleteness.

One large incomplete skull with sparse referred postcranium.
