## Overview

Poekilopleuron (meaning 'varied ribs') was a large carnivorous theropod from the Middle Jurassic of what is now Normandy, France, living approximately 168–165 million years ago. Described by Eudes-Deslongchamps in 1837–1838, it is estimated to have reached around 9 metres in length. It is known from a single partial skeleton—including forelimb, hind limb, and vertebral elements—found in the Calcaires de Caen Formation near Caen in Basse-Normandie.

Poekilopleuron belongs to Megalosauridae within the Tetanurae and is currently placed in the subfamily Eustreptospondylinae by some authorities; its PBDB record synonymises it partly with Megalosaurus. The original specimen was also lost during World War II bombing of Caen, leaving science dependent on earlier descriptions and casts. Its large forelimbs were an early observation that informed understanding of megalosauroid anatomy.

## Anatomy & life

Known elements include forelimb bones, vertebrae, pelvic fragments, and hind limb material. The forelimbs were notably robust, consistent with the megalosaurid body plan. Estimates of 9 metres in length make it one of the larger European theropods of the Jurassic, though this is approximate given the partial nature of the skeleton.

## Fossil record

Poekilopleuron bucklandii was described by Jacques-Amand Eudes-Deslongchamps in 1837–1838 from material excavated near Caen, Normandy. It was among the earliest large theropods to be scientifically described in France. The specimen was destroyed during Allied bombardment of Caen in World War II.

Poekilopleuron has had a complicated taxonomic history; material was at times referred to Megalosaurus, and the PBDB treats it as Megalosaurus (Poekilopleuron). It is currently regarded by many workers as a distinct genus within Megalosauridae, though its precise relationships within that family remain uncertain given the incompleteness of material.

Destroyed partial skeleton documented by old descriptions, illustrations, casts and limited surviving pieces.

## Where it lived

The Calcaires de Caen Formation represents shallow marine and coastal deposits of Bathonian age, suggesting Poekilopleuron lived in a near-coastal lowland environment. The French Jurassic was an archipelago landscape, and large theropods appear to have been apex predators across these island-studded seas.

Middle Jurassic limestone deposits near Caen that produced the only known Poekilopleuron skeleton.

## Open questions

- The original type is destroyed, limiting modern character testing.
- Some surviving material and casts require a modern comprehensive audit.
