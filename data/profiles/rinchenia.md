## Overview

Rinchenia (named in honour of Mongolian palaeontologist Rinchen Barsbold) was a small oviraptorosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 72–66 million years ago (Maastrichtian). Named by Barsbold in 1997, it is estimated at about 2.5 metres in length and is known from a skull and fragmentary postcranial material from the Nemegt Formation of Omnogov province. It is distinguished by a very tall, elaborate cranial crest.

Rinchenia belongs to Oviraptoridae within the Oviraptorosauria and is placed within the subfamily Oviraptorinae and tribe Citipatinae. Its highly developed crest is one of the most elaborate among oviraptorids and may have functioned in display or species recognition. The genus was originally described as a species of Oviraptor (O. mongoliensis) but was reclassified as a distinct genus following recognition of its unique crest morphology.

## Anatomy & life

Rinchenia's crest is among the most prominent of any oviraptorid, forming a tall, rounded, bony dome over the skull. Such crests in oviraptorids have been compared to those of cassowaries and are generally interpreted as display structures, possibly used for species recognition, sexual selection, or intraspecific communication. The internal structure of the crest may also have resonated sound.

Like other oviraptorids, Rinchenia had a toothless beak and robust jaws capable of crushing hard food items such as shellfish, seeds, or eggs. The omnivore designation in the PBDB reflects this dietary versatility. Oviraptorids are also known to have brooded their eggs, a behaviour confirmed by specimens preserved in brooding posture.

The Nemegt Formation preserves one of the richest and most diverse Late Cretaceous dinosaur assemblages in the world, including Tarbosaurus, Therizinosaurus, Deinocheirus, and multiple hadrosaurs and sauropods. Rinchenia was a small omnivore within this community, likely foraging opportunistically across the fluvial floodplain landscape.

## Fossil record

The specimen now referred to Rinchenia mongoliensis was first described as Oviraptor mongoliensis by Rinchen Barsbold in 1986. It was reclassified as a distinct genus, Rinchenia, by Barsbold in 1997, primarily on the basis of the uniquely tall and inflated cranial crest that differs markedly from the lower crest of Oviraptor philoceratops.

Single partial skeleton including crested skull and substantial postcranium.

## Where it lived

Maastrichtian-age formation in the Nemegt Basin yielding abundant and diverse dinosaur material including the Rinchenia holotype.

## Open questions

- Only one principal individual is known.
- The crest soft tissue, diet and exact oviraptorid relationships are uncertain.
