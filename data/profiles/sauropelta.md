## Overview

Sauropelta edwardsorum was a large nodosaurid ankylosaur from the Early Cretaceous Cloverly Formation of Montana and Wyoming (~108–110 Ma), reaching about five metres and protected by rows of keeled bony scutes and large spines projecting from the sides of the neck and shoulders. Unlike ankylosaurids, nodosaurids like Sauropelta lacked a tail club and relied entirely on passive armour for protection.

Sauropelta is among the most completely known nodosaurids, allowing detailed reconstruction of its armour arrangement. The prominent lateral neck spines — projecting outward from the sides of the neck — would have been hazardous for any predator attempting to bite the neck from the side, the most vulnerable region for a heavily armoured quadruped.

## Anatomy & life

Sauropelta's armour includes large keeled scutes over the back and sides, smaller flat ossicles filling the gaps, and prominent neck spines. The entire system was embedded in skin rather than attached to bone, forming a flexible mosaic that could accommodate body movement. The neck spines are particularly prominent — oriented laterally to protect the neck from attack.

Without a tail club, Sauropelta's defence was entirely passive: heavy armour covering the dorsal surface and flanks, combined with a low centre of gravity that would make flipping difficult for even large theropods. Nodosaurids were predominantly low browsers, feeding on ground-level vegetation with their relatively narrow beaks.

## Fossil record

Named by John Ostrom in 1970 from Cloverly Formation material — the same formation that yielded the Deinonychus specimens that sparked the Dinosaur Renaissance. Sauropelta and Deinonychus were thus both described by Ostrom from the same unit, providing an ecological snapshot of Early Cretaceous Montana. The species name honours the Edwards family on whose ranch the material was found.

Several partial skeletons, including substantial postcranium and osteoderms.

Armour plates and long shoulder spines define this nodosaurid ankylosaur.
