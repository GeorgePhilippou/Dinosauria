## Overview

Sonidosaurus (meaning 'Sonid lizard') was a small titanosaur sauropod from the Late Cretaceous of Inner Mongolia, China, living approximately 89–66 million years ago. It was described as roughly 9 metres long, making it a relatively small member of the typically enormous titanosaur group. First discovered in 2001 at Saihangaobi in the Iren Dabasu (Erlian) Formation, its description in 2006 added to the growing record of titanosaurs from Asia.

Named by Xu, Zhang, Tan, Zhao, and Tan in 2006, Sonidosaurus saihangaobiensis is notable for being discovered in the same quarry that would later yield the giant oviraptorosaur Gigantoraptor. Its titanosaur classification places it in the most diverse sauropod group of the Late Cretaceous, which survived until the end-Cretaceous extinction. Sonidosaurus contributes to understanding how titanosaurs were distributed across Late Cretaceous Asia and what faunas they shared.

## Anatomy & life

Sonidosaurus is classified within Titanosauria, the dominant sauropod group of the Late Cretaceous. Titanosaurs are characterised by wide-gauge limb posture, procoelous (anteriorly hollow) vertebrae, and in many species, osteoderms (bony skin armour). Sonidosaurus preserves vertebral features consistent with titanosaur membership, though detailed phylogenetic resolution within the diverse titanosaur tree remains challenging.

At approximately 9 metres long, Sonidosaurus was diminutive compared to other titanosaurs such as Argentinosaurus or even the contemporary Nemegtosaurus from Mongolia. Small body size in titanosaurs may reflect island dwarfism, resource limitations, or simply taxonomic diversity within the group. Further comparative work is needed to determine whether Sonidosaurus represents a genuinely small-bodied lineage or a juvenile individual.

## Fossil record

Sonidosaurus saihangaobiensis was described in 2006 by Xu Xing, Zhang Xingliao, Tan Lin, Zhao Xijin, and Tan Lin from material found in 2001 in Sonid Right Banner, Inner Mongolia. The genus name references the Sonid area of Inner Mongolia; the species name refers to Saihangaobi, the specific locality. The holotype consists of vertebrae and limb elements.

Single partial postcranial skeleton without skull.

- The specimen includes vertebrae, ribs, pelvis and limb bones but no skull.
- The catalogue nine-metre length is cleared pending modern specimen-level reassessment.
- Conflicting biostratigraphic correlations prevent a defensible narrow numerical age.

## Where it lived

The Iren Dabasu Formation preserves a diverse Late Cretaceous fauna including hadrosaurs (Bactrosaurus, Gilmoreosaurus), the tyrannosaur Alectrosaurus, alvarezsaurids, and the enormous oviraptorosaur Gigantoraptor. Sonidosaurus was one of the few sauropods in this otherwise theropod- and ornithopod-dominated assemblage, reflecting the general decline of sauropods relative to ornithischians in Late Cretaceous Asian faunas.

The holotype was found at Saihangaobi in this Late Cretaceous formation, which has also produced hadrosaurs, alvarezsaurids, and the giant Gigantoraptor.

The Iren Dabasu Formation has competing vertebrate and microfossil age models, including Turonian, Coniacian-Campanian and late Campanian-Maastrichtian correlations; the type cannot presently be assigned one narrow range.
