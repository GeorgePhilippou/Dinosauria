## Overview

Sinornithosaurus (meaning 'Chinese bird lizard') was a small feathered dromaeosaurid dinosaur from the Early Cretaceous Yixian Formation of Liaoning Province, China, living approximately 124.5–122 million years ago. It reached about 2 metres in length and is closely related to the lineage that eventually gave rise to birds. Named by Xu, Wang, and Wu in 1999, it was the fifth non-avian feathered dinosaur genus to be discovered, and specimens preserve clear integumentary filaments over much of the body.

Sinornithosaurus millenii holds particular significance because it was among the first dromaeosaurids found with direct evidence of feather-like body coverings, reinforcing the evolutionary connection between non-avian maniraptorans and modern birds. A controversial hypothesis proposed that Sinornithosaurus had venom-delivery grooves in its teeth, but this claim has been largely rejected by subsequent analyses that attribute the apparent grooves to post-mortem distortion.

## Anatomy & life

Multiple specimens preserve filamentous and possibly pennaceous feathers on the arms, body, and tail. The arm feathers, while not forming a full wing capable of powered flight, suggest that the paravian body plan with wing-like feathered forelimbs predates the origin of avian flight. Sinornithosaurus adds to the evidence that dromaeosaurids were feathered throughout their evolutionary history.

A 2009 study proposed that Sinornithosaurus had venom-delivering grooves in its teeth, analogous to rear-fanged snakes, and an enlarged maxillary cavity interpreted as a venom gland. This claim attracted wide attention but was challenged by subsequent analyses showing the grooves were more likely artefacts of crushing and displacement of teeth during fossilisation. The venomous hypothesis is now regarded sceptically by most researchers.

## Fossil record

Sinornithosaurus millenii was described in 1999 by Xu Xing, Wang Xiaolin, and Wu Xichuan from a specimen collected at the Sihetun locality in western Liaoning. The species name millenii celebrates the approach of the new millennium at the time of description. It was the first dromaeosaurid from China to preserve clear evidence of feathery integument.

Several articulated feathered skeletons with cranial and postcranial material.

- Pennaceous feathers and body covering are directly preserved.
- Claims of venom-delivery anatomy are not accepted; the proposed grooves and tooth form are not uniquely associated with venom.

## Where it lived

Sinornithosaurus lived in the lake-dominated Yixian Formation ecosystem of northeastern China, known as the Jehol Biota. This environment was characterised by volcanic ash falls, dense forests, and abundant freshwater lakes teeming with fish. Sinornithosaurus likely preyed on small vertebrates, proto-birds, and possibly fish, sharing its habitat with Microraptor, Sinosauropteryx, and many early birds.

The original specimen was collected from the 124.5 Ma Jianshangou beds of the Yixian Formation at the Sihetun locality.

High-precision dating brackets the Yixian Formation at 125.755-124.122 Ma, within the Barremian on the current time scale.

## Open questions

- Species boundaries and aerial ability remain debated.
