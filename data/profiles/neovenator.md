## Overview

Neovenator salerii is the most completely known large theropod from the United Kingdom, discovered on the Isle of Wight in the Wessex Formation (Barremian, ~130 Ma). At around 7.5 metres it was the apex predator of the Early Cretaceous Wessex ecosystem, which also included Baryonyx, Iguanodon and Hypsilophodon.

Originally described as an allosauroid, Neovenator is now placed within Neovenatoridae — a clade of large tetanuran theropods with Gondwanan affinities that includes Megaraptor and possibly Australovenator. Its discovery substantially broadened knowledge of European Early Cretaceous predator diversity beyond the spinosaurid-only picture that had previously prevailed.

## Fossil record

Found by the Salero family in a landslip on the Isle of Wight in 1978, the specimen was sold to Dinosaur Farm Museum before being formally described by Hutt and colleagues in 1996. The species name honours the Salero family. Much of the skull and most of the postcranium are represented.

One relatively complete partial skeleton with skull, axial column, girdles and limbs.

Large theropod anatomy with features linking it to carcharodontosaurids or megaraptorans — its exact placement is still debated.

## Where it lived

The Wessex Formation records a complex floodplain system with channels, swamps and dryland patches in a warm sub-tropical climate. Neovenator would have hunted Iguanodon herds and possibly Polacanthus (a nodosaurid ankylosaur). Its co-occurrence with Baryonyx suggests two large predators used different microhabitats — Neovenator on open land, Baryonyx near water.

Early Cretaceous river delta and floodplain environment of what is now southern England.

## Classification

The skull has a relatively long, low profile with large fenestrae (openings) reducing skull weight. The forelimbs are robust with large claws. Originally placed in Allosauridae, phylogenetic analysis now consistently recovers Neovenator in Neovenatoridae — a clade with probable Gondwanan origins that dispersed to Europe and possibly North America.

## Open questions

- The limits and membership of Neovenatoridae remain debated.
- Maximum body size is based chiefly on one individual.
