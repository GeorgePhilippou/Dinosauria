## Overview

Tuojiangosaurus multispinus was a large stegosaur (~7 m) from the Late Jurassic Shangshaximiao Formation of Sichuan, China (~160 Ma), known from two partial skeletons. It bore 17 pairs of plates and spikes along the back and tail — more than any other known stegosaur. The plates grade from smaller ones at the neck to taller ones over the hips and transition into paired tail spikes posteriorly.

As part of the rich Chinese Jurassic dinosaur fauna, Tuojiangosaurus shares its ecosystem with the sauropods Mamenchisaurus and Omeisaurus and the theropod Yangchuanosaurus. The diversity and quality of Chinese Jurassic stegosaur material (Tuojiangosaurus, Huayangosaurus, Chungkingosaurus) rivals that of the Morrison Formation and shows that stegosaurs were globally widespread and diverse in the Middle to Late Jurassic.

## Anatomy & life

The 17 pairs of dorsal elements begin at the neck as smaller, more conical spikes, grade to larger plates over the shoulders and hips, and then become paired spikes posteriorly toward the tail tip. This arrangement is more varied than in Stegosaurus, where large diamond-shaped plates dominate the back and spikes are restricted to the tail.

The Shangshaximiao Formation at Dashanpu (Zigong) is one of the richest Jurassic dinosaur sites in Asia. The Zigong Dinosaur Museum was built directly over the excavation quarry and displays Tuojiangosaurus alongside Mamenchisaurus, Omeisaurus and Yangchuanosaurus. The fauna is broadly contemporaneous with the Morrison Formation and shows comparable ecological structure.

## Fossil record

Dong Zhiming, Zhou Shiwu and Zhang Yihong described Tuojiangosaurus multispinus in 1977 from material found near the Tuojiang River in Sichuan Province. The name "multispinus" (many spines) refers to the high number of plate-spine pairs. Two partial skeletons provide a reasonable picture of the animal's overall anatomy.

One main partial skeleton with fragmentary skull, postcranium, plates and spikes.

Plates and spines along the back and stegosaur skeletal anatomy are well documented in the near-complete specimen.

## Where it lived

Late Jurassic terrestrial ecosystem of the Sichuan Basin alongside giant sauropods and large theropods.

## Open questions

- Armour order and skull completeness are limited.
