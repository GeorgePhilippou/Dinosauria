## Overview

Corythosaurus casuarius was a helmet-crested lambeosaurine hadrosaurid from the Dinosaur Park Formation of Alberta. Many skulls and skeletons across growth stages, including skin impressions, document its anatomy and the dramatic ontogenetic growth of its hollow crest.

Corythosaurus casuarius ("helmeted lizard") was a large crested hadrosaur from the Campanian of Alberta (~77–75 Ma), reaching about nine metres and distinguished by a tall, thin helmet-like hollow crest on the skull. The crest contained looping nasal passages that may have allowed deep, resonant calls — the most well-supported hypothesis for hollow hadrosaur crests generally.

Exceptional preservation of Corythosaurus skin impressions reveals a complex mosaic of large polygonal scales and smaller tubercles. The crest shape changed through ontogeny: juveniles had small rounded crests, young adults showed intermediate forms and large adults had the full helmet. This ontogenetic change helps distinguish Corythosaurus from the closely related Lambeosaurus.

## Anatomy & life

Corythosaurus was a large duck-billed dinosaur of the lambeosaurine group that lived along the river plains of what is now southern Alberta in the late Campanian. Adults were about eight to nine metres long and could walk on either two or four legs, with a deep tail stiffened by ossified tendons.

Its most striking feature was a tall, rounded bony crest that gave the animal its name, meaning "helmet lizard". The crest was hollow and housed looping nasal passages; because the airway is preserved inside the bone, its shape is known directly, although what sounds it may have produced is a matter of modelling rather than observation.

Skulls of many sizes show that juveniles had only a low bump where the adult crest would later rise. Several species named from Alberta in the early twentieth century are now regarded as growth stages or individual variants of Corythosaurus casuarius, although a second, stratigraphically higher species, C. intermedius, is still recognised by some workers.

- A tall, hollow, helmet-shaped crest containing the nasal passages, which grew dramatically between juvenile and adult stages.
- Skin impressions preserved on the holotype and a second skeleton show a covering of small, non-overlapping scales.
- One of the most completely known hadrosaurids, with near-complete articulated skeletons collected from the Dinosaur Park Formation.

**Feeding: Plant-eater.** A broad, toothless beak and dental batteries of hundreds of closely packed teeth with grinding wear are directly preserved. Reported gut contents from an early specimen are of uncertain reliability.

**Movement: Bipedal and quadrupedal.** Complete fore- and hindlimbs with hoof-like hand and foot bones fit a hadrosaurid that shifted between two- and four-legged walking. Speeds are modelled, not observed.

**Appearance: Skeleton and scales known.** Skin impressions record scale size and pattern over parts of the body, but colour, any soft crest extensions and other integument are inferred.

## Fossil record

Corythosaurus has one of the best fossil records of any hadrosaurid. The holotype and a second New York skeleton are nearly complete and articulated with skin, and many further skulls and skeletons from the Dinosaur Park Formation span juvenile to adult growth stages.

Complete skulls with crests and dental batteries, full vertebral series with ossified tendons, both girdles, all four limbs with hands and feet, and impressions of scaly skin over the flank, limbs and tail in at least two individuals.

The main gaps are not skeletal: the keratin beak, any soft-tissue crest extension, colour and the acoustic behaviour of the living animal are not preserved.

Skull crests and skeletal remains support its lambeosaurine hadrosaur classification.

- The internal nasal passages of the crest are preserved and could affect sound resonance, but no specific call can be reconstructed as fact.
- Many historical Corythosaurus species are now interpreted as growth or individual variation within C. casuarius.

## Where it lived

Late Cretaceous coastal plain and river systems of western Canada.

Diagnostic C. casuarius material is late Campanian in the Dinosaur Park Formation.

## Classification

Corythosaurus is a lambeosaurine hadrosaurid, part of the hollow-crested duck-billed dinosaurs. It is consistently grouped with Hypacrosaurus and Lambeosaurus among the helmet-crested lambeosaurines.

## Open questions

### What did the crest do?

The looping nasal passages could have resonated during calls and the crest probably served in visual display, but neither function can be confirmed directly, and no specific call can be reconstructed as fact.

### How many species are valid?

Growth-stage studies collapsed several early names into C. casuarius. Whether C. intermedius is a distinct, younger species or variation within one lineage is still discussed.

### What was lost at sea?

Two Corythosaurus skeletons collected by the Sternbergs were lost in 1916 when the ship carrying them to Britain was sunk, so part of the early material survives only in field records.
