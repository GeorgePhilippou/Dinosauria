## Overview

Arrhinoceratops brachyops was a large chasmosaurine ceratopsid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Alberta, Canada, living approximately 72–67 million years ago. Estimated at 6 to 8 metres in length, it is known from a single skull found in the Horseshoe Canyon Formation. Its name means 'without nose horn face', reflecting the original (and erroneous) interpretation that it lacked a nasal horn — subsequent examination revealed a small horn was present.

Named by Parks in 1925, Arrhinoceratops is placed within Chasmosaurinae, the ceratopsid subfamily characterised by elongate, often elaborate frills. Only a single undisputed specimen is known, limiting detailed anatomical description. It coexisted with other large ceratopsids and hadrosaurs in the diverse Horseshoe Canyon fauna of latest Cretaceous Alberta.

## Anatomy & life

Arrhinoceratops is known from a single skull that shows the large, swept-back frill characteristic of chasmosaurines. The brow horns were moderately long and curved. The skull is relatively compact compared to some other chasmosaurines. Because only one specimen is known, variation within the species cannot be assessed. The postcranial skeleton of Arrhinoceratops remains unknown.

Arrhinoceratops inhabited the coastal lowlands of the Western Interior during the latest Campanian to early Maastrichtian. It was a ground-dwelling herbivore, likely feeding on low-growing vegetation with its powerful beak. The Horseshoe Canyon fauna also included the hadrosaurs Edmontosaurus and Hypacrosaurus, the ankylosaur Edmontonia, and early tyrannosaurids, suggesting a diverse and productive ecosystem.

Despite being known from only a single skull, Arrhinoceratops contributes to understanding the diversity of chasmosaurine ceratopsids during the latest Cretaceous of North America. Its phylogenetic placement within Chasmosaurinae helps trace the evolution of frill and horn morphology in this lineage, and it represents part of the rich ceratopsid fauna that characterised the final stages of non-avian dinosaur history.

## Fossil record

Arrhinoceratops brachyops was described by William Arthur Parks in 1925 from a skull collected from the Horseshoe Canyon Formation of Alberta. The generic name was coined based on the mistaken belief that the nasal region lacked a horn; 'arrhin-' derives from Greek meaning 'without nose'. The species name brachyops means 'short-faced', referring to the relatively short proportions of the skull. Later study revealed that a small nasal horn was present but had been damaged or incompletely preserved in the type specimen.

One principal near-complete skull and a small amount of additional cranial material.

- The name, meaning no nose horn, is misleading because a nasal horn core is present.
- A juvenile skull adds growth information, but whole-body reconstructions rely on related ceratopsids.

## Where it lived

The only known Arrhinoceratops specimen — a partial skull — was recovered from this formation along the Red Deer River, one of the most productive sites for Late Cretaceous dinosaurs in Canada.

The diagnostic material is lower Maastrichtian within the Horseshoe Canyon Formation.

## Open questions

- Very little diagnostic postcranial material is known.
- Distortion complicates details of the holotype skull.
