## Overview

Podokesaurus (meaning 'swift-footed lizard') was a small carnivorous theropod from the Early Jurassic of North America, living approximately 195–183 million years ago. Named by Mignon Talbot in 1911, it is known from a single partial skeleton discovered in Massachusetts, USA, now assigned to the Portland Formation. It is estimated to have been about 0.9 metres long, making it one of the smaller dinosaurs of its time.

Podokesaurus belongs to Coelophysidae within the Coelophysoidea, a group of slender early theropods. The original specimen was unfortunately destroyed in a museum fire in 1917, making further study extremely difficult; casts survive. Some researchers have questioned whether Podokesaurus is truly distinct from Coelophysis, but it is currently maintained as a separate genus pending further analysis.

## Anatomy & life

Despite its fragmentary record, Podokesaurus is important as evidence that coelophysoid theropods were widespread across the Early Jurassic of eastern North America, a region that has yielded few body fossils compared to its rich trackway record.

## Fossil record

Podokesaurus holyokensis was described by Mignon Talbot in 1911 from a partial skeleton found near Mount Holyoke, Massachusetts. It was among the first dinosaurs described from New England. Tragically, the holotype was destroyed in a fire at Mount Holyoke College in 1917, and science now relies on casts made before that loss.

One destroyed partial skeleton documented by photographs, drawings and casts.

- The original specimen came from the Portland Formation, not the Middle Jurassic.
- Its anatomy is coelophysoid, but the lost material may not preserve a unique diagnosis.
- Proposed synonymy with Coelophysis cannot be tested adequately without the original fossil.

## Where it lived

The Portland Formation represents floodplain and lacustrine deposits in an Early Jurassic rift valley. Podokesaurus would have inhabited a semi-arid, seasonally wet environment alongside early crocodilians and other small vertebrates.

The only known occurrence of Podokesaurus, from Early Jurassic fluvial redbeds of the Connecticut Valley.

Portland Formation evidence places the specimen in the Early Jurassic.

## Classification

Some palaeontologists have argued that Podokesaurus may be synonymous with the better-known Coelophysis, given overlapping body plans and the fragmentary nature of the holotype. Without additional material, definitively resolving this question is not possible; the genus is cautiously retained pending new finds.
