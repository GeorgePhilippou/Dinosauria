## Overview

Procompsognathus (meaning 'before Compsognathus') was a small carnivorous theropod from the Late Triassic of Germany, living approximately 215–211 million years ago. Named by Eberhard Fraas in 1913, it is estimated to have been about 1 metre long and is known from a single, badly crushed partial skeleton from the Löwenstein Formation of Baden-Württemberg. It is classified within Coelophysoidea, making it one of the earliest members of that group.

Procompsognathus is scientifically significant as one of the earliest known small theropods from Europe, providing evidence of theropod diversity at the dawn of the dinosaur radiation. However, because the holotype is so poorly preserved—crushed and incomplete—its exact anatomy and phylogenetic placement remain uncertain. Some features attributed to it may be taphonomic artefacts rather than genuine anatomical details.

## Anatomy & life

The holotype specimen is severely crushed and disarticulated, making accurate anatomical reconstruction extremely difficult. Some features of the skull that were initially described may be preservation artefacts; this has led to uncertainty about its exact phylogenetic position, with some analyses placing it outside Coelophysidae.

Procompsognathus lived during the Norian stage, shortly after the Carnian Pluvial Event. Early dinosaurs were diversifying rapidly across the supercontinent Pangaea, and small carnivorous forms like Procompsognathus competed with other archosaurs including early crocodylomorphs in semi-arid European environments.

Despite its fragmentary nature, Procompsognathus represents one of the few body fossil records of small theropods from the Late Triassic of Europe, complementing the richer track record from this interval. It provides important biogeographic data on the early spread of Coelophysoidea across Pangaea.

## Fossil record

Procompsognathus triassicus was described by Eberhard Fraas in 1913 from a badly crushed partial skeleton collected in Württemberg. The name reflects its perceived similarity to the later Jurassic Compsognathus, though the two are not closely related. Fraas placed it among early theropods based on its small size and bipedal posture.

Poorly preserved incomplete skeleton with possibly misassociated cranial pieces.

- The postcranium is broadly consistent with a small neotheropod or coelophysoid.
- Cranial material was historically overinterpreted and may be chimaeric.
- Precise diet, size and relationships cannot be established confidently.

## Where it lived

Late Triassic (Norian) continental redbeds of southwestern Germany yielding the only known Procompsognathus specimen.

The type is Norian Late Triassic; the 221-210 Ma range is broader than its horizon supports.
