## Overview

Khaan (meaning 'ruler' in Mongolian) was a small omnivorous oviraptorid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia, living approximately 83–72 million years ago. Known from three skeletons with skulls from the Djadokhta Formation of Omnogov, it reached an estimated 1.8 metres in length. It was described by Clark, Norell and Barsbold in 2001 and is closely related to Oviraptor, Ingenia (Heyuannia yanshini), and Conchoraptor. Like other oviraptorids, it was a toothless, beaked dinosaur.

Khaan is placed within Heyuanninae (also called Ingeniinae) within Oviraptoridae, a position shared with Heyuannia. The availability of three skulls made it one of the better cranially represented oviraptorids from the Djadokhta Formation at the time of description. The Djadokhta Formation preserves a desert dune environment from the Campanian of southern Mongolia that has yielded an extraordinary diversity of small dinosaurs, including Protoceratops, Velociraptor, Pinacosaurus, and several oviraptorosaurs.

## Anatomy & life

Khaan was a small, lightly built oviraptorid reaching about 1.8 metres in length. It bore a toothless beak and a short cranial crest. Its hands were tridactyl and relatively large. Like other oviraptorosaurs, it was likely omnivorous — the diet possibly including seeds, small vertebrates, eggs, and invertebrates. The deep beak may have been suited for cracking hard food items.

Khaan contributes to the understanding of oviraptorid diversity and phylogeny within the Djadokhta Formation. Multiple specimens from the same formation have allowed researchers to compare Khaan with the morphologically similar Oviraptor, Conchoraptor, and Citipati, informing the phylogenetic structure of Oviraptoridae and the diversity of oviraptorosaurs in the Late Cretaceous of Mongolia.

## Fossil record

Khaan mckennai was described in 2001 by James Clark, Mark Norell and Rinchen Barsbold from three specimens collected from the Djadokhta Formation. The genus name means 'ruler' in Mongolian, and the species honours palaeontologist Malcolm McKenna. The three skulls available at the time made Khaan one of the better-known oviraptorids cranially.

Several substantially complete associated skulls and skeletons.

## Where it lived

The Djadokhta Formation records an ancient desert environment characterised by aeolian (wind-deposited) red sandstones, preserving evidence of sand dunes and dry interdune areas. Khaan coexisted with Protoceratops, Velociraptor, Pinacosaurus, and other oviraptorosaurs in this arid setting, likely exploiting a variety of food sources available in the desert ecosystem.
