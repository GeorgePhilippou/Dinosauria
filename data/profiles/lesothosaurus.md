## Overview

Lesothosaurus was a small, lightly built ornithischian dinosaur that lived during the Early Jurassic, approximately 200–190 million years ago, in what is now Lesotho and South Africa. It measured roughly 1–2 metres in length and is thought to have been a swift bipedal herbivore or omnivore, browsing on low-growing vegetation in semi-arid environments. The type species is Lesothosaurus diagnosticus.

Lesothosaurus is considered one of the most primitive ornithischian dinosaurs known, placing it near the base of the ornithischian family tree. Its anatomy provides important clues about the ancestral body plan from which all the diverse ornithischian lineages — including armoured dinosaurs, horned dinosaurs, and duck-billed dinosaurs — ultimately derived. Some earlier studies placed it within Fabrosauridae, but that grouping is no longer widely recognised as a natural clade.

## Anatomy & life

Lesothosaurus is frequently recovered as one of the most basal ornithischians in phylogenetic analyses, meaning it diverged before the major ornithischian subgroups separated. This position makes it informative for understanding ancestral ornithischian anatomy, including the early development of the predentary bone (a unique ornithischian jaw element) and the arrangement of teeth and cheeks.

The dentition of Lesothosaurus suggests a herbivorous or possibly omnivorous diet, with leaf-shaped teeth capable of processing plant material. Its long hindlimbs relative to its body indicate it was likely a fast runner, probably relying on speed to avoid predators rather than armour or bulk. Some specimens have been found grouped together, which may tentatively suggest some degree of social behaviour, though this is uncertain.

## Fossil record

Lesothosaurus diagnosticus was named in 1978 by Peter Galton based on material from the Upper Elliot Formation of Lesotho, with the genus name honouring the country of discovery. The species name diagnosticus reflects the diagnostic features that distinguish it from other early ornithischians. It has had a complex taxonomic history, with earlier specimens sometimes confused with the related Fabrosaurus, whose material is considered too fragmentary to be reliably distinguished.

Numerous skulls and partial skeletons spanning several growth stages.

- Multiple growth stages are represented.
- Its simple leaf-shaped teeth and beak support plant feeding, though opportunistic animal matter cannot be tested.
- It was a lightly built biped rather than a member of a single natural group called Hypsilophodontidae.

## Where it lived

Lesothosaurus inhabited the semi-arid floodplain environments of the Karoo Basin during the Early Jurassic, sharing its world with prosauropod dinosaurs such as Massospondylus and early theropods. The Upper Elliot Formation is one of the most productive Early Jurassic fossil beds in Africa, yielding a diverse fauna that documents the early diversification of dinosaurs after the end-Triassic extinction event.

Multiple partial skeletons of Lesothosaurus diagnosticus have been recovered from Early Jurassic redbeds in this formation.

Upper Elliot occurrences are Early Jurassic, primarily Hettangian-Sinemurian.

## Open questions

- Some growth-related referrals and its exact position near the base of Neornithischia remain debated.
- Diet breadth cannot be measured directly.
