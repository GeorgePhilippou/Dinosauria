## Overview

Microceratus was a very small basal ceratopsian dinosaur that lived during the Late Cretaceous (Cenomanian to Turonian), approximately 92–89 million years ago, in what is now Inner Mongolia and Gansu Province, China. It is known only from juvenile specimens, making its adult size uncertain, though the genus is thought to have been small and agile. The type species is Microceratus gobiensis, originally named Microceratops gobiensis.

The name Microceratus was coined in 2008 when researchers realised that the original name Microceratops had been preoccupied by a species of parasitoid wasp, necessitating a replacement name. Its small size and agility suggest it may have relied on speed to escape predators. As a basal neoceratopsian, Microceratus provides information about early ceratopsian evolution in Asia, though the juvenile-only material means important anatomical details about its adult form remain unknown.

## Anatomy & life

All known specimens of Microceratus appear to be from juvenile individuals, which creates significant challenges for interpreting its anatomy and relationships. Features visible in juveniles may differ substantially from those of adults, and adult body size cannot be reliably estimated from immature specimens alone. Researchers believe it was a relatively small ceratopsian even as an adult, but the exact dimensions remain uncertain.

The gracile proportions of known Microceratus specimens suggest a lightly built, potentially fast-moving animal. In the absence of body armour, horns, or other obvious defensive structures visible in juvenile specimens, speed may have been its primary antipredator strategy. It would have fed on low-growing plants in the semi-arid Late Cretaceous environments of Central Asia.

Late Cretaceous Asia supported a remarkable diversity of neoceratopsian dinosaurs, ranging from small basal forms like Microceratus and Protoceratops to larger, more derived genera. This diversity reflects the importance of Asia as both the cradle of ceratopsian evolution and a centre of ongoing diversification in the group throughout the Cretaceous Period.

## Fossil record

Microceratops gobiensis was originally described by Bohlin in 1953 based on fragmentary material from the Gobi Desert region of China. In 2008, it was discovered that the name Microceratops had already been applied to a genus of parasitoid wasp (Hymenoptera), making the dinosaur name a junior homonym. The replacement name Microceratus gobiensis was therefore erected to resolve this nomenclatural conflict.

Fragmentary type material; much historically referred material belongs to other taxa.

- The name change solved a nomenclatural homonym but did not improve the fossil diagnosis.
- Common skeletal reconstructions often incorporate material no longer referred to Microceratus.
- A small bipedal neoceratopsian identity is plausible, but detailed anatomy and precise size are weakly supported.

## Where it lived

Juvenile specimens of Microceratus gobiensis were recovered from Late Cretaceous aeolian and fluvial deposits in this famous Central Asian formation.

Only a broad Campanian age can be defended for the relevant Gobi material; the former 86-66 Ma range is excessive.

## Open questions

- The type needs comprehensive modern redescription.
- Diagnostic validity and assignment of referred material remain uncertain.
- Specialist confirmation is still required before this profile is marked primary-literature reviewed.
