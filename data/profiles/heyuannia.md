## Overview

Heyuannia is a genus of oviraptorid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous of China and Mongolia, living approximately 83–66 million years ago. It is notable as the first oviraptorid found in China; most other members of the family are known from Mongolia. Two species are recognised: H. huangi from the Dalangshan Formation of Guangdong, described by Lü in 2003, and H. yanshini (originally named Ingenia by Barsbold in 1981 from the Barun Goyot Formation of Mongolia). The animal reached about 2 metres in length and was likely omnivorous.

The taxonomic history of Heyuannia is complex: H. yanshini was originally placed in the separate genus Ingenia and later briefly renamed Ajancingenia in 2013 before ethical concerns about the author of that name led to the current arrangement. The genus belongs to the subfamily Heyuanninae (or Ingeniinae) within Oviraptoridae, a group of toothless, beaked theropods. Heyuannia contributes to understanding of oviraptorid diversity and the geographic extent of the family across East Asia.

## Anatomy & life

Heyuannia was a small, lightly built theropod approximately 2 metres long, with a toothless beak, a crest on the skull (possibly), and powerful forelimbs with clawed hands. Like other oviraptorids, it was likely omnivorous, possibly consuming seeds, eggs, small animals, and plant material. The beak morphology suggests it could crack hard food items.

Heyuannia's discovery in southern China extended the known range of oviraptorids beyond Mongolia and expanded understanding of the family's geographic diversity. Its complex nomenclatural history illustrates ongoing challenges in dinosaur taxonomy when original descriptions contain errors or ethical complications arise.

## Fossil record

H. huangi was named by Lü Junchang in 2003 from the Dalangshan Formation of Guangdong, China, making it the first oviraptorid formally described from China. H. yanshini has a more complex history: originally described as Ingenia yanshini by Barsbold in 1981, it was briefly renamed Ajancingenia in 2013 before taxonomic and ethical issues led to its current placement in Heyuannia.

Partial skull and substantial partial skeleton.

- The profile species is Mongolian and is not the Chinese type species Heyuannia huangi.
- The holotype preserves substantial skeletal material but is not complete.
- Oviraptorid jaws were toothless and locomotion bipedal, while exact diet remains unresolved.

## Where it lived

The Dalangshan Formation records Late Cretaceous terrestrial environments in southern China, while the Barun Goyot Formation of Mongolia preserves a sand-dune desert environment. Heyuannia thus occupied varied Late Cretaceous landscapes across East Asia, consistent with the broad geographic distribution of oviraptorid dinosaurs.

The source of H. huangi holotype material, making this one of the southernmost known oviraptorid localities.

Corrected from a 72-68 Ma range to the older Campanian Baruungoyot record; the formation is not securely late Maastrichtian.

## Open questions

- Whether yanshini should remain in Ajancingenia or be combined with Heyuannia is not settled.
- Diet cannot be specified from the toothless skull alone.
