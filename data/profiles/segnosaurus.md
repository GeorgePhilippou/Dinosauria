## Overview

Segnosaurus (meaning 'slow lizard') was a large therizinosaurid theropod from the Late Cretaceous of southeastern Mongolia, living approximately 102–86 million years ago. Multiple well-preserved but incomplete specimens were collected from the Gobi Desert in the 1970s, revealing a body roughly 4 metres long with broad hips, short forelimbs bearing large curved claws, and a toothless beak at the front of the jaw. Despite its classification within Theropoda, Segnosaurus was herbivorous, a dietary shift that evolved independently from the classic meat-eating theropod body plan.

Segnosaurus galbinensis was formally described by Perle in 1979 and became one of the founding members of the clade Therizinosauria (previously called Segnosauria). It played a pivotal role in demonstrating that therizinosaurs belonged within Theropoda rather than representing a separate dinosaur lineage, a debate that occupied palaeontologists for decades. Remains attributed to this genus have also been tentatively identified from Japan, further extending its geographic interest.

## Anatomy & life

Despite being a theropod, Segnosaurus was herbivorous, with a broad pelvis accommodating a large fermentation gut, a toothless beak, and small leaf-shaped teeth at the back of the jaw for processing plant material. Its enormous forelimb claws may have been used to pull down leafy branches rather than for predation.

## Fossil record

Fossils of Segnosaurus were excavated from the Galbin region of Mongolia by joint Soviet-Mongolian palaeontological expeditions in the 1970s. Altangerel Perle named the genus and species Segnosaurus galbinensis in 1979; the genus name references the perceived sluggish build, while the species name honours the Galbin region. The known material includes lower jaw fragments, vertebrae, pelvis, shoulder girdle, and limb elements.

Partial skull and lower jaws, vertebrae, pelvis and hind-limb bones from more than one individual.

- The material includes both lower jaws, vertebrae, pelvis and hind-limb elements, but no complete skeleton.
- The front of the lower jaw was toothless and probably bore a keratinous beak.
- Closely packed, coarsely serrated teeth formed a specialised food-processing surface.
- The 2016 dental study places Segnosaurus within the broader shift toward herbivory in derived therizinosaurs; exact foods remain unknown.

## Where it lived

The Bayan Shireh Formation preserves a seasonally arid to semi-arid environment with river floodplains. Segnosaurus shared this habitat with ankylosaurs, hadrosaurs, and theropods such as Garudimimus, indicating a diverse mid-Cretaceous ecosystem in central Asia.

The holotype and referred specimens were collected from this mid-Cretaceous formation during Soviet-Mongolian expeditions in the 1970s.

The type and referred specimens are from the Bayanshiree Formation. Its internal age calibration remains broader than a single point estimate.

## Classification

When first described, Segnosaurus was placed in its own family Segnosauridae and order Segnosauria, thought by some workers to represent a distinct dinosaur lineage. Later cladistic analyses revealed that therizinosaurs are maniraptoran theropods, closely related to oviraptorosaurs and birds. Segnosaurus was central to this reclassification because it preserves key anatomical features shared with other maniraptorans.

## Open questions

- No direct gut contents identify the plants or other foods eaten.
- The specimens are incomplete and do not form one complete individual.
