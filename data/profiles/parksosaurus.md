## Overview

Parksosaurus (meaning 'Park's lizard', honouring Canadian palaeontologist William Parks) was a small ornithischian dinosaur from the early Maastrichtian (approximately 72–66 million years ago) of Alberta, Canada. Known from most of a partially articulated skeleton and partial skull recovered from the Horseshoe Canyon Formation, Parksosaurus warrenae was approximately 2.5–3 metres long and weighed around 50–75 kg. It was a bipedal herbivore placed within Thescelosaurinae of Thescelosauridae.

Described by Sternberg in 1937, Parksosaurus is one of the few non-hadrosaurid ornithopods known from the latest Cretaceous of North America — a time when the ornithopod fauna was dominated by hadrosaurs. Its survival alongside hadrosaurs suggests it occupied a slightly different ecological niche, possibly feeding on different plant types or habitats.

## Anatomy & life

The holotype preserves most of the skeleton in partial articulation, including skull elements, much of the vertebral column, ribs, limb bones and girdle elements. The skull is relatively small and triangular with a simple dental battery and horny beak. The hindlimbs are proportionally long, consistent with a cursorial (fast-running) lifestyle. The short forelimbs suggest habitual bipedality.

Parksosaurus is one of very few non-hadrosaurid ornithopods documented from the latest Cretaceous of Laramidia (western North America), existing alongside hadrosaurs such as Edmontosaurus. The persistence of a small thescelosaurid alongside dominant hadrosaurs indicates ecological partitioning, likely involving differences in body size, foraging height or plant-type preferences.

## Fossil record

Parksosaurus warrenae was described by Charles Mortram Sternberg in 1937. The material was originally collected by William Parks, whose name the genus honours. The species name warrenae honours Mrs. H.D. Warren, a patron of the Royal Ontario Museum. The holotype was found in the Horseshoe Canyon Formation, an Early Maastrichtian deposit in central Alberta.

The genus has been variously placed in Hypsilophodontidae, Ornithopoda more broadly, and most recently within Thescelosauridae following the dissolution of the paraphyletic 'Hypsilophodontidae'. Its placement in Thescelosaurinae is based on shared derived characters of the skull and postcranium with Thescelosaurus. Wikipedia confirms Parksosaurus as one of the few non-hadrosaurid ornithopods from the end of the Cretaceous in North America.

One partial skeleton with incomplete skull and substantial postcranium.

- The holotype preserves much of the postcranium and parts of the skull but is not complete.
- It was a bipedal, low-browsing ornithopod; more specific diet claims are not directly evidenced.
- Its close relationship to Thescelosaurus is supported, while fine thescelosaurid branching varies.

## Where it lived

Early Maastrichtian fluvial deposits of central Alberta, yielding the holotype partially articulated skeleton of Parksosaurus warrenae.

The relevant Horseshoe Canyon strata are early Maastrichtian, younger than the former 76-74 Ma range.
