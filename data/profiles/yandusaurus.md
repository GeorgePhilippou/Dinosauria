## Overview

Yandusaurus ('Yandu lizard') was a basal neornithischian or small ornithopod dinosaur from the Middle Jurassic of Sichuan Province, China, living approximately 169–159 million years ago. The type species, Yandusaurus hongheensis, was described in 1979 from the Lower Shaximiao Formation and reached about 3 metres in length. It was a lightly built, bipedal herbivore, one of a suite of small dinosaurs known from the richly productive Jurassic beds of the Sichuan Basin.

Yandusaurus contributes to the picture of neornithischian diversity during the Middle Jurassic of China, a period and region that is increasingly recognised as important for understanding early ornithischian evolution. Its precise phylogenetic placement — whether it is a basal ornithopod or a more stemward neornithischian — has been debated, and it has at times been compared with Agilisaurus from the same formation, though these two are now generally treated as distinct genera.

## Anatomy & life

The Lower Shaximiao Formation is one of the richest Middle Jurassic dinosaur assemblages in the world, yielding sauropods such as Shunosaurus and Omeisaurus, stegosaurs, large theropods, and numerous small ornithischians. Yandusaurus shared this environment with a diverse array of contemporaries, and the formation preserves a valuable window into Middle Jurassic ecosystems in East Asia before the diversification events of the Late Jurassic.

Early literature sometimes confused or synonymised Yandusaurus with Agilisaurus louderbacki, another small neornithischian from the same formation that was described separately in 1990. Modern consensus treats them as distinct taxa with different anatomical features, though both are among the small bipedal ornithischians that characterise the basal radiation of Neornithischia in Middle Jurassic China.

## Fossil record

Yandusaurus hongheensis was named by He Xinlu in 1979 from material discovered in the Lower Shaximiao Formation of Sichuan Province. The genus name refers to Yandu, an old name for the Zigong area of Sichuan, while the species name hongheensis references the Honghe (Red River) area of the locality. The specimen consists of partial skeletal material including limb elements and vertebrae.

## Classification

Yandusaurus was a lightly built, bipedal herbivore with elongated hindlimbs suggesting a cursorial lifestyle. Its classification has fluctuated between Hypsilophodontidae (now considered a wastebasket taxon), basal Ornithopoda, and basal Neornithischia. Modern analyses tend to place it either as an early ornithopod or as a basal neornithischian outside Ornithopoda, in a position that makes it informative for early ornithischian diversification.
